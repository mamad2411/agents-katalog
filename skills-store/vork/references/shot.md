# Shot design

The reference and the subject's own world override these defaults. Write one
sentence naming the subject, mood and what the viewer should notice. Decide what
changes during the shot before writing geometry or animation. Respect requested stills.

For motion, give camera movement, subject action and secondary motion distinct
reasons. Avoid adding movement that competes with the subject. Size the
move from the subject's bounds and visible surroundings, not a fixed distance.
Ease starts and stops; check the entire path for collisions, cropping and empty
stretches. 

Choose lens, depth of field and motion blur deliberately. Keep identifying detail
legible; shallow focus and blur must earn their cost. Consult `vork help cameras` for controls rather than inventing fields.

Begin with environment illumination, then shape the key and reflections. Choose
the environment for the subject. Read the
exposure summary and inspect clipped highlights and near-black regions. Fix
lighting or framing before polishing materials. Read `vork help lights` and
`vork help render` for the relevant contracts.

Richness comes from appropriate assets, believable scale, grounded contact and
detail where the camera goes. Read [assets](assets.md) before modeling a real
object from primitives. Do not decorate unseen surfaces while the silhouette
or focal point remains weak.

Inspect stills at the beginning, middle and end, then a contact sheet dense enough
to catch changes between them. Remove dead stretches. Check framing and focus
through motion; first and last frames must each work as a still. Watch playback
when available and report honestly when only sampled frames were inspected.
