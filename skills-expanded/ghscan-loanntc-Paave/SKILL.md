# Behavioural Finance for Vietnam Gen Z Investors

## Why This Matters

Vietnam's retail investor base skyrocketed post-COVID: ~7M accounts opened 2020–2023, many under 30.
Gen Z investors often enter with high risk tolerance but limited experience, making them particularly
susceptible to emotional trading patterns.

## The Four Core Biases

### 1. FOMO — Fear of Missing Out
**What it looks like:** Buying a stock after it has already risen 10–15%+ in a session because
everyone is talking about it (social media, Zalo groups, TikTok).

**Vietnam signal:** A stock hits trần (ceiling) multiple consecutive sessions → retail piles in
at the top just before a reversal.

**Educational framing:** "Entering after a strong rally means you're often buying from someone
who bought earlier and is now taking profit."

---

### 2. Loss Aversion (Disposition Effect)
**What it looks like:** Holding losers too long ("waiting to break even") while selling winners
too quickly to lock in gains.

**Measured by:** Win rate vs. average holding duration. If average loss holds 3× longer than
average win, disposition effect is present.

**Vietnam signal:** Stocks hitting sàn (floor) multiple sessions but retail accounts hold,
hoping for a bounce that may not come.

**Educational framing:** "The stock doesn't know what price you bought at. Holding a loser
longer doesn't make it recover faster."

---

### 3. Overtrading
**What it looks like:** Trading every session even when no clear signal exists, driven by
the excitement of activity rather than a strategy.

**Measured by:** High trade frequency with negative or breakeven net returns. Fees + tax
erode returns significantly in Vietnam (0.15–0.25% per trade).

**Vietnam context:** VSD tax = 0.1% of sell value (regardless of profit/loss).
Frequent trading with small moves can result in net losses even with a positive win rate.

**Educational framing:** "Each trade costs money before you've even made a decision.
High frequency without edge = donating to the exchange."

---

### 4. Anchoring
**What it looks like:** Fixating on a specific price (e.g., "I'll sell when it gets back to
10,000 VND") rather than evaluating the stock on current fundamentals.

**Vietnam signal:** Heavily discussed "target prices" in community groups — users waiting
for round numbers (10k, 20k, 50k, 100k) to act.

**Educational framing:** "Market price is set by millions of buyers and sellers right now,
not by where you bought or what someone predicted months ago."

---

## Paper Trading Behaviour Patterns to Watch

| Pattern | Signal | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Buys mostly after 2%+ single-day gains | FOMO-driven momentum chasing |
| Average loss hold > 2× average win hold | Disposition effect |
| >3 trades per week, negative overall P&L | Overtrading |
| Repeatedly re-enters same stock at same price range | Anchoring |
| Portfolio concentrated in 1–2 stocks | Underdiversification / overconfidence |

## How to Present This to Users

- Frame as **observation**, not judgment: "Your trades show a pattern of X" not "You are doing X wrong"
- Always compare to **their stated goal** from onboarding: if they said "long-term wealth", point out
  how the pattern conflicts with that goal
- Suggest **one specific change**: don't overwhelm with all biases at once
- Use Vietnamese emotional vocabulary: "lo lắng" (anxiety), "tiếc nuối" (regret), "hứng khởi" (excitement)
