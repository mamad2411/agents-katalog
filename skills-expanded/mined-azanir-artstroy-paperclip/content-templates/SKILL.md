# Content Templates

Templates and structure guidelines for creating content across channels.

## Blog Post Structure

1. **Headline** — clear, benefit-driven, includes primary keyword (≤ 60 characters for SEO)
2. **Introduction** (100–150 words) — hook with question, stat, bold claim, or scenario; state what's covered; include primary keyword
3. **Body sections** (3–5 sections) — each with H2 subheading; one core idea per section with evidence, examples, or data
4. **Conclusion** (75–100 words) — key takeaways, reinforce main message, call to action
5. **Meta description** — under 160 characters, includes primary keyword, compels click

## Social Media Structure

- **Hook** — first line grabs attention (question, bold statement, number)
- **Body** — 2–4 concise points or short narrative
- **CTA** — what should the reader do next (comment, click, share, tag)
- **Hashtags** — 3–5 relevant (platform-dependent; LinkedIn: 3 max; Twitter: 1–2)

## Email Newsletter Structure

- **Subject line** — under 50 characters, creates curiosity or states clear value
- **Preview text** — complements subject, does not repeat it
- **Header** — visual anchor and one-line value statement
- **Body** — 2–3 content blocks, each scannable with a bold intro sentence
- **Primary CTA** — one clear action per email
- **Footer** — unsubscribe link, company info, social links

## Channel Guidelines

### LinkedIn

- Professional but human; paragraph breaks for readability
- Personal stories and lessons perform well
- 1,300 characters is the sweet spot before "see more"
- 3 hashtags max

### Twitter/X

- Concise and punchy; strong opening words
- Threads for longer narratives
- First tweet must hook independently (people RT without reading thread)

### Blog

- Write for technical professionals (Flesch-Kincaid grade 10–14 for ArtStroy)
- Short paragraphs (2–4 sentences)
- Subheadings every 200–300 words
- Include at least one data point per section
- Active voice throughout
- Front-load key information in each section

## Headline Formulas

- **How to [achieve result] without [common obstacle]** — "How to Double Playwright Coverage Without Slowing CI"
- **[Number] [adjective] ways to [achieve result]** — "5 Ways to Reduce OWASP Findings in Node.js APIs"
- **Why [common belief] is wrong (and what to do instead)**
- **[Do this], not [that]** — "Test the API, Not the Mock"
- **[Topic]: what [audience] needs to know** — "CSP Headers: What QA Engineers Need to Know"

## Hook Formulas (Opening Lines)

- **Surprising statistic**: "73% of QA teams say their biggest bottleneck is flaky tests, not test coverage."
- **Contrarian statement**: "The best pentesting report is the one that makes developers angry."
- **Question**: "When was the last time your CI pipeline caught a security regression?"
- **Bold claim**: "Most Playwright selectors break not because of Playwright — because of the tests."

## CTA Best Practices

- Use action verbs: "Get", "Start", "Download", "Join", "Try", "See"
- Be specific: "Read the full OWASP guide" is better than "Learn more"
- Reduce risk: "No login required", "All examples are open source"
- One primary CTA per page or email

## SEO Fundamentals

- One primary keyword + 2–3 secondary keywords per piece
- Primary keyword in: headline, first paragraph, one subheading, meta description
- Use secondary keywords naturally in body and subheadings
- Do not keyword-stuff — write for humans first
- Internal links: 2–3 links to related ArtStroy articles
- External links: 1–2 links to authoritative sources
