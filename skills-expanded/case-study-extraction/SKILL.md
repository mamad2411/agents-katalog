# Case Study Extraction

### When to use
After a significant client project closes, extract a deployable proof asset before the engagement fades from memory. A case study is a 3–5 minute LinkedIn post or a 500-word document that converts prospects 3–5x better than generic positioning. Run this within 30 days of project completion while details are fresh and client is still celebrating wins.

### The framework
1. **Schedule a debrief interview.** Spend 30 minutes with your champion (the person who hired you) asking: What did they expect before you arrived? What changed? What surprised them? What would they tell a peer in the same situation? Record or take detailed notes.
2. **Extract the before/during/after arc.** Before: their challenge, constraint, or gap (the status quo). During: your intervention and key decisions (the method: brief; don't oversell process). After: the measurable improvement in their terms (revenue, time, efficiency, risk reduction, staff retention, customer satisfaction).
3. **Quantify the result in their language, not yours.** "Increased conversion rate by 23%" beats "implemented a new sales process." "Cut customer acquisition cost from $200 to $84" beats "optimized the funnel." Dollar figures, percentages, and time-saved metrics resonate; methodological descriptions do not.
4. **Write a 3–5 sentence narrative.** Open with the problem (1 sentence). Middle: the intervention and why it worked (2 sentences). Close: the outcome and one insight or lesson (2 sentences). Keep it conversational, not marketing-speak.
5. **Add one insight or lesson.** After describing the result, ask your client: What would you tell a peer facing the same challenge? Capture their unfiltered answer. That's the credibility knot: not your genius, but real client voice.
6. **Attach a proof metric.** One number (revenue generated, time saved, percentage improvement, or staff impact) that proves the project mattered. Specificity beats roundness; $47k in new ARR generated beats "significant revenue impact."

### How to apply it
Your client is a SaaS founder who engaged you to systematize their sales pipeline. Before: chaos. They had no consistent process, deals fell through cracks, and sales reps were doing everything differently. During: you mapped their ideal customer, documented a 6-step discovery call structure, and trained the team on qualification criteria. After: their monthly contract value grew 34% in 6 months while team hours stayed flat.

Interview: "What would you tell another founder?" Client answers: "That most growth problems are process problems, not people problems. We thought we needed to hire, but we just needed structure."

You write: "Sarah, a SaaS founder, was frustrated. Her sales team was scattered; deals were dying at discovery. We spent two weeks mapping her ideal customer profile and built a repeatable discovery framework. Within 6 months, her team closed 34% more ARR without adding headcount. Her insight: 'Growth problems are usually process problems.'"

You post this on LinkedIn with a screenshot or a link to the full case study. Prospects in the SaaS-sales-process niche see proof. One person replies: "I need this." You reply with a calendar link. That post becomes an assets you share for a year; it converts cold prospects and warm inbound leads at a 3–5x rate vs. your "About Us" positioning.

### Common traps
- **Writing in consultant-speak instead of client-speak.** "Implemented a value-based pricing framework" loses the reader. "Raised their fees 30% without losing a single client" wins. Use client language and outcomes.
- **Overselling your process or methodology.** Buyers don't care how you did it. They care that the problem got solved. Bury method; lead with result.
- **Using vague metrics.** "Significant improvement" and "strong results" are noise. "$47k in new annual revenue" and "reduced sales cycle from 90 to 45 days" are facts. One is proof; the other is hope.
- **Waiting too long to capture it.** 3–6 months after the project, details blur and clients move on. Do the interview within 30 days while the win is hot and fresh.
- **Forgetting to ask permission.** Before publishing, send a draft to your client champion and ask: Does this feel accurate? Any details to change? Any concerns about attribution? This step prevents awkwardness and builds goodwill for future referrals.
