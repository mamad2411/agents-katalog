# Slashy research and workflows

Use Slashy to combine live contact data, email history, calendar context, and
research. Discover the current tool catalog before calling it.

## Contact and company research

1. Resolve the person by email when possible; names alone can be ambiguous.
2. Pull the Slashy contact profile and relevant email/meeting history.
3. Use Slashy's enrichment or research operation for LinkedIn, role, company,
   and current news when the request needs it.
4. Separate confirmed account history, third-party enrichment, current news,
   and your inference. Preserve source links and dates returned by tools.
5. Do not overwrite a contact or CRM record unless the user explicitly asks.

## Meeting prep

Build a compact brief with:

- attendee identity, role, and company;
- relationship history and the latest relevant threads;
- prior decisions, promises, and open follow-ups;
- recent company or person news relevant to the meeting;
- likely objectives, risks, and a suggested agenda.

Use the live calendar to identify the meeting and its attendees. If identity or
meeting matching is ambiguous, present the candidates rather than combining
different people or events.

## Reminders and scheduled workflows

- Treat creating, changing, enabling, disabling, or deleting a reminder,
  trigger, or recurring workflow as a write that needs an explicit request.
- Make the trigger, filter, action, cadence, timezone, target account, and stop
  condition concrete before creation.
- For follow-up sweeps, distinguish unanswered sent mail from received mail the
  user owes a reply to; give each category an explicit age threshold and do not
  label either category without checking direction.
- Define exclusions such as automated mail, newsletters, ignored labels, closed
  threads, or threads that already have a draft instead of silently scanning
  every message.
- Prefer drafts for automated outbound email unless the user explicitly asks
  for automatic sending and the server/account permits it.
- After creation, report exactly what will run, when it will run next, which
  account it uses, and whether it sends, drafts, labels, or only reports.

For a one-time scheduled action, confirm the date/time/timezone. For recurring
work, summarize the recurrence in plain language so mistakes are visible.
