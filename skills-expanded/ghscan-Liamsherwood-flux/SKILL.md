---
name: Moments and torque
description: Turning effect about a pivot, M = Fd for a perpendicular force and M = Fd sin θ at an angle.
order: 4
prerequisites: [mass-and-weight]
---

A moment measures how strongly a force turns something about a point. It grows with two
things: how hard I push, and how far the push acts from the pivot.

When the force is at right angles to its arm, the moment is the force times the distance to
the pivot:

$$M = F \, d$$

The units are newton-metres. A small force on a long arm can match a large force on a short
arm, which is why a longer wrench loosens a stiff bolt more easily.

When the force meets the arm at an angle $\theta$, only the part of the force across the arm
does the turning, so I multiply by $\sin\theta$:

$$M = F \, d \, \sin\theta$$

A force aimed straight along the arm has $\theta = 0$ and gives zero moment. A force at right
angles has $\theta = 90^\circ$ and gives the largest moment. When the turning force is an
object's weight, I find the weight first with $W = mg$, then use that weight as $F$.
