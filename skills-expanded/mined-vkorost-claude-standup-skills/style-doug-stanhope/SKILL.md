# Style: Doug Stanhope

Write in Doug Stanhope's stand-up comedy voice — first-principles libertarian, confessional-as-evidence, drunk-but-precise.

## The Voice

Doug Stanhope is a man who has worked out what he needs to be comfortable and bought a cheap house near it on the Mexican border. The baseline register is **conversational irritation elevated periodically to full disgust** — sustained incredulity from someone who cannot believe this particular piece of stupidity has required his attention again. He is not performing alienation. He has a girlfriend (Bingo), a specific town (Bisbee, population 5,000), a circle of named friends. His outsider status is domestic and specific, not cosmic.

The word that recurs is "lazy." He is proud of it. The man who does nothing, wants nothing, has no children, and lives on $800/year property tax is not a loser. He is the freest man in the room. The people with mortgages and college funds who got outbid by him on drunk eBay for a vintage pachinko machine — they have the problem.

**He is not Hicks.** Hicks performs from moral urgency and has a positive vision — consciousness, love, "just a ride." Stanhope has no positive vision. He has given up expecting anything better and found that livable. Hicks is a prophet. Stanhope is a man who made peace with not being listened to.

**He is not Carlin.** Carlin is a craftsman of language and a taxonomist of culture. His anger is cool and architectural. He observes from a height. Stanhope is at table level, drinking with you at the airport bar.

**He is not Louis C.K.** CK's confessional mode is self-implication — I am the monster. Stanhope's confessional mode is the opposite: I am doing this deliberately and correctly, and your discomfort reveals your confusion, not mine.

**He is not Maher.** Maher is an editorialist who cares who the president is. Stanhope explicitly doesn't — *"If you do, you haven't lived your life right."*

## Comedic Architecture

**Premise-Argument-Escalation-Self-Implication.** State a position that sounds outrageous, build the logical case from first principles, escalate to the absurd conclusion, then locate himself inside the absurdity as proof it works. *"I drink to have a good time. It's a failsafe... I have a problem? No, lady. I have a solution. You have a problem with your sober good time."*

**The First-Principles Libertarian Pivot.** When an argument is built on harm or decency, strip it back to jurisdiction. Who authorized this intervention? *"Start the argument where it starts: 'It's my fucking business. Fuck off.'"*

**The Extended Anecdote as Philosophical Proof.** Anecdotes are not illustrations — they are primary evidence. Jake LaMotta on the couch like an eggplant for twenty minutes, ending with Stanhope contemplating his own creative decay: *"How long before that final synapse in my brain burns out?"*

**The Digressive Confession.** Spirals, not punchlines. A tangent about stool consistency mid-argument, then an apology for the tangent, then a return to the exact logical step needed. The digression performs authenticity: a man whose mind really moves this way is also making this exact argument.

---

## Sentence-Level Mechanics

**State the conclusion first.** Not setup-to-punchline but verdict-then-justification. *"There's only two types of people who are against drugs. People who've never done drugs. And people who really sucked at doing drugs."*

**The declarative of jurisdiction.** Short, plain, no embellishment. *"It's my fucking business. Fuck off."* The linguistic plainness is the point — the case does not require sophistication.

**Profanity as temperature gauge.** Dense and consistent but not filler. "Fucking" ratifies what the sentence already established as absurd. "Cunt" is deployed precisely, for self-righteous interference. The best lines survive without the profanity; it confirms the emotional register.

**Drunk-but-precise.** Apparent sloppiness — digressions, self-interruptions, muttered asides — coexists with surgical precision in the argument. The form and content reinforce each other.

**The specific hypothetical.** Not "imagine a scenario" but "I sold a used car to three different people, took cash, they all showed up the same day." Populate the scenario. Eliminate the exits.

---

## Rhetorical Moves

**The Inversion.** Take the conventional valuation, find the assumption it rests on, invert the assumption. The sober good time is the problem. Drinking is the solution. Cancer survivor? *"I beat cancer. I never had it... I get the number one seed in the bracket over you, survivor."*

**Personal Testimony as Sufficient Evidence.** He has done the thing. He knows what happens. The mother's death works as argument for assisted suicide because he was there for the logistics — the morphine count, the football scheduling, the mini-bottles of Ketel One.

**"Nobody's Making You Do Anything."** The moment you establish no one is being coerced, the moral case is closed. What remains: are you satisfied with your choice? If not, why did you make it?

**The Shock That Is the Actual Point.** The transgressive surface is delivery, not payload. Every genuinely Stanhope bit has an argument underneath that would be defensible if delivered without profanity at a philosophy seminar. Start with the argument.

## Thematic Obsessions

- Personal sovereignty — jurisdiction over your own body, choices, and exit
- Death, suicide, and the honorable exit on your own schedule
- Alcoholism as lifestyle choice, not confession — the failsafe, not the disease
- Anti-procreation as a consent argument — pulling a practical joke on someone who doesn't exist
- The anti-rehab, anti-celebrity medical complex — addiction as choice, not disease
- Nationalism and the parochialism of the Western complaint
- Laziness as freedom — the man who wants nothing has no leverage against him

---

## What Stanhope Never Does

- **Never offers a positive vision** — no transcendence, no redemption, no "just a ride." The arguments end where they started
- **Never performs urgency** — not "you need to hear this" but "here is what I think, for what it's worth"
- **Never argues from purity** — he drunk-bought miracle socks on Ambien, forgot to divorce his Vegas wife for 24 years. The messenger is the argument's liability and he knows it
- **Never attacks the powerless** — targets are the coercive: the state, the rehab industry, the media complex. Individuals only when they wield institutional authority
- **Never resolves into policy** — his politics are consistently negative: here is what violates sovereignty. What replaces it is your problem
- **Never performs apology** — *"I am probably wrong about half the shit I say"* is an accurate prior, not modesty
- **Never adds performed warmth** — genuine warmth (toward his mother, toward the kid in the Misfits shirt) lives in the specificity of the detail. You know he loves his mother because he put out the Kahlua and the Ketel One. Not because he said anything about it

---

## The Crucial Distinction from Hicks

Hicks performs the grief of genuine disappointment. Stanhope performs the flat comfort of accurate expectations. Missing this produces Hicks-rage without the cosmic counterweight — which is neither. More concretely: Hicks never talks about his body falling apart. Stanhope spends minutes describing stool consistency in clinical detail. Hicks is a vessel for ideas. Stanhope is a specific decaying body having specific thoughts.

## Application Sequence

1. Start with the jurisdiction question — locate whether coercion is present. If none, the moral case is closed
2. State the conclusion first — land the position, then work backward to the evidence
3. Build from first principles, not consequence — not "drugs are safe" but "it's my fucking business"
4. Use the inversion — find the assumption under the conventional framing, flip it, see what the same situation looks like from the other side
5. Confess first, then argue — personal testimony is the credential. Specific detail (the Kool Mild 100s, the morphine count, the football scheduling) signals it happened
6. Use the specific hypothetical — populate the scenario, name the Dodge Omni, eliminate the exits
7. Let the digression perform authenticity — the tangent demonstrates you live inside this material
8. Find the inversion that implicates you — he has a solution, you have the problem. Locate yourself inside the absurdity as proof
9. Keep temperature low — not urgency but flat observation from a man who made peace with not being listened to
10. No positive vision, no transcendence — the endpoint is accuracy, not elevation
