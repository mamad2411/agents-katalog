# Chef Training Progression

Professional cooking skill is not talent — it's a curriculum: culinary schools run technique→station ladders, kitchens run commis→chef progressions, and competitors (Bocuse d'Or teams) train like athletes — months of rehearsing ONE menu until it's reflex. All of it translates to a home kitchen. This skill is the coach.

Read `references/curriculum-ladder.md` for the full level-by-level curriculum with graduation tests.

## How professionals actually learn (the five mechanisms)

1. **Fundamentals before dishes.** Schools drill knife cuts, stocks, mother sauces, and egg cookery for MONTHS before "recipes" — because dishes are combinations of ~25 base techniques. Learn techniques and you get all dishes free; learn dishes and you get only dishes.
2. **Repetition with feedback (deliberate practice).** A commis makes the same brunoise a thousand times — but with a chef checking each one. The magic isn't repetition, it's repetition + immediate correction + slight stretch. Home version: cook the same dish 5× in two weeks, change ONE variable each time, write down what changed. Five roast chickens teach more than fifty different recipes.
3. **Palate training is literal training.** "The palate is a muscle": pros do blind tastings, salt-ladder drills, single-ingredient meditations. Taste is the ONLY sense that guides seasoning — and it's trainable in weeks (drills below).
4. **Mise en place as a worldview.** Everything prepped, in place, before fire. Kitchens enforce it because under pressure you fall to your level of preparation — never rise to the occasion. It's the transferable superpower (Bocuse-level teams and line cooks agree on this one thing above all).
5. **Rehearsal and benchmarks (the competition method).** Bocuse d'Or teams practice one program for months with dress rehearsals against the clock, judged against defined standards. Home version: before an event, cook the centerpiece once as a full dress rehearsal; define the benchmark ("crust shatters, center 54°C") and grade yourself against IT, not against vibes (benchmarks per dish in `chef-world-classics`).


## Attention and training load

Technique level and executive-function load are independent. Do not place an experienced cook at Level 0 merely because they have ADHD/СДВГ, fatigue, or prefer one active task at a time. Use `chef-accessible-cooking` to structure execution while keeping the technical drill at the correct level.

On a low-resource day, prescribe **one drill on one familiar dish** with one success criterion; do not turn every practice request into a daily curriculum.

## The palate gym (10 min/day, transformative in a month)

- **Salt ladder:** split a soup/puree into 4 cups, salt them at 0.6/0.9/1.2/1.5%, taste in order. You are calibrating your internal salinometer — the single highest-ROI drill in cooking.
- **Fix-the-dish drill:** deliberately make a bland or over-acidic base, then rescue it with the balance levers (`chef-flavor-balance` repair table). Diagnosis under pressure = the actual chef skill.
- **Single-element meditation:** eat one ingredient (tomato, cheese, an olive) with full attention: attack, mid-palate, finish, texture, aroma via retro-nasal breath. Sounds silly, builds vocabulary — you can't adjust what you can't name.
- **Blind pairs:** two brands/versions of one thing (butter, soy sauce, canned tomato) blind. Differences pop when labels stop talking.
- **Taste-as-you-go, always:** every stage of every dish — raw sauce, mid-simmer, pre-serve. Every taste is a rep. (Restriction: not raw-meat mixtures — fry a mini test-patty; that's the pro move too.)

## Deliberate-practice project templates

| Project | Structure |
|---|---|
| **The ×5 dish** | One dish, 5 sessions, one variable per session (salt timing / heat level / rest time...). Journal each. Owns the dish forever. |
| **Knife month** | 10 min/day on onions, carrots, herbs; film your hands weekly — visible progress fuels the loop. Goal: consistency, not speed (speed is a byproduct). |
| **Mother-sauce week** | One mother sauce per day + one derivative (`chef-sauces-guide`); by Sunday you understand “sauce” as a system. |
| **Mystery-basket drill** | 5 random ingredients, 45 min, no recipe (chain `chef-pantry-improviser`). The improvisation muscle — also the most fun. |
| **The timed service** | Cook a 3-dish menu to land simultaneously at a set minute (chain `chef-menu-event-planning` timelines). This is a home version of line pressure — do it before hosting, not during. |
| **Failure autopsy** | Every flop gets 3 written lines: what happened / probable mechanism (consult the guides' failure tables) / the test for next time. Pros fail constantly — they just invoice their failures for data. |

## Habits that mark trained cooks (install these first)
- Mise en place before flame. • Clean-as-you-go (safety AND speed — `chef-food-safety`). • Sharp knife, honed weekly (dull knives cause the injuries and the ugly cuts). • Thermometer + scale as defaults, senses as verifiers (`chef-constraints-adaptation` — cues + numbers, both). • Taste before serving, every time, no exceptions. • Recipe read TWICE fully before shopping. • Write on your recipes — a recipe without margin notes is a recipe you haven't learned yet.

## What to learn next (the router)
Ask: what did you cook this week, what failed, what scared you? → Failure = drill its mechanism (route to the relevant guide's failure table). Fear = schedule it as a low-stakes weeknight experiment (fear of caramel/yeast/whole-fish dissolves on contact). Boredom = climb the ladder in `references/curriculum-ladder.md` to the next level's project.
