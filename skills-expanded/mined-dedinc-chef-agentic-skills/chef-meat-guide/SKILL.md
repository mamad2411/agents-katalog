# Chef Meat Guide

Meat cookery is one decision followed by physics: **match the cut to the method**. Everything else (temps, times, salt) follows from that match. Get it wrong and no seasoning saves the dish.

Read `references/cuts-and-methods.md` for per-species cut tables and temperatures. Read `references/brines-marinades-rubs.md` for salting schedules, brine ratios, and marinade design.

## The one rule that governs everything

Muscles are either **tender/lean** (little connective tissue — loin, breast, tenderloin) or **tough/collagen-rich** (hard-working — shoulder, shank, brisket, thigh-adjacent cuts).

- **Tender cuts** → fast + hot + short (sear, grill, roast hot). Done when they hit target internal temp. Every minute past it removes juice permanently.
- **Collagen cuts** → slow + moist + long (braise, stew, low roast). Collagen melts to gelatin from ~70°C over hours; the meat first toughens, then surrenders. "Cook until tender" is literal — time is measured in probe-slides-in, not minutes.

Mismatches are the #1 meat failure: braising a tenderloin makes cardboard; flash-frying a shank makes rubber.

## Universal protocol (all meats)

1. **Salt early.** 0.8–1.2% of meat weight, ideally 40 min to 24 h ahead (thick cuts: overnight, uncovered in the fridge = dry brine). Salting 5–25 min before cooking is the one bad window (moisture pulled out but not reabsorbed).
2. **Dry the surface.** Wet meat steams. Pat dry aggressively before any sear.
3. **Temper thick cuts** 20–40 min at room temp for even doneness (skip for thin cuts).
4. **Sear for flavor, not "sealing".** Hot pan, high-smoke-point fat, don't crowd, don't move it until it releases.
5. **Cook to temperature, not time.** A thermometer is the single biggest upgrade in home meat cookery. Pull 3–5°C below target — carryover finishes it.
6. **Rest.** Steaks/chops 5–10 min, roasts 15–30 min, loosely tented. Cutting immediately dumps the juices on the board.
7. **Slice against the grain**, especially flank/skirt/brisket — it halves perceived toughness.

## Key temperatures: separate quality from safety

For ordinary home safety endpoints, use `chef-food-safety`: poultry **74°C**; ground non-poultry meat **71°C**; ground poultry **74°C**; whole cuts of beef/pork/veal/lamb **63°C + 3 min rest**.

Lower steak/duck or low-temperature targets are **culinary texture choices**, not interchangeable with those ordinary agency minima. If the user intentionally wants a lower-temperature or sous-vide process, state that distinction and use a complete applicable process rather than a generic timer. Do not pull chicken at 63–65°C and call carryover a universal safety method.

For braises, safety is usually satisfied before the desired collagen texture; keep cooking until fork-tender.

## Method-selection quick logic

- Weeknight + tender cut → pan-sear + pan sauce (see `chef-sauces-guide`).
- Cheap cut + time → braise: sear hard → soften aromatics → deglaze → liquid ⅔ up the meat → 150–160°C oven, lid ajar, 2.5–4 h.
- Crowd/roast → low-and-slow then hot finish (reverse sear) for even edge-to-edge doneness.
- Ground meat → don't overwork (toughens), season the mix at 1% salt only right before forming (salt early = sausage texture — desirable in sausage, wrong in burgers).

## Pairing (see chef-flavor-balance for full tables)

Beef→pepper/mustard/mushroom/red wine · pork→apple/sage/mustard/fennel · lamb→rosemary/cumin/yogurt/mint · chicken→lemon/garlic/thyme/paprika · duck→orange/cherry/star anise. Fatty meats demand an acid counterpoint somewhere on the plate.
