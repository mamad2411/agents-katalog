# Chef Indian Cuisine

Indian cooking is applied spice chemistry: WHEN a spice enters the pot matters more than how much. The same five spices produce a hundred dishes depending on timing, fat, and grind. Spice identities and blend formulas live in `chef-spice-guide`; this skill is the reaction engine that uses them.

## The chemistry core (why, not just how)

1. **Fat-solubility law:** most spice flavor compounds (curcumin, capsaicin, terpenes) are fat-soluble, barely water-soluble. Spices boiled in watery sauce = flavor locked in particles. Spices BLOOMED in hot fat = flavor extracted and distributed. This is why every Indian recipe starts or ends with fat + spices, and why "stir curry powder into the simmering pot" recipes taste flat (`chef-recipe-optimizer` auto-flag).
2. **Tadka/tarka (the bloom):** whole spices into hot oil/ghee 30–60 s until they sizzle-pop-fragrant (mustard seeds MUST crackle and dance; cumin darkens one shade). Two positions: opening tadka (base of the dish) and finishing tadka (poured sizzling over dal at service — aroma at maximum because volatiles have no time to escape). Cliff-edge: burnt = bitter pot, start over; keep a lid nearby for popping seeds.
3. **Whole vs ground timing:** whole spices = slow-release, go in early (cinnamon, cardamom, clove, bay in hot fat first). Ground spices = fast-release + fast-burn, go in mid (onto the softened base, 30–60 s, with a splash of water as insurance). Delicate aromatics (garam masala, kasuri methi) = at the END, off heat — they are perfume, not foundation.
4. **Freshly ground beats pre-ground by months:** volatile oils leave ground spice in weeks. A coffee grinder for whole spices is the single biggest Indian-food upgrade (`chef-spice-guide` freshness tests).
5. **Souring agents are regional dialects:** tomato (north), tamarind (south), yogurt (Punjab/Hyderabad), kokum/lime — the acid lever wears local costume (`chef-flavor-balance`).

## The base grammar (the engine of most curries)
**Onion → ginger-garlic → tomato → ground spices → main ingredient → liquid → finish.**
- Onions have THREE doneness dialects: soft-translucent (light curries), golden (standard), deep-brown/bhuna (rich gravies, biryani) — the color you stop at defines the dish's soul. Rushing this stage is the #1 home failure; honest timing: golden 15 min, brown 25–30 (doneness table rules apply).
- Ginger-garlic paste (1:1) is useful mise en place; refrigerate only for a source-supported short window or freeze in portions. Do not infer a week-long allowance from added oil.
- **Bhuna (fry-till-oil-separates):** after tomatoes+spices, cook until fat visibly beads at the edges — the cue that raw tomato water is gone and the masala is DONE. This visual checkpoint is the Indian equivalent of the fork-test: wait for it, always.

## Dal mastery (the daily protein of a billion people)
Red lentils 20 min / mung 30 / whole dals 60+ (or pressure — equipment guide) with turmeric, to porridge-soft; salt late; then the finishing tadka (ghee + cumin + garlic + chili + asafoetida) poured over. Dal + rice + yogurt + pickle = complete protein, complete meal, near-zero cost (`chef-kitchen-economics` MVP; `diet-goal-nutrition` staple). Legume science in grains guide.

## Regional map
| Region | Character | Steal |
|---|---|---|
| North (Punjab) | Dairy-rich: ghee, cream, paneer, yogurt marinades; wheat breads; tandoor | Butter-chicken family, paneer dishes, naan logic |
| South (Tamil/Kerala/Andhra) | Coconut, curry leaves, mustard seeds, tamarind, rice + FERMENTS (dosa/idli batters — ferment skill crossover) | Coconut-tamarind curries, the mustard-curryleaf tadka, sambar |
| East (Bengal) | Mustard oil, panch phoron (5-spice), fish, sweets | Panch phoron on vegetables; mustard-fish poach |
| West (Gujarat/Maharashtra/Goa) | Sweet-sour-spicy triangles; Goa: vinegar+chili (vindaloo is a preservation dish!) | Vindaloo as vinegar-braise; dhansak lentil-meat fusion |
| Hyderabad | Biryani capital: layered rice + marinated meat, dum (sealed-pot steam) | Dum technique = oven-braise cousin; saffron-milk finishing |

## The repertoire ladder
(1) Dal + rice + finishing tadka (the whole chemistry in one cheap pot) → (2) chana masala (base grammar practice, pantry-only) → (3) butter chicken / paneer makhani (bhuna checkpoint training) → (4) a south-Indian coconut curry (new tadka dialect) → (5) weekend biryani with dum (event-planning timeline recommended). Breads: chapati/roti on skillet (no-oven bread family in `chef-baking-doughs-guide`), naan in skillet-broiler (tandoor mechanism swap, `chef-equipment-gastrophysics`).

## Heat calibration for every table
Chili is an independent dial: build the dish full-flavor mild, serve heat at the table (chili oil, fresh chilies, pickle) — kids and heat-averse guests eat the same dish (`chef-kids-sensory` deconstruction law). Dairy rescues over-chilied mouths (casein binds capsaicin); water does not — tell the guests.
