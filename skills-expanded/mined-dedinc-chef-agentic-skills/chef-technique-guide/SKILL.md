# Chef Technique Guide

Recipes are 20% ingredients and 80% execution. The same ingredient list produces garbage or greatness depending on heat control, pan discipline, and sequencing. This skill encodes the execution layer.

## The heat model (internalize this, it explains everything)

- **Water caps temperature at 100°C.** Anything wet — crowded pans, unpatted meat, lidded sautés — cooks at boiling temp: no browning, no crust, gray food. Maillard needs ~140°C+, caramelization ~160°C+.
- **Heat is a rate, doneness is a state.** High heat cooks the outside fast; the inside follows slowly. Thick items need lower/longer or two-stage (sear + gentle finish). Thin items need violence and speed.
- **The pan is a battery.** Preheating stores energy; food withdraws it. Cold food into a barely-heated pan flatlines the temperature — that's why the first pancake fails and why batches beat piles.
- **Carryover is real:** interior temp keeps climbing after heat stops (+3–8°C depending on mass). Pull early; rest finishes.

## Ten commandments of execution

1. **Mise en place**: cut, measure, and line up everything BEFORE heat. Mid-cook chopping is how garlic burns and steps get skipped.
2. Preheat properly: pan is ready when water drops dance (Leidenfrost) or oil shimmers. Oil into hot pan, food into hot oil.
3. Don't crowd. Ever. Two batches take 10 extra minutes; one crowded batch ruins the dish.
4. Don't fiddle. Food releases when the crust forms; prying early tears it.
5. Dry surfaces before fat. Water + hot oil = spatter and steam.
6. Taste at every stage; adjust in small moves.
7. Use the right fat for the heat: high-smoke (refined sunflower, ghee, avocado) for searing/frying; butter and extra-virgin for medium heat and finishing.
8. Rest proteins; rest grains; rest doughs. Resting is a cooking step, not idleness.
9. Deglaze fond — the browned stuck bits are concentrated flavor, not mess.
10. Clean as you go; a buried station produces buried thinking.

## Method selection matrix

| Goal / ingredient state | Method |
|---|---|
| Tender protein, crust wanted | Hard sear (+ oven finish if thick) |
| Tough/collagen anything | Braise or stew, 150–160°C oven, hours |
| Vegetables, max flavor | High roast 200–220°C |
| Delicate fish/eggs/dairy | Gentle: poach, steam, low oven, bain-marie |
| Even edge-to-edge doneness on thick cuts | Reverse sear (low oven → rest → violent sear) |
| Speed + small pieces | Stir-fry / sauté: hottest pan, staged additions |
| Crisp coating | Deep/shallow fry 170–185°C, batches, rack-drain |
| Free deep flavor from scraps | Stock: bones/veg, barely simmering, skimmed, 1–4 h (see references) |

## Knife work (minimum viable)
- Sharp knife is safer than dull — it goes where aimed. Hone often, sharpen sometimes.
- Claw grip; board stabilized with damp towel underneath.
- Uniformity beats speed: even pieces = even cooking. Recipe cut sizes are engineering specs, not suggestions.
- Master three cuts: medium dice, thin slice, chiffonade. Everything else is a variation.

## When executing any recipe
Before starting, scan for: which step is heat-critical (needs full attention), which steps can overlap, what must be prepped ahead. Announce these in the recipe's Critical Points. A recipe that interleaves "stir constantly" with "meanwhile, chop" was written by someone who never cooked it.
