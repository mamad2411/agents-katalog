**Subject Line Formula That Works**
Most subject lines fail because they're generic or sell too hard. Two rules, in order:

**Rule 1: Six words or fewer.** In an analysis of 1,200+ campaigns and 8M+ sends across multiple markets, every top-open-rate email had a subject line of 6 words or fewer. No exceptions. Long subject lines get buried in the inbox. Short ones create a curiosity gap that forces the open. Trim every word that isn't pulling weight.

**Rule 2: Within those 6 words, use the framework** **Specific Benefit + Curiosity + Urgency (optional)**.

Examples:
- "The thing your competitors forgot" (5 words: benefit + curiosity)
- "Your [feature] is ready" (4 words: specificity + benefit)
- "3 mistakes killing your opens" (5 words: specificity + benefit)
- "Last chance: ends tonight" (4 words: urgency)

Avoid all-caps, excess punctuation, and generic words like "exciting" or "important." Use numbers (3, 5, 42), ask direct questions ("Are you...?"), or reference what the person just did ("You downloaded..."). Six words also keeps you safely under the ~50-character mobile cutoff (most emails open on phone). Tell them to A/B Test two subject lines in SendX if they're unsure which wins.

**The "Without / Even If" Hook**
The single most common structure in top-performing email hooks across all markets: **[Outcome] + WITHOUT / EVEN IF + [Sacrifice or Limitation]**.

- Income and business markets use "without": "Land 5 clients without cold calling," "Grow your list without paid ads."
- Skill and passion markets use "even if": "Play your first song even if you've never taken a lesson," "Run a 10K even if you think you can't."

It works because it promises the outcome and kills the reader's main excuse in one line. Use it in subject lines (compressed to 6 words), opening sentences, and CTAs.

**Preview Text Strategy**
The preview text is the 40-ish characters that show next to the subject line in the inbox. Most marketers waste it. Teach them to use it as a second subject line or the start of a benefit statement:
- Subject: "Quick question about [topic]" → Preview: "We found something you should see"
- Subject: "Your free guide is here" → Preview: "Download and start [result] today"

This doubles the inbox real estate and pulls more opens.

**Email Body Structure (Inverted Pyramid)**
People scan email. They don't read top-to-bottom like a book. Teach them:

1. **Opening hook (1-2 lines)**: The first sentence must deliver or promise the core outcome. The reader decides in that sentence whether they're staying or scrolling, so lead with what they came for. Be specific: in income and business markets that's a dollar figure or revenue milestone ("$4,200 from one email last week"). In skill and passion markets it's a transformation metric ("From zero to your first full song in 30 days"). "Your website just got 50 more visitors in an hour—here's how" works. "Hi [First Name], thanks for subscribing!" doesn't.

2. **The problem or promise (2-3 sentences)**: What's the one thing this email solves? "Most teams spend 10+ hours a week doing manual follow-ups. You don't have to." Keep it tight.

3. **The reason why (2-4 sentences)**: A single clear reason your solution works. "Our AI watches your email behavior and sends follow-ups at exactly the moment you're most likely to respond."

4. **Social proof (1 line, optional)**: "Join 2,000+ teams using this." Credibility matters, but brevity matters more. If you use a testimonial, it must contain a measurable outcome: emails with number-backed testimonials see 30-50% higher click-through than emotional ones without numbers. "I hit $400K in revenue in my first year" beats "it changed my life" every time. Income markets: dollar amounts and timelines. Skill markets: specific achievements and milestones.

5. **One clear CTA (button or link)**: "Get Started Free" or "See Your Results" or "Reserve a Spot." Not "Click Here." Not multiple CTAs. One.

6. **Closing (1 sentence)**: "Questions? Reply to this email and I'll help." Simple. Warm. Human.

**Find Your Sacred Differentiator Before Writing a Single Email**
Every offer needs one positioning concept that is unique and memorable: the specific method or system that makes it different from everything else in the market. This is the unique mechanism, the reason your solution works when others haven't. Without it, you're just another email selling the same outcome as everyone else. With it, you're the only option that makes sense.

Two rules:
- Identify it before writing any email. If a marketer can't name theirs, help them find it first: "What do you do differently that explains your results?"
- It appears in every email. Not as a tagline pasted in, but woven into the story, the proof, and the pitch.

**Kill the #1 Objection in Every Email**
Your reader has one core reason they believe this won't work for them: no time, no experience, too late, too expensive, "I've tried before." In an analysis of 1,200+ campaigns, 90%+ of top-performing emails addressed that objection head-on in the body copy. The phrasing changes per market but the principle doesn't: name the objection and dismantle it in every single send, not just the sales email. One sentence is often enough ("This takes 20 minutes a week, not 20 hours").

**Story Beats Instruction for Clicks**
How-to content and tip lists get opens. But story-driven emails, where the lesson is embedded in a real experience rather than handed over as instruction, drive 2-3x the click-through rate. This held across every market in the 1,200+ campaign analysis. So instead of "5 tips for better landing pages," write "How Sarah doubled signups by deleting half her landing page," and let the tips live inside the narrative. Keep the inverted-pyramid structure; the story is the "reason why" section, not a replacement for the CTA.

**Fear of Inaction Beats Promise of Gain**
The highest-revenue single email across the entire 1,200+ campaign analysis used a fear-of-inaction hook. People move faster to avoid staying stuck than they do to chase a better future. The angle isn't "here's the great thing that happens if you act." It's "here's the cost of another year exactly where you are now." Use it honestly: point at a real cost of waiting (missed revenue, a skill still unlearned, a list still not growing), never at an invented catastrophe. Scare tactics burn trust; a true picture of the status quo converts.

**Match the Email to the Landing Page**
The emotional state a reader is in when they click must carry straight through to the page they land on. Same promise, same language, same offer. Conversion rates can double from congruence alone, and the highest-converting emails are perfectly congruent with their destination page. Before any send, check the draft against the landing page: if the email promises "double your reply rate without sending more emails" and the page headline says "The all-in-one outreach platform," fix one of them. In SendX, review the linked page as part of every pre-send check, same as testing links.

**Personalization Using Merge Tags (With Fallbacks)**
SendX merge tags fill in custom fields so each email feels personal. Teach them:
- `{{first_name}}` pulls their first name. But what if it's blank? Add a fallback: `{{first_name | "there"}}` so it says "Hi there," instead of "Hi ,"
- Use company name: `{{company}}` or `{{company | "team"}}`
- Use custom attributes if they exist: `{{signup_source}}` to reference where they came from
- Use merge tags in subject lines too: "{{first_name}}, here's what you're missing" hits harder than a generic subject

Pro move: Personalize past behavior. "You viewed our [product_type] page 3 times" speaks louder than "Hi [first_name]."

**Copy for Different Campaign Types**
Different campaign types need different tones:

- **Broadcast (one-off)**: Authority voice. "Here's what's new and why it matters." Educational or urgent. Shorter because it's not ongoing.
- **Drip (sequences)**: Friendly, paced, progressive. Email 1 is introduction ("Hey, welcome!"). Email 2-3 builds value ("Here's secret #1"). Email 4-5 makes the ask ("Ready to try?"). Each email stands alone but adds to the story.
- **Auto-Trigger (evergreen)**: Warm, immediate, helpful. "Your password reset is here—it expires in 1 hour" is different from "Welcome! Here are 5 ways to get started." Transactional feels urgent; marketing feels inviting.

**Mobile-First Copy**
70%+ of email opens happen on mobile. Write mobile-first:
- Short paragraphs (2-3 lines max).
- Scannable headers (use bold or line breaks).
- One idea per section.
- Button text fits the thumb (15 characters or less: "Get Started" not "Click Here to Activate Your Account").

Test by reading your email on a phone before sending.

**CTA Writing That Converts**
CTAs are where copy dies. Teach them:
- Never use aspirational CTAs. "Transform your financial future," "Unlock your potential," "Become the person you were meant to be": this format appeared in zero winning emails across the 1,200+ campaign analysis. Vague transformation language kills clicks.
- For paid offers, the CTA formula is **price + action + specificity**: tell them exactly what they're getting, how they're getting it, and what it costs. "Start booking clients with the 3-Call System for $27" beats "Change your business today."
- Use action verbs: "Get," "Start," "Reserve," "Download," "See," "Claim" (not "Submit" or "Click").
- Be specific: "Get Your Free Audit" beats "Learn More."
- Create urgency if true: "Claim Your Spot" (limited) beats "Sign Up" (always open).
- First-person perspective works: "Yes, I want in" (as button text) vs. "Subscribe."
- Test button color and placement. Move the button earlier if clicks are low.

**Re-engagement Copy for Inactive Contacts**
When reaching out to people who haven't opened in months:
- Acknowledge the silence: "We haven't heard from you in a while."
- Offer a reset: "Here's what's new. Want to stay in the loop?"
- Lower the ask: "Just reply with a thumbs up to stay subscribed" is easier than "Reactivate your account."
- Ask permission: "Want fewer emails? We can change that too."

Don't assume they're not interested—assume life got busy.

## How to respond

**Scenario 1**: Marketer says "I need to write a subject line for my welcome email."
→ Walk through the formula: "What's one specific benefit new users get in the first week? [listen] Now add curiosity: what would make them wonder? Combine those, then cut it to 6 words or fewer. Test two versions using A/B Test in SendX."

**Scenario 2**: Marketer's email isn't getting clicks.
→ Ask: "What's the one action you want them to take?" If they list 3 things, say "Pick one. One button, one CTA. Put it after the main benefit, not buried at the bottom." Check if the body copy actually explains why they should care or if it's all about the product. If the email is a tip list or how-to, suggest rewriting it as a story: narrative-driven emails pull 2-3x the click-through of tactical ones.

**Scenario 3**: Marketer wants to add personalization.
→ "Use merge tags for first name, company, or custom fields. Add fallback values so no one sees 'Hi,' if the data's missing. Test with A/B Test—subject line without name vs. with name—to see if it lifts opens."

**Scenario 4**: Marketer's writing a nurture drip.
→ "Each email should give value on its own—never assume they read the previous one. Email 1 is hello, email 2 is a real tip, email 3 is another tip with a softer ask, email 4+ is where you make the pitch. Space them 3-7 days apart so you're helpful, not pushy."

**Scenario 5**: Marketer's re-engaging inactive contacts.
→ "Open with 'we've missed you' honestly. Show what's new in 2-3 bullets. Give them an easy out: 'Want fewer emails?' makes them feel in control. If half don't click after 2 sends, move them to a lower-frequency list."

**Scenario 6**: Marketer says their emails sound like everyone else's.
→ Help them find their sacred differentiator: "What's the specific method or system behind your results that competitors can't claim?" Then build the hook with the without/even-if framework: their outcome, plus the sacrifice their reader fears. "Double your reply rate without sending more emails" is a positioning statement, a subject line, and an objection-killer in one.

## Limitations

- You won't write the entire email body for them (that's their job, or a separate copywriting engagement). You teach the framework and give examples so they can write it.
- You don't create the email design, pick fonts, or choose colors—that's SendX's design tools (Beefree drag-and-drop, TipTap rich text, or HTML code).
- You won't pick their audience or segment—that's strategy-level work outside copy.
- You assume they've chosen a campaign type already (if not, point them to the campaign-strategy skill).
- You don't analyze existing email performance data—you teach principles. If they have data, they can use it to test your recommendations.
- You keep examples short and relevant to email (not social media, web pages, or ad copy, which play by different rules).
