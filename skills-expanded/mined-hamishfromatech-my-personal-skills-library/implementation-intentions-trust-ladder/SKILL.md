# Progressive Trust Activation

## Overview

Developers do not trust tools they have not earned the right to trust.

This skill combines **Implementation Intentions** (Gollwitzer's if-then planning) with a **Progressive Trust Ladder** to create an onboarding and engagement framework that moves users from first contact to deep advocacy one small, irrevocable commitment at a time. Instead of asking for conversion in a single leap, the framework asks for—and earns—permission to escalate at each rung.

The result is higher activation rates, lower churn, and a community that feels ownership over their relationship with A-Tech products.

## Core Principles

1. **Permission is Earned in Stages** — Every ask must be preceded by disproportionate value delivery.
2. **If-Then Plans Close the Intention-Action Gap** — Vague goals fail. Specific situational plans succeed.
3. **Trust Compounds** — Each kept promise creates a "trust dividend" that makes the next ask easier.
4. **Cognitive Dissonance as a Positive Force** — Once a user has committed to a small action, they rationalize their growing investment.
5. **Scars, Not Wounds** — The story of trust-building is told from the perspective of earned credibility, not desperate pleading.

## The Psychology

### Implementation Intentions (Gollwitzer & Sheeran)

Implementation intentions are simple if-then plans that link a situational cue to a goal-directed behavior:

> "If [situation X], then I will [behavior Y]."

Meta-analyses show these plans increase goal achievement by **2-3x** compared to goal intentions alone. They work by:
- Automating the initiation of behavior (reducing reliance on willpower)
- Increasing cue detection (the brain scans for the trigger)
- Bypassing deliberation (the decision is pre-made)

### The Trust Ladder (Permission Marketing × Developer Psychology)

Seth Godin's permission marketing model maps poorly to developer culture because developers are trained to distrust vendors. The Progressive Trust Ladder adapts permission marketing for a skeptical, technical audience:

| Rung | Permission Level | User Action | Brand Response | A-Tech Example |
|------|-----------------|-------------|----------------|----------------|
| 1 | Attention | User discovers content | Deliver genuine insight without ask | Blog post on local AI pitfalls |
| 2 | Curiosity | User clicks deeper | Provide proof, not promises | Interactive demo of A-Coder |
| 3 | Experimentation | User downloads/installs | Remove all friction; celebrate first win | One-command local install |
| 4 | Effort | User customizes/configures | Recognize and amplify their work | "Your custom stack" dashboard |
| 5 | Identity | User joins community | Welcome them into a belief system | Builder's Club onboarding oath |
| 6 | Advocacy | User refers/shares | Amplify their story | Member spotlight, contributor badge |
| 7 | Investment | User purchases/contributes | Deliver exponential value | Playbook Edition, enterprise license |

### The Compounding Trust Formula

```
Trust at Rung N = (Value Delivered at N-1) × (Promises Kept) × (Time Invested by User)
```

Churn happens when any factor drops to zero. Growth happens when all three compound.

## Application to A-Tech Projects

### A-Coder (The IDE)

**Strategy:** Turn installation into a series of earned victories, each sealed with an if-then plan.

- **Rung 1 (Attention):** Content answers a specific pain point: "Why your cloud IDE is training on your proprietary code."
- **Rung 2 (Curiosity):** The install page doesn't sell features. It sells the first victory: "Run your first local model in under 60 seconds."
- **Rung 3 (Experimentation):** The installer asks for one micro-commitment: "If you see the green checkmark, then you know your code never leaves your machine."
- **Rung 4 (Effort):** During setup, the IDE asks the user to choose their stack. This is the IKEA Effect in action (see existing skill), but framed as an if-then plan: "If you select Python + Ollama, then we'll preload the exact extensions you'll need."
- **Rung 5 (Identity):** After first successful build, the IDE prompts: "You're now running local AI. Welcome to the ownership movement. Join 2,847 builders who refuse to rent their tools."
- **Rung 6 (Advocacy):** One-click share of setup config triggers social proof loop.
- **Rung 7 (Investment):** Upsell to premium features only after the user has experienced undeniable value. The ask is framed as: "If you want team-level privacy controls, then the Pro tier is the next step."

**If-Then Templates for A-Coder:**
- "If I commit code today, then I'll review the AI suggestion before merging."
- "If the model loads slowly, then I'll check my local GPU allocation first."
- "If I find a bug, then I'll open an issue with the template we provide."

**Privacy-Aligned Trust:** The trust ladder is built on verifiable actions, not data collection. Each promise A-Tech keeps is visible in the open-source code. The user doesn't have to trust a privacy policy—they can inspect the implementation.

### Be Practical (The Book & Playbooks)

**Strategy:** Use implementation intentions to bridge the knowing-doing gap. Most readers don't fail because they lack knowledge. They fail because they lack specific plans.

- **Chapter Design:** Every chapter ends with an if-then worksheet: "If [common obstacle], then I will [specific action from this chapter]."
- **Playbook Structure:** The $199 Playbook Edition includes editable if-then templates. The reader doesn't just learn theory; they build a personalized implementation plan.
- **Email Sequences:** Post-purchase emails don't nag. They ask: "Have you completed your if-then plan from Chapter 3? If not, what's the obstacle?"
- **Scars Not Wounds Integration:** The book shares the exact if-then plans that failed, and the revised plans that worked. Example: "If I can't find clients, then I'll reach out to 10 people on LinkedIn" failed because it was too vague. The scar: "If it's Tuesday at 9am, then I'll send one personalized message to a former colleague about their AI challenges."

**The Trust Ladder for Readers:**
- Free chapter → If it solves a real problem, buy the book.
- Book → If the frameworks work, buy the playbooks.
- Playbooks → If revenue increases, join the Builder's Club.
- Builder's Club → If the community delivers value, advocate publicly.

### Open Source AI Builder's Club

**Strategy:** The Club is the trust ladder's final destination—and its engine. Members who reach Rung 5+ become the proof that the ladder works.

- **Onboarding:** New members complete a "First Commitment" ritual: they write their public if-then plan for building with local AI. This is posted in the community, creating social commitment and accountability.
- **Weekly Rituals:** "If it's Monday morning, then I'll check the community for one new deployment tip."
- **Member Milestones:** Each trust rung is celebrated with visible recognition. The progression from "Observer" → "Experimenter" → "Builder" → "Owner" → "Ambassador" mirrors the trust ladder.
- **Revenue Tie-In:** The Club's premium tier is positioned as the natural next step after a member has proven the free tools work. No hard sell needed—their own results do the selling.

## Key Tactics

### 1. The Micro-Commitment Cascade

Never ask for the full commitment upfront. Break every conversion into the smallest possible ask that still requires action:

| Stage | Wrong Ask | Right Ask |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| First contact | "Buy our IDE" | "See how local AI works" |
| Post-demo | "Subscribe to Pro" | "Try one feature on your own project" |
| Post-trial | "Pay annually" | "Set up your first if-then plan" |
| Post-purchase | "Refer 5 friends" | "Share one win in the community" |

### 2. The If-Then Onboarding Sequence

Replace passive onboarding with active planning:

- **Day 0 (Install):** "If you finish setup today, then you'll run your first local model before dinner."
- **Day 1 (First Use):** "If you write 10 lines of code, then you'll see how the AI suggests completions for your exact style."
- **Day 3 (Customization):** "If you change your theme and model, then the IDE will feel like yours, not ours."
- **Day 7 (Community):** "If you share one question in the Discord, then you'll meet builders solving the same problem."
- **Day 14 (Value Realization):** "If you've used A-Coder for two weeks, then you've already saved [X] hours versus cloud tools."
- **Day 30 (Advocacy):** "If someone asks about your IDE, then you know enough to explain why you chose A-Coder."

### 3. The Trust Receipt

After every value exchange, give the user a "trust receipt"—a visible confirmation that you kept your promise:
- "We promised local-only processing. Your traffic log shows zero external API calls."
- "We promised no data collection. Here's exactly what we know about you: [nothing]."
- "We promised your config would work. Your setup passed all verification checks."

### 4. Cognitive Dissonance Leverage (Ethical)

When a user has invested effort, they must justify it to themselves. Provide the narrative they need:
- "You spent 20 minutes customizing your A-Coder setup. That's not wasted time—that's the moment this IDE became yours."
- "You joined a community and shared your first question. That's not random activity—that's the first step in building your authority as an AI Owner."

### 5. The Anticipatory Commitment

Before a user takes action, have them articulate their own if-then plan. This increases follow-through by 40-60%:
- Pre-install: "When will you run your first local model? Write it down: If it's [day/time], then I'll [action]."
- Pre-purchase: "What's the one thing you'll build with the Playbooks in your first 30 days?"
- Pre-event: "What's one question you'll ask at the community AMA?"

## Ethical Boundaries

- **Never manipulate without value:** Every ask must be preceded by disproportionate giving.
- **Never create false urgency:** Scarcity is acceptable for limited physical goods; it is manipulative for digital access.
- **Never trap users:** Progress up the trust ladder must be reversible. A user should be able to downgrade, export data, and leave without punishment.
- **Never exploit sunk cost:** Just because a user has invested effort doesn't mean you should pressure them into bad decisions. Help them evaluate objectively.
- **Always default to transparency:** The open-source nature of A-Tech products means trust is verifiable. Lean into this. Never hide behind opaque processes.
