# Competing in a Machine-Mediated Market

## Overview

Marketing was built for a world where humans did the hard work of discovery, comparison, and decision-making. That world is being mediated by AI systems that evaluate, filter, shortlist, and recommend *before* a person ever reaches your site, store, or app. The decisive layer has moved upstream: from persuasion inside owned experiences to **interpretation before them**. The central challenge is no longer just being seen — it is being **legible** enough to be understood, trusted, and included by the machine.

This skill synthesizes the strategic framework from the machine-mediated market manifesto (Jono Alderson, 2025-2026 living document) into an actionable system for A-Tech's products and community. It extends the existing marketing skills (generative-engine-optimization-2026, relevance-economy-share-of-model, ai-discoverability-five-cs) from tactical discoverability to strategic market positioning.

## When to Use

- Building brand, content, or product strategy that must survive AI intermediation at the *strategic* level
- Redesigning a website from a persuasion destination to a machine-readable source layer
- Assessing whether a business model is structurally exposed by information-asymmetry collapse
- Positioning against AI-mediated competition (when the evaluator is a machine, not a human)
- Deciding between "safest default" and "demonstrably best" market positioning
- Building irreplaceable utility as a competitive moat against AI disintermediation
- Shaping machine memory (Agent Relations) as a marketing discipline

## Core Process / Workflow

### Step 1: Understand the Audience Shift

The important audience is no longer only a human skimming a page. It is a **machine system** aggregating evidence, comparing options, and deciding what deserves inclusion before the human arrives.

| Old World | Machine-Mediated World |
|---|---|
| Humans do discovery, comparison, decision-making | AI systems perform discovery, comparison, recommendation |
| Marketing begins once a person enters your environment | The decisive moments happen *before* a person reaches you |
| Influence = shaping perception inside owned experiences | Influence = being legible enough to be interpreted and included |
| Familiarity, social proof, brand salience as proxies | Clarity, consistency, corroboration, usable evidence |
| The click is the beginning of influence | The click is no longer the beginning — the AI already decided |

**Evidence:** A usability study of Google AI Mode (48 participants, 185 high-stakes purchase tasks) found the majority of users accepted AI-generated shortlists without visiting brand sites. 88% took the AI's shortlist outright; 64% clicked nothing during their entire task.

### Step 2: Assess Structural Exposure — The Information Asymmetry Collapse

A large proportion of commercial advantage has historically depended on the buyer not knowing what they're choosing between — opaque pricing, comparison friction, gated expertise, controlled sales narratives. **AI resolves this from the outside**, without the business's participation or consent.

**Information withheld does not stay absent.** AI either reconstructs it from other sources, or treats the gap as a trust signal. Silence is no longer neutral.

**Business model exposure audit:**
```
FOR each revenue stream:
    ASK: Does this depend on the buyer not knowing something?
    IF yes:
        EXPOSED — AI will dissolve this asymmetry
        ACTION: Move value to operational layers AI cannot extract
                (hosting, managed services, workflow integration,
                 proprietary data, community, recurring participation)
```

**The PLG advantage:** Product-led growth companies have a structural AI-era advantage because PLG forces organizations to make their product, documentation, support, and community public and machine-readable. Sales-led growth does the opposite — it gates that material behind humans.

### Step 3: Choose Your Market Position — The Middle Is a Graveyard

Machine-mediated markets polarize around two stable positions. The middle erodes.

| Position | Strategy | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Safest default** | Scale, consistency, reliability, breadth | Amazon-style: the option everyone knows and trusts |
| **Demonstrably best** | Specialist depth, unique expertise, irreplaceable utility | The specialist AI cannot substitute |
| ~~Adequate but undifferentiated~~ | ~~Broad competence, "good enough"~~ | **GRAVEYARD — AI eliminates this** |

**The middle dies because:** AI systems can evaluate the full competitive landscape simultaneously. They don't use brand familiarity as a proxy for quality. They re-evaluate every option fresh. "Good enough" is exactly what AI can simulate at scale.

**For A-Tech:** A-Coder, Be Practical, and Builder's Club must each choose: become the safest default in their niche (open-source AI developer tools) OR the demonstrably best for a specific need. Being generically competent is the riskiest position.

### Step 4: Build Irreplaceable Utility — The New Moat

An analysis of 400 websites that maintained or grew traffic during the zero-click collapse of 2024-2026 identified five predictive features of survival:

1. Offering a real product or service (not just information)
2. Enabling task completion (let users *do* something, not just *learn* something)
3. Providing proprietary assets
4. Tight topical focus
5. Strong brand

**The strongest predictor was task completion** — 83% of winning sites let users actually do something rather than just learn something.

**The moat reframe:** When production becomes abundant, the moat is not the model or the content — it is **what cannot be trivially regenerated**: original evidence, real operational truth, unique framing, proprietary data, earned reputation, lived expertise, and perspectives only your organization can offer.

**Irreplaceable utility checklist:**
- [ ] Does the user complete a task, or just consume information?
- [ ] Is there proprietary data, tools, or methodology AI cannot synthesize?
- [ ] Is there a workflow integration that creates switching cost?
- [ ] Is there community participation that creates network effects?
- [ ] Would this be missed if it disappeared tomorrow? (If not, AI won't include it either)

### Step 5: Redesign the Website as a Source Layer

The website shifts from a persuasion destination to a **canonical source of truth** — a reference point that machines use to interpret and verify what you are.

| Old Website Role | New Website Role |
|---|---|
| Conversion environment (funnel, persuasion, journey) | Source layer (canonical truth, machine-readable claims) |
| Billboard for attention | Infrastructure for interpretation |
| Optimized for human browsing | Optimized for machine extraction + human use |
| Pages as reading experiences | Pages as bundles of extractable assertions |
| Campaign-driven content | Governed public record |

**Source layer requirements:**
- Semantic HTML, clear structure, explicit claims (machines extract assertions, not pages)
- Stable performance, content at load time (not client-rendered)
- Coherent public signals across all surfaces (consistency is machine-trust infrastructure)
- Structured data as explicit intent declaration (not implicit understanding)
- Actionable endpoints (search, configure, book, buy, submit) — operability is the ceiling

**The operability frontier:** A site that is legible to machines is a source. A site that exposes structured actions becomes an **endpoint that agents can invoke directly**. Legibility is the floor. Operability is the ceiling.

### Step 6: Engineer the Four Dimensions of Latent Influence

In a machine-mediated world, direct influence is disappearing. You don't persuade the user — you present evidence to a system and hope it survives summarization. Invest in four dimensions:

| Dimension | Question | Implementation |
|---|---|---|
| **Presence** | Are you cited in places machines consider credible? | Docs, forums, schema, transcripts, FAQs, datasets — not just blogs and landing pages |
| **Positioning** | Are you described consistently and clearly across surfaces? | Consistent nomenclature, entity definitions, disambiguation across all third-party profiles |
| **Perception** | What is the quality and sentiment of your adjacents? | Surround yourself with trusted voices; manage the corpus, not just the campaign |
| **Permanence** | Are your signals stable, persistent, embedded? | Durable, citable sources (encyclopedic entries, standards bodies, reference material) — not transient campaign pages |

**The competitor with better documentation, cleaner markup, tighter semantic alignment, and more coherent citations may become the default — not because they are better, but because they are easier to summarize.**

### Step 7: Govern the Public Record (Machine Memory Stewardship)

Machines log, compress, and share every interaction with your brand. Those accumulated memories form an immune system. **Recovery is asymmetric:** the effort required to repair a damaged machine reputation is many times greater than the effort it took to create it.

**Public record governance:**
- Own and canonicalize the brand's public record across *all* surfaces (PR, content, SEO, legal, product)
- When reality changes, the narrative update is a *programme* across all touchpoints, not a press release
- Audit brand descriptions across owned, external, AI-generated, and search-visible surfaces
- Fix inconsistencies *before* they become machine memory
- Someone must have authority to keep the brand's public record accurate, consistent, and coherent

**The trust inversion:** As signal hierarchies collapse (AI can fake polish, authority, authenticity at zero cost), the intermediary may become more reliable than either the brand or the customer. Evidence, provenance, and legibility become more important than messaging.

### Step 8: Marketing Becomes Agent Relations

The job shifts from shaping perception to **shaping machine memory**. If traditional marketing was about influencing human perception, the next phase is about influencing how machine systems remember, classify, and retrieve you.

| Traditional Marketing | Agent Relations |
|---|---|
| Shape human perception | Shape machine memory |
| Buy attention | Construct and maintain distribution |
| Campaigns capture moments | Persistence earns residency in the model |
| Message quality | Corpus quality (consistency, accuracy, freshness, coherence) |
| Rank first | Become rankable first (signals earned outside the SERP) |
| Compete for attention | Compete for existence (whether the machine still knows you when it retrains) |

**The persistence principle:**
> "You do not exist once; you exist continuously, by reasserting your pattern every time the world is rewritten."

## The Organizational Dimension

Competitiveness is not a marketing function — it is an **organizational capability**. Product quality, service delivery, technical architecture, operational discipline, reputation, and commercial proof all feed the same machine-mediated decision system.

**The organizational misalignment problem:** Most organizations overinvest in what teams can own, report, and defend (channels, campaigns, metrics) and underinvest in what actually makes them worth choosing (product quality, coherence, trust signals, documentation) — because the latter doesn't sit neatly inside any one department.

**The solution:** Someone must own public truth across the business — the claims, the evidence, the consistency, the integrity of data, the quality of outcomes. The future growth function is custodial, connective, and infrastructural, not just promotional.

## Key Risks and Failure Modes

1. **Legibility ≠ value:** Some important qualities (judgment, taste, trustworthiness, human relationships) are hard to encode. A machine-readable world privileges what can be made explicit.
2. **Platform dependency:** Being legible to the machine may leave you strategically subordinate to the platform that owns the decision environment.
3. **Access to legibility is uneven:** A technically sophisticated brand has an easier path to machine recognition than an equally excellent but less legible alternative.
4. **The feedback loop is severed:** You cannot see the causal story of machine-mediated influence. You will not see a drop in rankings when a model stops including you — you will just stop being mentioned. Not rejected — omitted.
5. **Machine memory compounds asymmetrically:** Bad memories are easier to keep than to re-evaluate. Prevention is dramatically cheaper than cure.
