# The Contingent Workforce datapoint: 35% — and the AI Side-Hustle Response

## The two numbers to hold together

**Number one — the labor market:** 35% of the American workforce — **more than 55 million people** — now falls into some form of contingent or marginal work: freelancers, contractors, gig workers, and employees hired with a tacit understanding they won't last (Osterman, *Disposable Workers*, 2026, original survey of 6,000+ US adults). This isn't an attitude shift; it's a structural description of the present.

**Number two — the response:** Timothy Armoo (built and sold Fanbytes for eight figures before 30; built his first business at 14) calls the current moment "the greatest era of wealth creation ever" and puts £5M of his own money into the Legon Fund, backing AI-powered startups by minority entrepreneurs — removing the capital excuse. His operating claim: **"It is scarily easy… This is the greatest technology ever in history, and you're not obsessing over it."**

Read together they tell one story: **the traditional job is no longer a safe default, and the window for building something of your own has never been wider.** The real risk isn't starting a business — it's not starting one.

## The supporting data

- **35% contingent/marginal** — >55M people (Osterman survey, 2026).
- **Bankrate:** 39% of Americans had a side hustle as of 2025, up from 27% three years prior — tracking exactly the mass-tech-layoff and AI-restructuring period.
- **McKinsey:** ~35% of small businesses meaningfully adopted AI; generative AI alone could add $2.6–4.4 trillion in annual value — the bulk accessible to individual operators, not just enterprises.
- **Glassdoor:** burnout mentions in reviews +43% YoY (May 2026) — the pressure toward alternatives is intensifying.
- **Entry-level hiring slowed sharply** as companies use AI to cover the same output with fewer full-time employees.
- **BLS pattern:** self-employment in technical and professional services has grown faster than any other employment category over the last two years.

The crucial grounding: Gen Z isn't opting out of careers because they prefer freedom — **the jobs they wanted aren't being offered**. That's a labor-market problem, not a generational attitude problem; the rational response is the same either way.

## Armoo's framework, taken literally

1. **Pick a small AI project, not a business plan.** A working tool, a niche newsletter, a productized service — shippable in a week with the AI tools already in your browser. The point is testing distribution before investing serious time.
2. **Use social media as free advertising.** "You just need to spend time on the platform and just keep posting." Organic reach on short-form video remains the most efficient zero-cost distribution.
3. **Think revenue per hour, not salary.** A lean service business around existing skills can generate $50–150/hour — often more than the equivalent salary after taxes and overhead.
4. **Capital exists if the idea works.** The Legon Fund (AI startups, minority founders) — the last excuse (no capital) is being removed for qualifying founders.
5. **Get ahead of the 35%.** The research is a description of right now; building an independent income stream is the exit from contingent work, not a backup plan.

## The honest counterweights

- **Base rates don't flatter the mean.** For every ContractLens-style success there are many stalled side projects; the 39% side-hustle figure includes marginal earners, not just profitable operators. The 35% contingency figure is a *risk* statement, not an opportunity guarantee.
- **"Easiest time in history" is founder rhetoric.** The underlying claim (collapsed startup costs, free distribution) is well-evidenced; the implication (success is likely) is not.
- **Selection effects.** Armoo's Legon Fund targets undercapitalized founders — the real barrier is distribution and product sense, which capital doesn't buy.
- **The comfort risk.** Contingency can also mean precarity without upside; the framework assumes the builder has marketable skills and time, which the gig segment often lacks.

## Use in content and advice

- **The pairing frame:** never cite the 35% without the build response, and never cite the build enthusiasm without the contingency risk — the two numbers *are* the story.
- **For career content:** reframe from "will AI take my job" to "is my employment class already contingent?" — the survey makes the second question empirically answerable.
- **For solopreneur advice:** anchor to revenue-per-hour and ship-in-a-week; avoid income-replacement promises.
- **For the channel's financial-freedom beat:** this is the macro layer under the solopreneur playbooks — the labor market data that makes the practical playbooks urgent rather than optional.

## Honest caveats (aggregated)

- Osterman's figure is a survey estimate with a broad "contingent or marginal" definition — treat 35% as an upper-bound framing, not a precise headcount; classification boundaries (gig vs freelance vs temp) vary across studies.
- Armoo's claims are founder commentary in a business-press interview; the £5M fund is real but its outcomes are unwritten.
- US-centric data; NZ/other-market translation requires local labor statistics.
- Bankrate/McKinsey/Glassdoor figures are directional indicators from different methodologies — cite as convergent signals, not one dataset.
