# Minds Versus Codes: The Aversion-Inversion Pattern

## Overview
Shitova, Kabalska, Varga & Wagner (Psychology & Marketing, 2026, DOI 10.1002/mar.70235) ran a laboratory study combining EEG, eye tracking and qualitative reflection on AI-generated versus human-made video ads. Framed by dual-process theory and algorithm-aversion theory, the study pinpoints **where human-made stories retain relevance — and where aversion dissolves.**

Central findings:
- **AI-driven content matched and even outperformed human-led ads on emotional response** — surprise, fascination, empathy, rather than the disbelief or purely rational scrutiny the "unnaturalness" hypothesis predicted
- **Pre-test skeptics flipped after exposure:** consumers who doubted AI's capacity for emotional authenticity at pre-test changed their attitudes after watching AI-generated content
- The inversion is *experience-mediated*: attitudes toward AI creative are not fixed priors but updated by contact with the stimulus itself

## When to Use
- Forecasting audience response to AI-generated creative in video, audio, or visual campaigns
- Sequencing ad tests: when to run pre-tests vs when exposure itself is the intervention
- Challenging "consumers will reject AI content" assumptions in creative-strategy decks or content-channel positioning
- Designing research protocols: this study's triangulation design (physiological + qualitative) is a template for mixed-method creative testing

## Core Process / Workflow

### The mechanism
1. **Aversion is a pre-exposure attitude, not a stable disposition.** Doubts about AI's emotional authenticity live at the *belief* level; the stimulus operates at the *experience* level — and experience wins when the output is emotionally competent.
2. **Emotional competence outranks provenance.** The brain responds to the emotion in the artifact (dual-process System 1), while provenance scrutiny (System 2) is disengaged when the content is competent — this is why "unnaturalness" never triggered.
3. **Human stories retain the narrow moat:** the human-lead advantage survives where *narrative authenticity* and *intent attribution* matter (the study points to where human-created stories have continuing relevance), not where sensory polish is the differentiator.

### The practical playbook
- **Test AI creative on skeptics deliberately.** Pre-test aversion scores are the wrong targeting signal; run exposure-first designs where the strongest doubters see the output before the label.
- **Disclose after engagement, not before, where regulation permits.** The pattern implies pre-exposure "AI-made" labels activate aversion that exposure would have dissolved — check jurisdictional disclosure rules first.
- **Expect attitude updates, not persistence.** Model repeat-exposure effects in media plans: first-exposure skepticism decays; second-exposure novelty effects may also decay (fascination is not permanent).
- **For creator channels:** AI-assisted creative can lift emotional engagement benchmarks; the human moat is intent, narrative and accountability — compete there.
