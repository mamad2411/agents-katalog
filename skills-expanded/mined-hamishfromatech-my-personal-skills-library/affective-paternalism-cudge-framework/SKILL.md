# Affective Paternalism and the Cudge Framework

## Overview

Affective Paternalism (AP) is a behavioral public policy framework that positions affect (good-bad feelings) as a primary policy resource rather than a bias to be corrected. While cognitive nudges modify the structure of a choice environment (defaults, ordering, information), the "cudge" (cue + nudge) modifies the affective architecture: what options feel like, how emotionally compelling they are, and what felt consequences they promise. The CARE taxonomy provides a systematic framework for designing affective interventions across four modes: Create, Attenuate, Reinforce, and Eliminate.

Source: Västfjäll, Asutay & Tinghög (Linköping University, Frontiers in Behavioral Economics, June 2026).

## When to Use

- Designing interventions for scope-insensitive domains where cognitive approaches have failed (charitable giving, climate action, preventive health)
- Building affect-informed choice architecture beyond standard cognitive nudging
- Creating "cudges" that redesign the emotional texture of choices
- Applying the CARE taxonomy for systematic affective intervention design
- When affective equivalence between low-impact and high-impact options enables welfare-preserving redirects
- NOT for: domains where the primary barrier is cognitive/informational rather than affective

## Core Framework

### The Affective Gap in Behavioral Public Policy

Standard behavioral economics treats affect as noise to be corrected. AP treats affect as a mechanism to be engaged. The domains where behavioral public policy is most urgently needed—climate change, charitable giving, long-term health investment—are precisely those where the primary failure is not cognitive but affective. People endorse high-impact options in the abstract but feel emotionally drawn toward low-impact alternatives.

### Scope Insensitivity and Impact Neglect

- Scope insensitivity: emotional/behavioral responses do not scale with magnitude of problems (2,000 vs 200,000 birds at risk produces same willingness to pay)
- Impact neglect: choices systematically fail to track actual welfare impact of available options
- Cognitive approaches to impact neglect have largely failed (adding statistical information to identifiable victim appeals REDUCES giving by attenuating the affective response)
- Affective equivalence: felt reward is approximately equal for low-impact and high-impact options → redirecting emotional engagement costs the individual nothing

### The Cudge vs The Nudge

| Dimension | Nudge | Cudge |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Mechanism | Modifies cognitive architecture (defaults, ordering) | Modifies affective architecture (emotional texture) |
| Target | Cognitive shortcuts | Emotional meaning and motivational intensity |
| Signal | Incidental affect (ambient, normatively unrelated) | Integral affect (genuinely about the decision) |
| Durability | Less durable (exploits cognitive shortcuts) | More durable (genuine motivational engagement) |
| Example | Default enrollment in green energy | Vivid mental imagery of local climate consequences |

### The CARE Taxonomy

**Create**: Build new integral affective associations calibrated to actual consequences. For options that are affectively neutral despite adequate information. Example: mental imagery of local climate consequences generates anticipatory concern.

**Attenuate**: Reduce affective intensity that is disproportionate to actual properties. For signals that are miscalibrated in either direction. Example: pairing high-threat health information with credible efficacy message prevents fear-driven avoidance.

**Reinforce**: Amplify and channel existing positive affective engagement toward higher-impact alternatives. For behaviors that already carry some positive affect but insufficiently strong or misdirected. Example: donation bundling adds "competence glow" alongside "warm glow" → 76% increase in effective giving without reducing total donations.

**Eliminate**: Disrupt specific affective responses that are demonstrably miscalibrated. For responses whose residual functional value is minimal. Example: graphic tobacco warning labels shift affective valence of smoking at point of consumption.

## The Integral vs Incidental Distinction

The critical design principle: integral affect (feelings genuinely responsive to the actual properties of the decision) outperforms incidental affect (feelings normatively unrelated to the decision).

Evidence from BIT trials:
- Organ donation (N=1,085,322): reciprocity message (integral) increased registrations 38%; adding smiling-people photograph (incidental) REDUCED effectiveness
- Charitable giving: photographs of real colleague donors (integral) doubled enrollment; generic stock photos (incidental) did not
- Court fines: text with recipient's name + specific amount (integral) increased payment 189%; DVLA: photograph of recipient's own vehicle (integral) increased payment 6x vs 4x without

## Design Process

### Step 1: Diagnose the Affective Problem
- Is the high-impact option affectively neutral? → Create
- Is affective intensity disproportionate? → Attenuate
- Is positive affect present but misdirected? → Reinforce
- Is a specific affective response demonstrably miscalibrated? → Eliminate

### Step 2: Ensure Affective Equivalence
- Verify that felt reward is approximately equal between low-impact and high-impact options
- If equivalence holds, interventions can be welfare-preserving at individual level
- This is the ethical core: large societal gains at negligible affective cost

### Step 3: Design Integral Affective Cues
- The affective content must be genuinely about the decision and its consequences
- Avoid incidental positive affect (generic imagery, ambient mood)
- Use narratives, imagery, reframing that connects to actual consequences

### Step 4: Verify Asymmetric Paternalism
- Large benefits for those whose decisions are affectively miscalibrated
- Little or no cost for those whose felt responses are already well-aligned
- This satisfies the Camerer et al. (2003) asymmetric paternalism criterion

## Ethical Considerations

- Integral affect interventions work WITH the individual's actual values; incidental manipulation works AGAINST them
- The temporal paradox: should policy target anticipated affect (uptake), experienced affect (reality), or remembered affect (repetition)? All three are poorly correlated
- Transparency, proportionality, and alignment with considered values remain necessary
- Eliminate mode is most ethically demanding: target the specific maladaptive expression, not the underlying motivational system

## Key Evidence

- Caviola & Greene (2023) donation bundling: 76% increase in effective giving, $1.5M+ raised in 14 months, no reduction in total donations or satisfaction
- Karlsson et al. (2024): vivid mental imagery of local climate consequences increases high-impact behavior intentions
- Evans et al. (2015): graphic warning labels elicit affective responses that drive behavioral effect beyond text-only warnings
- Sallis et al. (2018, N=1,085,322): reciprocity message 38% increase in organ donor registrations

## A-Tech Alignment

- **Open-source**: framework is theoretical and freely applicable; uses standard behavioral science methods
- **Data privacy**: affective interventions can use behavioral proxies without physiological measurement
- **Financial freedom**: welfare-superior interventions achieve large gains at negligible individual cost
- **Practical implementation**: clear four-mode taxonomy with diagnostic decision rules and ethical boundary conditions
