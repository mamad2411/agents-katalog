# Neuromarketing Stimuli and Impulsivity: What Carried, What Faded

## The model

The study integrates the Stimulus–Organism–Response framework with dual-process theory: six neuromarketing determinants (stimuli) → perceived neuromarketing efficacy (organism) → consumer impulsivity (response), with consumer traits (emotional susceptibility, FOMO, social-influence sensitivity) as direct predictor and moderator. N=609 valid responses (97% effective rate) from digitally active Delhi–NCR consumers screened for exposure to neuromarketing-driven content; common-method-bias controlled procedurally and statistically; measurement quality solid (alphas 0.756–0.952; HTMT <0.90). Model explains ~70% of variance in both impulsivity (R²=0.708) and neuromarketing efficacy (R²=0.697).

## What carried (and how hard)

| Determinant | β on efficacy | Read |
|---|---|---|
| **Emotional appeals** | **0.469*** | The dominant lever — affectively resonant storytelling (identity/aspirational themes; short-form video) reduces cognitive resistance and primes System 1 evaluation |
| **Cognitive processing cues** | **0.378*** | The headline nuance — structured, credibility-enhancing information (certifications, comparison charts, concise value bullets) *strengthens* perceived efficacy; persuasion is not emotion-only |
| **Sensory triggers** | **0.173*** | Multisensory congruence (color, motion, sound, scent) contributes meaningfully but is an order of magnitude weaker than emotion |
| **Neuro-pricing** | **0.134*** | Charm pricing, anchoring, reference framing work — with the transparency caveat |
| **Scarcity & urgency** | 0.043 (p=.095) | **Failed.** Time-bound promotions and "only a few left" cues showed no significant efficacy |
| **Endorsement influence** | 0.045 (p=.127) | **Failed.** Reviews, influencer, and peer-proof cues showed no significant efficacy |

**The transmission and moderation:** neuromarketing efficacy → impulsivity β=0.562***; consumer traits direct β=0.416***; traits × efficacy interaction β=0.075*** — the efficacy-impulsivity link **strengthens at higher dispositional susceptibility**. Impulsive response is mechanism-driven *and* trait-contingent, not uniform.

## Why scarcity and endorsements faded (the saturation hypothesis)

Prior literature highlights both as impulse triggers; this sample says diminished returns in **digitally saturated environments**. Repetitive exposure to time-bound promotions and influencer endorsements may attenuate persuasive impact through reduced perceived authenticity. Two design cautions from the study's own instrumentation: the social-norm message used was relatively weak (60% vs the 80% benchmarks common in the field), and boomerang effects can push the already-converted the wrong way — but the pattern (two classic levers nulling at N=609) is itself the finding. **The strongest levers in a saturated attention economy are emotional resonance plus cognitive credibility — not urgency theater or borrowed authority.**

## The dual-process implication (the most transferable insight)

The significant cognitive-processing path reframes impulsivity: impulsive behavior emerges from an **interaction between intuitive activation and cognitively reinforced persuasion**. Structured information doesn't cool the impulse — it strengthens evaluative confidence, which strengthens the impulse. Practically: pair emotional narrative with credible, scannable substantiation; the combination beats either alone. And the trait moderation mandates **trait-informed segmentation** (novelty-seekers → launch alerts; emotionally susceptible → narrative campaigns; socially influenced → peer proof) — one-size-fits-all persuasion leaves measured effect on the table.

## Design rules

1. **Lead with emotional narrative; substantiate with structure** — β=0.469 + β=0.378 are the two paths that carried; design both into every high-stakes asset.
2. **Deprioritize countdown timers and stock-count widgets** in saturated channels — the evidence no longer supports them as primary levers.
3. **Treat endorsements as table stakes, not a lever** — at null effect, spend the budget on message substance.
4. **Segment by dispositional susceptibility** — emotional susceptibility/FOMO/social sensitivity moderate everything; measure the trait, then choose the lever.
5. **Keep neuro-pricing transparent** — psychologically-informed pricing works, but overuse tips into brand-damaging manipulation (the paper's own ethical caveat).
6. **Expect context dependence** — one region, one product segment, cross-sectional design; the direction of effects is the portable part, not the coefficients.

## Honest caveats

- Survey-based perception measures, not neurophysiological data — this is how consumers *respond to neuromarketing-style stimuli*, not neural activation (the authors say so explicitly).
- Delhi–NCR lifestyle-product sample; generalization across cultures/categories is limited; cross-sectional design limits causal claims.
- Scarcity/endorsement nulls may partly reflect weak operationalizations (norm message at 60%; convenience sample) — treat as "no evidence of effect in this context," not "proven null."
- f² sizes are modest for the weaker paths (sensory 0.098, pricing 0.059) — meaningful but small.
