## Neurodesign Principles

### Contrast & Rhythm
Keep brain engagement steady without overload. Alternate between high-stimulus and low-stimulus sections. Use visual contrast to reset attention. Avoid uniform pacing.

### Emotion Anchors
Tie visuals, sounds, and narratives to consistent emotional triggers. Pick 2–3 core emotions for your brand and repeat them across all touchpoints.

### Neural Fluency
Simplify layouts to reduce cognitive friction without eliminating productive friction. Use familiar patterns so the brain spends energy on meaning, not decoding.

### Reward Timing
Time emotional payoff for dopamine release that reinforces memory encoding. Place the emotional peak at the 60–70% mark of any experience (Peak-End Rule alignment).

## Memory Encoding Techniques

### The Hippocampus Hook
Memory encoding is strongest when information is tied to:
- **Novelty:** Unexpected pattern breaks (the Awe Gap)
- **Emotion:** Any strong feeling (positive or negative)
- **Repetition:** Spaced re-exposure at increasing intervals
- **Context:** A specific environment or state

### Mirror Neuron Simulation
Ads built with neural empathy (showing shared triumph, relief, or awe) activate audience mirror neurons, deepening identification and emotional retention.

## Ethical Neuromarketing Rules
- **Explicit consent:** No neural or biometric tracking without permission
- **Transparency:** Label "neuro-tested" or "emotion-optimized" content clearly
- **Fair use:** Prohibit targeting based on mental health conditions
- **Human oversight:** Keep neuroscientists in AI decision loops
- **No emotional manipulation:** Only amplify emotions already present in the user journey
- **Opt-out available:** Any adaptive experience must have a static, non-adaptive mode
