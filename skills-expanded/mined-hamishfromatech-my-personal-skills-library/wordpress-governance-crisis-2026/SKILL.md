# WordPress Governance Crisis: The Community Cost of a Two-Year Slide

## Overview
The WP Open Community Collective's State of the Community 2026 (published Aug 18, 2026) is the first independent, anonymous survey measuring sentiment across the people who work with, build on, contribute to, or care about WordPress — and it quantifies the community cost of the 2024 governance crisis: **open-source sentiment (81.7% positive) remains strong while WordPress sentiment (32.7%) has collapsed, with 95.5% of those whose feelings changed saying they feel worse.**

## The 11 findings, condensed to the sharpest five

| # | Finding | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **81.7% positive about open source; 32.7% positive about WordPress** | The gap isolates *governance*, not the open-source model, as the problem |
| 2 | **77.3% feel differently about WordPress than two years ago; 95.5% of them feel worse** | The slide is recent, large, and nearly unidirectional |
| 4 | **Governance and leadership is the problem the community reports most** | Self-reported cause — not license disputes, not technical debt |
| 5 | **Participation is contracting, and the people stepping back have 10+ years tenure** | The loss is veterans, not transient newcomers |
| 6 | **Half the people doing community work don't consider themselves contributors** | The contributor base is larger — and less legible — than commit counts show |

The full 11 also include: belonging matters more than money to almost everyone (including the paid); the community uses AI daily but does not want AI in WordPress; business owners are the only group expecting to win on AI-and-money; a third of event attendees have stopped attending and pulled back from everything else; and 90% still agree on the importance of security, web standards, open-source values, and the software itself.

## The methodological bar that makes it usable

- **Fielded May 27–July 17, 2026** — 431 respondents, 231 questions.
- **First independent, anonymous** sentiment survey for the WordPress project — not affiliated with the WordPress Foundation or the project.
- **Every number is checkable.** `findings-figures.csv` maps every cited figure to its source file and lookup keys; the full significance register (499 statistically significant subgroup differences across 1,374 tests) is published; per-question tallies and derived variables are released; 280 verbatim free-written responses (including criticism of the survey itself) are published.
- **Aggregate data is CC BY 4.0** (commercial reuse fine, attribution required); report text CC BY-NC 4.0.
- **The WPOCC's stated aim is repeatability** — annual fielding with a consistent instrument so future reports show change, not just snapshot.

This is the transparency standard the library has been advocating for open-source governance data: publish the instrument, publish the data, publish the limitations, publish even the responses that criticize you.

## The deeper pattern: governance is the load-bearing wall

The survey's sharpest structural insight is Finding 11's own framing: **"A community that had genuinely given up would not still be at 90% agreement on what is most important for the project."** The community has not left — it is *dissenting with data*. The values layer (security, web standards, open-source values, the software) holds 90% agreement while the governance layer bleeds trust. That dissociation is the diagnostic signature of a governance problem, not a product problem — the same dissociation the library tracked in the 2024 WP Engine dispute, now measured at sentiment scale.

## How to use this in a video or brief

1. **Open on the gap** — 81.7% vs 32.7%. "The community still loves open source. They've lost faith in the governance of its biggest project."
2. **Anchor the slide** — 77.3% changed, 95.5% of those worse, top-reported problem = governance.
3. **Show the veteran contraction** (Finding 5) — this isn't churn of newcomers; it's the long-tenure core pulling back.
4. **Use the contributor-legibility finding** (half of community workers don't self-ID as contributors) to argue for broader contribution accounting beyond commit counts.
5. **Close on the method** — publish instrument + data + limitations; it's the reproducibility standard for any ecosystem health survey.

## A-Tech alignment
- **Open source:** governance health is an open-source infrastructure problem; this survey is the community-side counterpart to the maintainer-funding evidence base.
- **Privacy:** the fully-anonymous, data-published methodology is a model for privacy-respecting community research.
- **Financial freedom:** Finding 7 (belonging beats money, including for the paid) warns against assuming stipends alone fix retention — governance and belonging are co-equal levers.
- **Practical:** the 11 findings are a ready-made diagnostic checklist for any ecosystem audit.

## Honest caveats
- 431 self-selected respondents from a community under acute strain; it measures *sentiment of the engaged*, not the full WordPress user base (the WPOCC publishes its own limitations section).
- "Feeling worse" is self-reported and retrospective ("than two years ago") — recall effects apply.
- The survey is WordPress-specific; generalizing the governance-crisis dynamics to other ecosystems is a hypothesis, not a finding.
- The WPOCC is independent of the WordPress Foundation but is itself a member-based organization with a stake in community health.
