# AI-Managed ETFs: The Alpha Question Meets Its Three-Year Test

## Overview
Two Sept 16, 2026 data points define the moment: **Ai Funds launched HIAI** — an AI-managed ETF using a proprietary model to select and risk-manage 40-60 US stocks against the S&P 500, with founder Tal Schwartz arguing AI-managed funds could become "the third option in the active-versus-passive binary" — while **Morningstar's scorecard** of the existing category shows the evidence is still open: of 7 currently trading AI ETFs, 5 beat the S&P 500 over the past year but **only 2 over the past three years**; of 21 funds ever launched in the category, **8 have folded**; **all seven** currently show outflows year-to-date, one-year, and three-year. Analyst skepticism is structural: HIAI's 0.87% expense ratio is high vs the 0.74% 2025 active-ETF average; FUSE's Loren Fox frames the bar as "a three-year track record that big broker-dealers look at faithfully"; Bloomberg's Athanasios Psarofagis poses the "final boss" problem — the best AI might conclude it can't beat the index and recommend buying the S&P 500. This is the wealth-side companion to the AI-alpha question: the category exists, the mechanism is real, the proof is pending.

## The Evidence Base
- **HIAI launch (Ai Funds; ETF Upside, Daily Upside — Sept 16, 2026):** proprietary AI model selects 40-60 US stocks, adjusts risk on market signals; 0.87% expense ratio (analysts: unusually high); benchmark chosen *because* it is difficult; Schwartz expects "years and possibly decades" before AI-managed funds become a third pillar because the industry is conservative.
- **Morningstar category scorecard (via the same print):** 7 trading AI ETFs; 5/7 beat S&P 500 past year; 2/7 past three years; all 7 in outflows YTD, 1-year, 3-year; 8 of 21 launches folded.
- **Analyst positions:** Fox (FUSE) — the broker-dealer track-record gate is three years; Psarofagis (Bloomberg Intelligence) — uncertainty whether the model finds what the market misses; the "final boss" endpoint is index-recommendation.
- **Adjacent same-day:** Anthropic launched **Claude for Financial Advisors** (custodial connectors: Addepar, BlackRock, Schwab Advisor Services, Envestnet, iCapital, Orion, SS&C Black Diamond, Vanguard, Wealthbox, Wealth.com, Zocks; workflow skills for meeting prep, portfolio analysis, compliance checks; advisors must still review and approve regulated outputs) and **Dynasty Financial Partners** announced Dynasty AI (Claude-embedded "AI operating system for wealth management," Book-Level AI Chat early release). Schwab is distributing to its 16,000+ RIAs. The advisor-workflow category is moving to production while the alpha category waits on its track record — a useful separation.

## Core Findings (the pattern)
1. **The category is real but the verdict is deferred.** AI-managed funds exist as a distinct third option; their legitimacy question resolves at the three-year mark, not the launch.
2. **Survivorship is the first data point.** 8 of 21 folded (38% mortality) — the category's *base rate* matters more than any single fund's benchmark claim.
3. **Expense ratios face an unforgiving bar.** HIAI's 0.87% vs the 0.74% active average vs ~0.03% passive — the alpha must clear the fee drag before any claim is credible; this is the same fee-drag math the library's advisor-cost skills apply to human advisors.
4. **The "final boss" endpoint is a category-level insight.** If the best AI portfolio model converges on recommending the index, that is not a failure of AI — it is a statement about market efficiency, and a content hook for any AI-vs-alpha discussion.
5. **Alpha and workflow are separating.** AI-as-portfolio-manager (pending proof) vs AI-as-advisor-copilot (shipping now, connectors live, human review mandated) — the wealth industry's AI adoption is bifurcating into evidence-gated and workflow-gated tracks.

## Core Process / Workflow
1. **Screen claims against the base rate.** Any AI-alpha product should be read against: 38% category mortality, 2/7 three-year outperformance, universal outflows.
2. **Apply the fee-drag gate.** Compute the expense-ratio hurdle the alpha must clear; HIAI's 0.87% vs 0.74% active average is the reference bar.
3. **Separate alpha from workflow.** Classify the product: is it evidence-gated (portfolio management) or workflow-gated (advisor copilots, connectors)? Different risk profiles, different evidence standards.
4. **Set the verification timeline.** The category's verdict date is the three-year track record; HIAI's verdict lands ~2029.
5. **Watch the "final boss" convergence.** If top AI models converge on index-recommendation, treat it as the strongest possible evidence for passive strategies — a counterintuitive but powerful content angle.
