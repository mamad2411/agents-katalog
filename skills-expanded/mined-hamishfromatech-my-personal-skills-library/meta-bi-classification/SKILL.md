# META BI: Behavioral Intervention Classification

## Overview

META BI (Mapping of Environment, Target group and Agent for Behavioural Interventions) is a validated, transdisciplinary classification system for nudges and behavioral interventions. Developed through a Delphi process with 44 international experts across psychology, economics, and policy, it describes interventions across **20 dimensions** spanning five system-level elements and identifies **17 distinct psychological mechanisms**.

Published in *Behavioural Public Policy* (Cambridge University Press, September 2025).

## When to Use

- Designing a new nudge and needing to consider all relevant dimensions
- Comparing two or more behavioral interventions systematically
- Conducting field mapping or evidence synthesis for meta-analysis
- Communicating about nudges across disciplinary boundaries (psychology vs economics vs policy)
- Selecting interventions most likely to succeed in a specific context
- Avoiding "buzzword-driven" behavioral interventions by thinking comprehensively

## The Five System-Level Elements

META BI organizes every behavioral intervention across five elements, each characterized by 3-5 dimensions:

### 1. Intervention (4 dimensions)
The behavioral tool or strategy itself.

- **Mechanisms**: Immediate changes in mental states the intervention seeks to trigger. 17 non-exclusive mechanisms grouped into 5 categories (see below).
- **Format**: The physical appearance of the manipulated component (the nudge's form).
- **Intrusiveness**: The extent to which the intervention interferes with people's lives and goals, and whether targets can avoid/exit it.
- **Personalization**: Whether different members of the target group receive different versions based on their characteristics.

### 2. Agent (4 dimensions)
The organization, institution, or individual launching the intervention.

- **Objectives**: The agent's goal — improving decision quality, targeting internalities (consequences for self), externalities (consequences for others), or social/group characteristics (e.g., groupthink, polarization).
- **Legitimacy**: Whether the agent is reasonably allowed to deploy the nudge and promote the targeted behavior.
- **Reputation**: The subjective perception of the agent by the target group (trust, authenticity, attractiveness).
- **Sameness**: The relationship between agent and target group — identical (self-nudging), equal (peer influence), or different (government influencing citizens).

### 3. Target Group (4 dimensions)
The individuals whose behavior the agent aims to influence.

- **Preferences**: Evaluations of the target behavior and nudge objective — can be aligned/unaligned, homogeneous/heterogeneous, or time-inconsistent.
- **Engagement**: The target group's involvement — awareness of the intervention's purpose/source/mechanism and active role in development/delivery.
- **Autonomy**: Three facets — freedom of choice (constraints), outcome autonomy (alignment with interests), and process autonomy (fair, well-reasoned decision-making).
- **Ability**: The extent to which the target group has the means to process and act upon the nudge as planned.

### 4. Behavior (4 dimensions)
The behaviors targeted by the nudge.

- **Temporality**: One-off vs. frequently repeated behaviors; whether there's a specific influence period.
- **Nature of Change**: Avoid, reduce, maintain, intensify existing behaviors, or instigate novel ones.
- **Mental Mode**: The mental processes bringing about desired change (multiple theoretical orientations possible).
- **Discreteness**: How tightly the target behavior is linked to other behaviors through identity, motivation, or context.

### 5. Environment (4 dimensions)
The broader structural and systemic context.

- **Opportunities & Affordances**: Whether the environment supports/invites or blocks/limits the intended behavior change.
- **Resources**: Means the agent uses beyond the nudge — information, authority, financial sanctions/incentives, services/goods, symbolic signals.
- **Interplay**: Relationship between nudges and structural/systemic levers — neutral, supportive (reinforcing), or countervailing (preventing).
- *(4th dimension varies by context)*

## The 17 Psychological Mechanisms

Grouped into 5 categories (non-exclusive — an intervention can activate multiple):

1. **Attention & Memory**: Salience enhancement, memory cueing, information accessibility
2. **Social Influence**: Social norms, endorsement, peer effects, authority
3. **Judgment & Decision**: Framing, anchoring, reference points, simplification
4. **Self-Regulation**: Commitment, reminders, self-monitoring, default setting
5. **Affect & Motivation**: Emotional arousal, incentive salience, loss aversion activation

## Classification Process

1. **Identify the intervention**: What is the nudge? (Format dimension)
2. **Map the agent**: Who is launching it and why? (Objectives, Legitimacy, Reputation, Sameness)
3. **Characterize the target group**: Who is affected? (Preferences, Engagement, Autonomy, Ability)
4. **Specify the behavior**: What should change? (Temporality, Nature, Mental Mode, Discreteness)
5. **Assess the environment**: What context surrounds it? (Opportunities, Resources, Interplay)
6. **Identify mechanisms**: Which of the 17 mechanisms does it activate?
7. **Evaluate intrusiveness and personalization**: How invasive is it? Is it tailored?

## Practical Applications

### For Designing Interventions
Use the 20 dimensions as a mental organizing device. Answer the classification questions for each dimension to avoid common pitfalls like ignoring context, negative spillovers, or unintended backfiring effects.

### For Comparing Interventions
Code interventions across dimensions to enable apples-to-apples comparison. Filter by mechanisms, agent objectives, and target group characteristics to find comparable interventions.

### For Evidence Synthesis
META BI captures key factors influencing external validity (mechanisms, intervention nature, target group, behavioral outcomes). Use it to explain heterogeneity in meta-analysis findings.

## Comparison to Other Frameworks

| Framework | Focus | Dimensions | Mechanisms | System Lens |
|-----------|-------|-----------|------------|-------------|
| META BI | Comprehensive classification | 20 | 17 (detailed) | Yes (5 elements) |
| COM-B | Diagnosis → intervention design | 3 (Capability, Opportunity, Motivation) | Via Behavior Change Wheel | Partial |
| EAST | Accessible design principles | 4 (Easy, Attractive, Social, Timely) | Implicit | No |
| MINDSPACE | Messenger, Incentive, Norms, Defaults, Salience, Priming, Affect, Commitment, Ego | 9 | Implicit | No |
| Münscher et al. | Choice architecture taxonomy | 3 clusters (Info, Structure, Assistance) | Implicit | No |

META BI integrates these into a unified, modular framework that connects diagnosis, design, and scalability.

## Limitations

- Developed with WEIRD (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic) expert panel
- Dimensions are not fully independent — shifts in one may moderate others
- Requires operationalization before coding (defining observable criteria for subjective dimensions)
- Abstract by design; may need adaptation for specific disciplines

## Key Distinction from Prior Frameworks

META BI introduces several novel dimensions not in previous classifications:
- **Objectives**: Adds decision-making quality and social/group characteristics beyond internalities/externalities
- **Resources**: Distinguishes interventions steering free choice from those increasing adherence with existing regulations
- **Interplay**: Acknowledges that traditional instruments (bans, sanctions) can be re-analyzed from a behavioral perspective and combined with nudges
