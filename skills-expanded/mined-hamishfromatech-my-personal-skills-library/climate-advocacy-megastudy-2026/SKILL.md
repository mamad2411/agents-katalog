# Climate Advocacy Megastudy 2026: The 17-Intervention Test

## Overview
Goldwert, Constantino, Patel et al. (PNAS Nexus 5(1), pgaf400, 2026; advance 27 Jan 2026; NYU/Stanford/Princeton/Cambridge/Yale/LSE et al.) — a behavioral megastudy testing **17 expert-crowdsourced theory-informed interventions** on a quota-matched US sample of **n=31,324** across public-awareness, political, and financial advocacy.

## When to Use
- Selecting which advocacy framing to deploy when the goal is collective (not individual) action
- Allocating message budget across audiences (Democrat vs Republican identifiers)
- Designing megastudy-style intervention tournaments for any collective-action goal (open-source contribution mobilization, community advocacy, donation campaigns)
- NOT for converting climate skeptics' beliefs — the study measures advocacy behavior, not belief change
- NOT for individual lifestyle-change framing as the primary outcome (a different intervention won there)

## Core Process / Workflow
1. **Crowdsource interventions** via open call to experts (the study received 60 submissions → 31 unique → 17 selected by a blinded expert advisory board ranked on theoretical merit + practical effectiveness).
2. **Normalize all outcomes** to a 0–1 scale to compare intervention effects across heterogeneous behavior categories.
3. **Run mixed-effects models** per advocacy domain with participant random intercepts; apply Benjamini–Hochberg correction and James–Stein shrinkage to correct the winner's curse.
4. **Measure actual behaviors, not just intentions** — the design's strength: it included real letter-writing to representatives and real donation allocation.
5. **Test political-affiliation moderation** — the effects differ sharply by party.

## Key Evidence
- **Winner: "collective efficacy + emotional benefits"** — emphasized successful collective campaigns (Sunrise Movement → IRA) and the emotional benefits of collective action; increased public advocacy by **up to 10 percentage points** (d=0.30) and political advocacy by 6pp (d=0.16). Mechanism: efficacy beliefs + positive emotion, additive — neither alone elicited the effect.
- **Runner-up: "binding moral foundations"** (purity/sanctity framing) — most effective for financial advocacy (6pp, d=0.21) and the top intervention for **real donation behavior**, and the only intervention effective among **Republican identifiers** (7pp financial advocacy among Republicans, d=0.23).
- **System-justification framing** ("patriotic conservation") was the top strategy for **actual letter-writing** (OR 1.27) — advancing system-sanctioned change for policy influence.
- **Nulls worth noting**: "co-benefits" (economic growth + health framing) and "guilt-based collective responsibility" produced NO effect on any facet of climate action — calling into question two of the most popular message strategies.
- Most effective interventions **explicitly foregrounded collective action** (real-world organizing, collective efficacy, joining broader efforts) rather than individual action.
- Sequencing matters: engagement monotonically declined for outcomes appearing later in the randomized sequence — place high-effort actions (donations, letters) EARLY in any campaign.
- Democrat-vs-Republican asymmetry: among Democrats, many interventions increased advocacy (activating preexisting values); among Republicans, most interventions had limited impact except the binding-moral framing.

## A-Tech Alignment
- **Open source**: data + interactive web tool (goto.stanford.edu/climate-advocacy) public; intervention texts reusable.
- **Data privacy**: no behavioral surveillance; survey-based with preregistration.
- **Financial freedom**: directly informs donation/divestment campaign design (the financial-advocacy layer is where "binding moral foundations" wins — relevant to financial-education content mobilization).
- **Practical**: the 17-intervention table is a ready-made menu for community mobilization content; the null interventions ("co-benefits", "guilt-based collective responsibility") are equally actionable — do NOT use them.
