# Neuromarketing Research Landscape — Cycle-20 Refresh

*(Core skill established in cycle 16: the landscape map, the PRISMA meta-analysis calibration bar, and the small-N-single skip rule.)*

## Cycle-20 addendum (2026-09-06): three synthesis-grade additions recalibrate the map

The September wave delivered an unusually dense cluster of synthesis-grade work, each extending the landscape into a new corner:

1. **The fMRI pillar consolidated** (Alsharif et al., Current Psychology 2026 — `fmri-neuromarketing-systematic-review`): 70 studies, 2008–2024, PRISMA — decision-making 52.85% / cognition 24.28% / emotion 22.85%; the canonical BOLD mechanisms (NAcc preference, insula price, mPFC net value); neuroforecasting evidence; and the standing caveats (reverse inference, small samples, ethics) — the modality pillar the EEG and meta-analytic layers complemented but didn't cover.
2. **The S–O–R structural test with trait moderators** (Nagpal et al., Frontiers, N=609 — `consumer-neurophysiological-moderators-2026`): emotion (β=0.469) and cognitive-credibility cues (β=0.378) carry; **scarcity/urgency and endorsement influence null** in saturated digital contexts — the first survey-grade evidence that two classic persuasion levers have faded, with trait moderation confirmed (β=0.075 interaction).
3. **The firm-integration framework** (Topcugil & Hiziroglu, Future Business Journal — `neuromarketing-ai-cxm-firm-framework`): neuro data as a distinct AI input class, in-situ affective inference, four touchpoint actions, three firm vignettes — the management-layer map connecting measurement to deployment.

**Landscape implication:** the field now has effect-size discipline (cycle 16 meta-analysis), a mechanistic canon (fMRI review), a structural stimulus taxonomy with moderators (S–O–R), and a deployment framework (CXM). The remaining gap is longitudinal ecological validity — the recurring limitation across all four. For calibration: cite the layer that matches the claim (effect sizes → meta-analysis; mechanisms → fMRI review; stimulus design → S–O–R; deployment → CXM framework), never a small-N single.
