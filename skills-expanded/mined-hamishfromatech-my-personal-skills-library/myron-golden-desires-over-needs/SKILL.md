# Myron Golden — Desires Over Needs

## Overview
Myron Golden's framework on the fundamental distinction between operating from the **arena of needs** (scarcity, competition, bondage) versus the **arena of desires** (infinite abundance, collaboration, freedom). The core thesis: the arena you operate in determines your success or failure far more than how hard you work, how long you work, or how much education you have.

**Source:** YouTube video — "Desires Over Needs" (52 min)
**URL:** https://m.youtube.com/watch?v=_mShhgSNcYo

---

## Core Framework: Two Arenas

### The Arena of Needs (The Plantation)
A matrix-like arena where most people operate, characterized by five guarantees:

1. **Competition** — Intense competition for scarce resources
2. **Judgment** — Constant evaluation by others
3. **Justification** — Need to explain/defend your actions
4. **Political Positioning** — Jockeying for position based on what others want
5. **Bondage** — Enslavement to the system

> "The arena of needs is a very, very, very small arena with billions of players. All competing for seemingly the same limited resources."

### The Arena of Desires (The Promised Land)
An infinite arena with infinite resources for an infinite number of people, but only a limited number ever choose to play there.

> "There's plenty of room at the top. It's the bottom that's crowded." — Jim Rohn

## The Four Trades (When You Move from Needs to Desires)

| From (Needs Arena) | To (Desires Arena) |
|---|---|
| **I can'ts** | **I cans** |
| **Competition** | **Collaboration** |
| **Constraints** | **Compounding** |
| **Compulsion** | **Creativity** |

### 1. Can'ts → Cans
Focus on limitation perpetuates limitation. Shift from "I can't because I don't have time" to "I can because I get to decide what to do with my time."

### 2. Competition → Collaboration
In the needs arena, every person is a threat. In the desires arena, every person is a potential collaborative partner. Operating from abundance means you don't need to take from others.

### 3. Constraints → Compounding
In the land of needs, everything is a constraint (limited time, energy, money, relationships). In the land of desires, everything compounds — leverage replaces labor.

### 4. Compulsion → Creativity
Shift from "I have to" (compulsion) to "I get to" (creativity). From corroboration (justifying yourself) to calling (purpose-driven action).

## The Etymology of "Desire"

Myron breaks down the word **desire**:
- **De** (Latin root) = of or from
- **Sire** = Father

**Desire = Of the Father / From the Father**

This connects to Psalm 37:4 — "Delight thyself also in the Lord, and He will give you the desires of your heart."

The insight: God gives you **what** to desire first, then gives you **what you desire** because you desire what He desires you to desire.

### Why "Desire" vs. "Want"
- **Want** is a double entendre — it means both *desire* and *lack*
- The subconscious mind doesn't have access to a dictionary
- **Desire** has a singular definition — pure aspiration without lack

> "The Lord is my shepherd, I shall not want" — means "I shall not lack," not "I shall not desire the Lord."

## The Three-Step Belief Activation

To activate desire using the catalyst of **belief**:

### Step 1: Intend to Believe
Set an intention — an objective you see in the future. "From now on, I'm going to believe this."

### Step 2: Decide to Believe
**Decide** comes from Latin *de* (of/from) + *caedere* (to cut). When you decide, you cut yourself off from any other possibility. A decision is a covenant with yourself — "I am going to do this or die in the doing of it."

> "Choosing means pick. Deciding means cut yourself off from any other possibility."

### Step 3: Discipline Yourself to Only Believe
Discipline = doing what you're supposed to do, the way you're supposed to do it, when you're supposed to do it, every time. Actively overcome the temptation to doubt.

> "Faith is created in the ears. Doubt is created in the eyes."

## The Tug of War: Desire vs. Expectation

**D** = Desire
**E** = Expectation

**E always wins the tug of war.** But you can recruit E to pull in the same direction as D.

Your perpetual job: recruit your expectation to align with your desire. When desire and expectation pull together, you win.

## The Upside/Downside Decision Principle

| Scenario | Upside | Downside | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bungee jumping | Few minutes of thrill | Death/injury | **Hard no** |
| Writing a book | 10M readers, changed lives | 100 readers, 2 lives changed | **Easy yes** |

> "When the upside is limited and the downside is unlimited, it's always a hard no. When the upside has unlimited potential and the downside is limited, it's an easy yes."

## Key Distinctions

### Needs vs. Desires
- **Needs arena**: Scarcity mindset, zero-sum thinking, "the economy is a pie"
- **Desires arena**: Abundance mindset, infinite expansion, "the universe is ever-expanding"

### Covetousness vs. Desire
- **Covetousness**: Desire to have without willingness to do the right thing
- **Desire**: Willingness to do the right thing to achieve what you want

### Analogy vs. First Principles
- **Analogy**: Copying others stuck in the same arena
- **First Principles**: Letting the desired outcome tell you what actions to take in the present

## The Peter Walking on Water Metaphor

> "When Peter saw the wind... he was afraid and began to sink."

The wind represents circumstances. The problem isn't the circumstances — it's **seeing** them (focusing on them). Faith comes through the ears (hearing the calling), doubt through the eyes (seeing the storm).

> "Maybe your real problem is you've been spending too much time looking at the wind."

## Practical Application

1. **Identify your arena** — Are you operating from needs or desires right now?
2. **Ask yourself** — Do I like the results I'm getting in this arena?
3. **Ignore your needs** — Stop thinking about what you lack
4. **Focus on your desires** — Pick things you desire to have, do, and be
5. **Intend, decide, discipline** — Activate belief as the catalyst
6. **Recruit your expectation** — Align what you expect with what you desire
