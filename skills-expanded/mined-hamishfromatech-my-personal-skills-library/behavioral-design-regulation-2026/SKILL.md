# Behavioral Design Regulation — Cycle-20 Refresh

*(Core skill established in prior cycles: the regulatory pattern-matching rules for behavioral design deployments.)*

## Cycle-20 addendum (2026-09-06): two datapoints sharpen the transparency duties

1. **California's social-media warning law takes effect Jan 1, 2027** (Grummon et al., Stanford Medicine, JAMA Health Forum, Sept 4, 2026): four states now mandate warnings, language specified in California's statute — and the first rigorous wording test shows the legally-burdened California wording ("may have benefits for some young users… has not been proven safe") performed **on par with shorter alternatives** on perceived effectiveness among 1,012 teens/young adults. Regulation-relevant: statutory wording drafted for litigation survived effectiveness testing — compliance and persuasion are compatible; the design lesson for regulated warnings is *name the specific harm* (mental-health, depression, anxiety messages outranked generic and addiction framings).
2. **Autonomy-restoration duties can't be discharged by disclosure** — the standing Cuypers et al. finding (n=1,916: nudge worked, all disclosure types left effectiveness unchanged, and *no disclosure offset the perceived-autonomy loss*) now has a design-level complement: Lipman et al.'s doubly randomized trial (Behavioural Public Policy, Mar 27, 2026; N=839) shows **letting users choose their intervention** produced significant healthy-choice gains while randomly assigned versions did not. For regulation drafting and compliance design: **choice architecture that offers user selection is the evidence-backed instrument for autonomy duties** — disclosure labels are not.

Combined rule for any behavioral-design compliance review: (a) if a warning duty applies, test the specific statutory wording against the resonant-topic evidence; (b) if an autonomy safeguard is required, a user-choice layer beats a disclosure banner; (c) document both in the consent surface — the two findings are complements, not alternatives.
