# Wayshaping: A Multiscale Framework for Behavior Change

## Overview

Wayshaping is a novel framework for individual behavior and habit change grounded in embodied cognitive science, complexity theory, and design. It reconceptualizes the individual as a **multilevel, multiscale collective intelligence** — not a single rational agent — and reframes the intention-action gap as a set of **interdependent coordination challenges** rather than a simple failure of willpower.

Published as a preprint (April 2025) by James, Jamaluddin, Froese, Belhadi, Panagiotou & Snowden.

## When to Use

- When simple nudging or willpower-based approaches fail to produce lasting change
- When designing behavior change interventions that need to respect individual complexity
- When addressing the intention-action gap in personal, organizational, or health contexts
- When moving beyond individual-level solutions toward systemic realignment
- When coaching individuals through difficult habit or identity changes
- When designing for sustainable, context-sensitive behavior change

## Core Insight: The Self-Collective

The individual is not a single agent but a **nested system of semi-autonomous agents** — habits, micro-identities, meso-identities, macro-identities — each with their own concerns, operating across different timescales and levels of being.

### Four Levels of Being
1. **Biological**: Organismic needs, physiological constraints, genetic predispositions
2. **Psychological**: Subjective experience, habits, attentional biases, core needs
3. **Social**: Interpersonal relationships, cultural norms, shared practices, group identities
4. **Existential**: Meta-cognitive capacity for self-reflection, values, meaning, self-narratives

### Temporal Scales
- **Habits**: Milliseconds to seconds (sensorimotor patterns)
- **Micro-identities**: Minutes to tens of minutes (situation-specific roles)
- **Meso-identities**: Across related situations (e.g., "me at work")
- **Macro-identities**: Enduring patterns (personality-level)

Behavior change requires **multiscale realignment** across these levels and scales — not just modifying a single action.

## The Intention-Action Gap: Three Coordination Challenges

### 1. Challenges of Non-Linearity
The self-collective is a complex adaptive system with emergent, unpredictable dynamics:

- **Non-stationarity**: What worked before may not work now; the system and context keep changing
- **Non-ergodicity**: What worked for others may not work for you; individual trajectories are unique
- **Non-proportionality**: Small inputs can produce large outputs (tipping points); large efforts may yield nothing
- **Non-additivity**: Combined interventions produce qualitatively different outcomes, not just summed effects

**Implication**: Avoid one-size-fits-all approaches. Use adaptive, personalized, locally-sensitive strategies.

### 2. Challenges of Alignment
The nested agents within the self-collective have competing demands:

- **Intra-level misalignments**: Conflicts within the same level (e.g., health habit vs. indulgence habit)
- **Inter-level misalignments**: Tensions across levels:
  - *Indirectness*: Changes at one level influence others through mediated effects
  - *Temporal lag*: Sudden changes take time to propagate across levels
  - *Asynchrony*: Different levels operate on different rhythms/cycles
  - *Asymmetry*: Some levels exert more constraint than others (e.g., social pressure overriding biological needs)

**Implication**: Behavior change is not about dictating a path forward but enabling negotiations that lead to realignments among invested parts.

### 3. Challenges of Anticipation
We must align present actions with future needs in uncertain environments:

- **Stigmergy**: We leave traces in our environments (physical, virtual, bodily) that shape future actions — the environment itself fills the intention-action gap
- **Rhythms and flows**: Biological rhythms (circadian, metabolic) and social rhythms must be aligned with behavioral changes

**Implication**: Design environments to scaffold future actions. The "intention-intention gap" — the discrepancy between our intentions and the distribution of intentions in our environment — matters as much as the intention-action gap.

## Trust as Meta-Regulator

Trust functions as a **meta-regulatory mechanism** that determines whether misalignments can be tolerated during change:

| Feature | High Trust | Low Trust |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Indirectness | Sustained engagement despite unseen processes | Skepticism of unseen processes |
| Time delays | Patience with delayed rewards | Premature abandonment |
| Asynchrony | Coordination across mismatched timescales | Out-of-sync changes feel like failures |
| Asymmetry | Top-down constraints tolerable; bottom-up adaptation possible | Resistance to authority; insensitivity to signals |

**Building trust**: Recognize small meaningful adjustments, understand progress is non-linear, reframe relapses as recalibration phases, acknowledge past successful adaptations.

## Shapers: Scaffolds and Shocks

### Scaffolds (Sustained Stress)
Environmental supports that regulate the flow of stresses within adaptive limits:

**Temporal classification:**
- **Temporary-static**: Table leg helping a child stand (transient, fixed)
- **Temporary-dynamic**: Adult guiding a child's walking (transient, evolving)
- **Permanent-static**: Abacus (enduring, fixed)
- **Permanent-dynamic**: Caregiver (enduring, adapting)

**Interactional dimensions**: Intrapersonal (self-talk), Interpersonal (caregiver), Extrapersonal (environment)
**Reality conditions**: Imaginal, Virtual, Physical

Scaffolds shape change by managing stress — preventing excessive stress while applying targeted stress that promotes cross-level interaction and new habit emergence.

### Shocks (Acute Stress)
Disruptions that push the system beyond its comfort zone, creating openings for novelty:

- **Generative shocks**: Catalyze positive change (like tilling soil for new growth)
- **Endogenous shocks**: Sighs, yawns — the body's own reset mechanisms
- **Cultural examples**: Zen koans, cathartic rituals, psychedelic therapy, cold exposure

Shocks destabilize entrenched habits, create permeability to new patterns, and can serve as temporal resets that synchronize processes across scales.

**Dosage principle**: Higher-intensity shocks used less frequently; lower-intensity shocks can be regular. Combine with scaffolding (e.g., psychedelic therapy pairs shock with heavy "set and setting" scaffolding).

## Practical Strategies

### 1. Wayfinding (Not Navigation)
Adopt the stance of a wayfinder — finding meaning in movement, continuously reading shifting affordances, building the map as you go. Contrast with navigation (following a pre-existing map).

**Cynefin integration**: Discern which elements of your change landscape are:
- **Clear**: Sense → Categorize → Respond (follow best practices)
- **Complicated**: Sense → Analyze → Respond (use expertise)
- **Complex**: Probe → Sense → Respond (run safe-to-fail experiments)

### 2. Estuarine Mapping
Map constraints (norms, frames, structures) on a grid of **energy vs. time to change**:
- Elements requiring minimal time/energy: near origin (easy wins)
- Elements demanding most: top-right (bracket out as unrealistic)
- Highly volatile elements: bottom (may not hold long enough)

This builds trust in a direction of travel by identifying feasible, impactful changes.

### 3. Dynamic Loading
Shape spatiotemporal trajectories under uncertainty:
- Use moments of abundance (time, energy) to shape trajectories that lower costs of desirable activities during scarcity
- Load environments with intentional scaffolds (meaningful artifacts, immersive cues, obstructions in established habits)
- Leverage spatial transitions (doorways, entering workspaces) as reset points
- Use temporal thresholds (new week, new season) as "fresh start" narrative resets

### 4. Negotiation and Playful Exploration
- Clarify regulatory dynamics (habits, identities) impacted by change
- Scaffold to support negotiations within and between scales
- Use playful exploration to find polyfunctional resolutions (actions satisfying multiple needs across levels)
- Deploy shocks strategically to open channels for communication and coordination

## Key Principles

1. **Individual as collective intelligence**: Sustainable change = multiscale realignment, not single-action modification
2. **Intention-action gap = coordination challenges**: Non-linearity + alignment + anticipation, not willpower failure
3. **Trust is meta-regulatory**: Cultivate trust to sustain change through misalignments and delays
4. **Shapers regulate stress flow**: Scaffolds (sustained) and shocks (acute) modulate adaptive pressure
5. **Idionomic approach**: Each person's change trajectory is unique — equip with self-monitoring capacity, not prescribe universal solutions
6. **Participatory design**: All behavior change is social and ethical — coordinate with communities, not in isolation

## Limitations

- Currently a preprint (not yet peer-reviewed)
- Generalization from individual to collective/community change is theoretical
- Empirical validation of key hypotheses (shocks enable realignments, polyfunctionality → habit formation) is ongoing
- Formal mathematical articulation (e.g., via active inference) is future work
