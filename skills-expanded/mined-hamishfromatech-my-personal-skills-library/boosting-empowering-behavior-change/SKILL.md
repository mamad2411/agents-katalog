# Boosting: Empowering Behavior Change

## Core Thesis

The dominant "nudge" approach (Thaler & Sunstein, 2008) operates on a **deficit model** of human cognition: it leverages or works around people's biases and limitations to steer behavior. The **boosting** approach (Hertwig & Grüne-Yanoff, 2017; Hertwig, Michie, West & Reicher, 2025) works on a **competence model**: it builds people's capacities to make informed choices that align with their own goals.

**Nudges tweak behavior at the margins. Boosts build durable competence.**

For A-Tech, this is a paradigm-level shift. Where existing nudging skills (choice architecture, defaults, social proof, friction reduction) steer users toward better one-time decisions, boosting skills build lasting capability — the difference between auto-enrolling someone in a pension (nudge) and teaching them financial literacy so they make good decisions for life (boost).

---

## The Nudge vs. Boost Distinction

| Dimension | Nudge | Boost |
|---|---|---|
| **Underlying model** | Deficit model — humans are biased, need steering | Competence model — humans can be empowered |
| **Mechanism** | Changes choice architecture (environment) | Changes the person (competences, opportunities, motivations) |
| **Duration** | Often one-time or context-dependent | Durable, transferable across contexts |
| **Agency** | May bypass deliberation; user may be unaware | Explicitly builds user agency and self-governance |
| **Ethics** | Libertarian paternalism (steers without forcing) | Empowerment (expands freedom by expanding capability) |
| **Failure mode** | Non-persistent effects, compensatory spillovers, psychological reactance | Requires more time/investment; competences take time to build |
| **Best for** | Habitual, low-attention, System-1 behaviors | High-stakes, deliberate, System-2 behaviors requiring sustained effort |

**Key insight:** Nudging and boosting are not mutually exclusive. The most powerful interventions combine both: reduce friction (nudge) AND build competence (boost). Use nudges to clear the path; use boosts to build the traveler.

## The Boost Taxonomy

Boosts are classified by the kind of competence they build. Six proven boost categories from the research:

### 1. Digital Literacy Boosts
- **Lateral reading** — teach users to assess credibility like professional fact-checkers (open new tabs, search for the source, cross-reference) instead of vertical reading (staying on the page and evaluating design/content)
- **A-Tech application:** A-Coder can teach developers to evaluate AI-generated code by cross-referencing against documentation, security advisories, and test results rather than trusting the output's surface plausibility

### 2. Risk Literacy Boosts
- **Experienced simulations** — let people experience risks over time (e.g., simulating cumulative health risks) instead of just describing them statistically
- **A-Tech application:** Be Practical can use interactive simulations to let learners experience the compound effects of financial decisions over time

### 3. Financial Literacy Boosts
- **Simple heuristics** — teach rules of thumb (e.g., separate business and personal accounts using physical analogues) rather than complex formulas
- **A-Tech application:** Be Practical financial modules teach simple decision heuristics (the 5-question investment screen from Seven Laws of Money) rather than requiring full financial modeling

### 4. Statistical Reasoning Boosts
- **Frequency representations** — train people to convert complex probabilities into natural frequency formats (e.g., "1 in 10" instead of "10% probability")
- **Contrast training** — show correct solutions alongside typical biased responses in single or repeated trials
- **A-Tech application:** A-Coder can present code quality metrics as frequencies ("3 out of 10 AI-generated functions had security flaws") rather than abstract percentages

### 5. Health Literacy Boosts
- **Drug label comprehension** — teach people to understand and act on medical instructions
- **Implementation intentions** — teach "if-then" planning for self-regulation ("If I feel anxious before deploying, then I will run the test suite one more time")
- **A-Tech application:** Be Practical teaches implementation intentions for habit formation (connects to existing habit-driven-design skill)

### 6. Decision-Making Boosts
- **Decision trees** — provide transparent, simple decision aids for domains like finance, medicine, and hiring
- **A-Tech application:** A-Coder can provide decision trees for common architectural choices (monolith vs. microservice, sync vs. async, etc.)

## Self-Nudging: The Bridge

Self-nudging is where boosting and nudging converge. In self-nudging, **the individual serves as both the nudger and the nudgee** — they become their own choice architect. This addresses the main ethical objections to nudging (paternalism, preference heterogeneity) while building agency.

**Example:** The "one sec" app empowers users to substantially reduce their own social media consumption by designing their own friction (introduction delays, breathing exercises before opening apps).

**A-Tech application:** A-Coder can offer a "self-nudge studio" where developers design their own friction — e.g., setting mandatory comprehension checkpoints before accepting AI-generated code, configuring focus-mode defaults, or designing their own notification boundaries. This transforms users from passive subjects of choice architecture into active designers of their own environment.

## The COM-B Model: Competence, Opportunity, Motivation

Boosting involves more than enhancing competences. All behaviors involve an interaction of **C**apability, **O**pportunity, and **M**otivation (Michie et al., 2011). Competences are only useful to the extent that the environment enables them.

- **Competence** is useless if the **Opportunity** doesn't exist (e.g., understanding the importance of ventilating indoor spaces is useless if buildings don't allow windows to open)
- **Motivation** is needed to deploy competence even when opportunity exists
- **A-Tech application:** Teaching developers to review AI-generated code (competence boost) is only effective if A-Coder provides the review interface (opportunity) and the workflow incentives to actually do it (motivation)

## When to Boost vs. Nudge

### Boost when:
- The behavior involves high stakes and deliberate deliberation (System 2)
- The desired behavior requires sustained effort over time, not a one-time choice
- Strong existing habits, values, or identity are involved
- The underlying barrier is an Anxiety (fear, distrust) that friction reduction cannot address
- You want durable, transferable capability
- The user's long-term autonomy and self-governance matter

### Nudge when:
- The behavior is habitual or low-attention (System 1)
- People are essentially indifferent between options
- The desired behavior is easy to perform once the path is cleared
- Social context can be mobilized (clear "what most people do")
- The stakes for any individual choice are relatively low
- You need fast, cost-effective population-level impact

### Combine both when:
- You need to clear the path (nudge) AND build the traveler (boost)
- The behavior is important enough to warrant investment in competence
- The user will face similar decisions repeatedly in different contexts

## The Nudge Limitations That Boosting Addresses

1. **Non-persistent effects** — nudges often produce temporary behavior change that fades when the nudge is removed. Boosts build lasting competence.
2. **Compensatory negative spillovers** — a nudge in one domain may cause people to "let go" in another. Boosts create generalizable skills.
3. **Psychological reactance and backfiring** — when people feel manipulated, they may do the opposite. Boosts explicitly respect and build agency.
4. **The trust problem** — nudging assumes the nudger knows what's best. Boosting empowers the user to decide for themselves.
5. **The collective action problem** — nudging focuses on individuals; boosting can build collective competence and shared identity for group challenges.

## A-Tech Application Matrix

### A-Coder (AI Coding IDE)
- **Competence boost:** Teach developers AI code review skills (lateral reading for code: cross-reference docs, security advisories, tests). Provide decision trees for architectural choices. Present quality metrics as natural frequencies.
- **Self-nudge studio:** Let developers design their own friction — comprehension checkpoints, focus modes, notification boundaries, mandatory review thresholds.
- **Opportunity design:** Ensure the review interface and workflow incentives support the competence being built.
- **Privacy alignment:** Boosting builds user agency — the core of privacy-first design. Users who understand their tools make better privacy decisions.

### Be Practical (Learning Platform)
- **Financial literacy boosts:** Simple heuristics over complex formulas. Interactive simulations for compound effects. The 5-question investment screen as a decision tree.
- **Risk literacy boosts:** Let learners experience the temporal and cumulative nature of decisions through simulations, not just descriptions.
- **Implementation intentions:** "If-then" planning for habit formation. Connects to existing habit-driven-design and rapid-habit-transition-switch skills.
- **Privacy alignment:** Financial literacy is a form of data sovereignty — understanding money is understanding leverage, which is understanding autonomy.

### Builder's Club (Community)
- **Collective competence building:** Boosts can be deployed at group level — teaching communities to assess information collectively, make decisions together, and build shared identity for sustained action.
- **Self-nudging as community practice:** Members share their self-nudge configurations, creating a library of citizen choice architecture patterns.
- **Privacy alignment:** Collective competence in privacy practices (threat modeling, data minimization) is more durable than individual nudges toward privacy-friendly defaults.

## Ethical Guardrails

1. **Transparency** — boosts are inherently transparent (you are teaching someone a skill, not manipulating their environment covertly)
2. **Voluntariness** — boosts should be offered, not imposed. The user must want to build the competence.
3. **Goal alignment** — boosts should build competence aligned with the user's own goals, not the designer's goals
4. **Equity** — ensure boosts are accessible to users with varying baseline competences, not just those who already have high capability
5. **No competencewashing** — don't label a nudge as a boost. If you're changing the environment without building the person's capability, that's a nudge, not a boost. Be honest about which you're doing.

## Measurement Framework

| Metric | What It Measures | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Competence transfer rate | % of users who demonstrate the skill in a new context | >40% |
| Persistence at 30/90 days | % of users still applying the competence after nudge/context removed | >60% at 30d, >40% at 90d |
| Self-efficacy score | User's confidence in their own ability (pre/post) | +1.0 on 5-point scale |
| Reactance rate | % of users who push back or do the opposite | <5% (boosts should have lower reactance than nudges) |
| Decision quality | Measurable improvement in decision outcomes | Context-dependent, baseline +20% |
| Self-nudge adoption | % of users who create and maintain their own choice architecture | >15% |

## Decision Framework: Nudge, Boost, or Both?

```
1. Is the behavior high-stakes or requiring sustained effort?
   YES → Lean toward BOOST (or combine with nudge)
   NO  → Consider NUDGE

2. Does the user need to make similar decisions in different contexts?
   YES → Lean toward BOOST (competence transfers)
   NO  → NUDGE may suffice

3. Is the underlying barrier an Anxiety (fear, distrust) rather than friction?
   YES → BOOST (nudges cannot address fear)
   NO  → NUDGE (reduce friction)

4. Does the user's long-term autonomy matter for this domain?
   YES → BOOST or self-nudge
   NO  → NUDGE is acceptable

5. Do you need fast, population-level impact at low cost?
   YES → NUDGE first, BOOST as follow-up
   NO  → BOOST for durable change

6. Are strong existing habits or identity involved?
   YES → BOOST (nudges are less effective against strong habits)
   NO  → NUDGE may work

7. Can you combine both?
   ALWAYS PREFER COMBINATION when resources allow
   Nudge clears the path; Boost builds the traveler
```

## Key Takeaway

The nudging-to-boosting shift is not about abandoning nudges. It is about recognizing that **steering behavior is insufficient for the challenges that matter most**. For A-Tech's mission of open-source AI, data privacy, and financial freedom, boosting is the paradigm that builds users who can govern their own tools, protect their own data, and build their own wealth — not users who are gently steered toward those outcomes by someone else's choice architecture.

**Boost the user. The nudge is the scaffolding, not the building.**
