# The Relevance Economy: From Share of Voice to Share of Model

## Overview

For three decades marketing ran on one rule: reach equals influence. That rule is breaking. AI summaries now sit between brands and buyers, AI agents make or shape purchasing decisions, and organic click-through rates collapse by ~61% when an AI Overview is present. The new currency is **relevance** — being the right answer, in the right context, for the right intent, whether the decision-maker is a human or a machine. This skill operationalizes the Relevance Economy thesis (Andreas Fuchs, *Future of Marketing*, June 2026) and the "Relevance Wins, Not Ranking" framework (Sequel Consulting, May 2026) into a measurable system centered on **Share of Model**: how often a brand appears in AI-generated answers, with what frequency, position, and sentiment.

## When to Use

- Designing content or brand strategy that must survive AI intermediation (AI Overviews, agent recommendations, conversational search)
- Measuring marketing performance beyond Share of Voice — building a Share of Model dashboard
- Deciding where to invest between earned media, structured data, and third-party credibility vs. paid reach
- Positioning open-source or developer-tooling brands to be cited by AI agents in category queries
- Building a "Business-to-AI" (B2A) marketing capability — treating AI agents as a new customer segment
- Re-architecting product pages, documentation, and developer content for extractability by LLMs

NOT for:
- Pure paid-media campaigns where AI intermediation is not yet material to the funnel
- SEO strategies that ignore AI search and agent discovery entirely
- Brands whose buyers never use AI in their journey (rare in 2026 B2B and developer markets)

## The Structural Shift: Why Attention Is No Longer Enough

Two forces are simultaneously dismantling the Attention Economy:

1. **Humans are getting better at ignoring ads.** 85% of digital ads receive fewer than 2.5 seconds of attention — the minimum for a memorable impression (Amplified Intelligence, Dr. Karen Nelson-Field). Gen Z loses active ad attention after 1.3 seconds. The share of consumers who say social ads capture attention dropped from 43% to 31% in a few years (Kantar Media Reactions, 2024).

2. **AI is stepping between brands and buyers.** When Google shows an AI summary, organic CTR drops **61%** and paid CTR drops **68%** (Seer Interactive, 3,119 queries / 25M impressions, Nov 2025). Only 8% of users click a traditional result when an AI summary is present vs. 15% without one, and 26% end their browsing session after reading the AI answer (Pew Research Center, July 2025, 68,879 Google searches).

**Agent intermediation scales the problem.** 45% of consumers now use AI during buying journeys (IBM IBV + NRF, 2026, 18,000+ consumers across 23 countries). Gartner forecasts that by 2028, **90% of B2B buying will be intermediated by AI agents**, channeling more than $15T in spending through automated exchanges (Gartner, 2025). An AI agent doesn't see your ad, can't be reached with a banner, and doesn't respond to brand campaigns — it selects based on structured information, third-party signals, and trust indicators it can read.

Visa's global CMO Frank Cooper III framed this in April 2026 as the rise of **"Business-to-AI"** — AI agents becoming a new customer segment brands must learn to sell to, inside the decision architectures agents use.

## The New Currency: Relevance and Share of Model

Relevance means being the right answer, in the right context, for the right intent — whether the decision-maker is human or machine. In the Attention Economy you could buy your way in (volume game). In the Relevance Economy you must **earn** your place through four levers:

### The Four Relevance Levers

| Lever | What it means | Why AI rewards it |
|-------|---------------|-------------------|
| **1. Expertise & depth** | Content that actually answers questions with substance and specificity | AI systems reward depth; thin keyword-stuffed content is filtered out |
| **2. Third-party credibility** | Earned media, press, expert mentions, independent reviews | 82–89% of AI citations come from earned/non-brand sources; when paid placements are excluded, non-paid sources rise to ~94% (Muck Rack, 1M+ AI citations, 2025). A brand blog no one references is nearly invisible to AI agents. |
| **3. Consistent brand signals** | Coherent entity definition across Wikipedia, LinkedIn, review platforms, trade press, analyst reports | AI builds "knowledge" of a brand from hundreds of sources; inconsistent or thin signals mean the brand doesn't appear in AI answers |
| **4. Structured, extractable information** | Clear headings, structured data, FAQ formats, statistics, tables, answer-first formatting | Sites with structured/answer-first formatting are cited far more often in AI answers (BrightEdge AI Search Study, 2025) |

### The New Metric: Share of Model

In the Attention Economy, the dominant metric was **Share of Voice** — how much of the total media landscape you occupied. The emerging metric is **Share of Model**: how often your brand appears in AI-generated answers, with what frequency, position, and sentiment.

Early data on the stakes:

- Brands cited in AI Overviews earn **35% more organic clicks** and **91% more paid clicks** on the same queries than brands not cited (Seer Interactive, 2025).
- AI-referred website traffic converts at far higher rates than traditional organic search. One benchmark of 312 tech firms found AI visitors converting at **14.2%** on average vs. **2.8%** for Google organic — roughly a **5x advantage** (Opollo 2026 AI Search Benchmark Report). Magnitude varies widely (under 2x in some e-commerce to over 20x in research-heavy B2B); the direction is consistent across every major study.
- **Vercel** grew ChatGPT from <1% to 10% of all new signups in six months by consistently being recommended in AI answers about its category (CEO Guillermo Rauch, 2025).
- **Tally**, an 8-person SaaS team, made ChatGPT its #1 referral source — 2,000+ new users/week from AI platforms, without increasing ad spend.

### AI Search Growth Context

AI search platform traffic grew **42.8% year over year** in Q1 2026 vs. 2.4% for Google, surpassing 27 billion global visits (SimilarWeb + Wix Studio "AI Search vs Google" report). Search no longer always starts with Google — especially among younger users who begin on social platforms or AI tools to compare products, ask for recommendations, or form initial opinions (Sequel Consulting, 2026). Stanford AI Index Report 2026: global organizational AI adoption has reached 88%; generative AI surpassed 53% population adoption in three years — faster than the diffusion of the internet or personal computers.

## Core Process / Workflow

### Step 1 — Audit Current Share of Model

Run a structured citation audit before changing strategy. For each priority category query your buyers ask:

1. Query ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews.
2. Record: (a) is the brand cited? (b) in what position? (c) with what sentiment? (d) what sources does the AI cite for your brand?
3. Score each query: **Cited & Positive / Cited & Neutral / Cited & Negative / Absent**.
4. Aggregate to a **Share of Model score** = (cited queries with positive or neutral sentiment) / (total priority queries).
5. Identify the **citation source gap**: list the third-party sources the AI relies on that you are absent from.

| Query | ChatGPT | Gemini | Claude | Perplexity | Google AIO | Avg Position | Sentiment | Cited Sources | Action |
|-------|---------|--------|--------|------------|------------|--------------|-----------|---------------|--------|

### Step 2 — Build the Four Relevance Levers

For each gap identified:

1. **Expertise & depth** — Publish answer-first, substantive content for every category question. Replace keyword-driven posts with reports, benchmarks, vertical analyses, and original data. A-Tech application: open-source benchmarks, privacy-by-design case studies, Be Practical learning outcome data.
2. **Third-party credibility** — Earn coverage in the sources AI already cites. Map the citation graph for your category and target the top 20 sources. For A-Tech: developer trade press (The Stack, InfoQ), analyst reports (Gartner, Forrester), independent review platforms (G2, Capterra), open-source community references (Hacker News, dev.to, GitHub Discussions).
3. **Consistent brand signals** — Ensure the entity definition is coherent across Wikipedia, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, review platforms, and trade press. Inconsistency or thinness = invisibility in AI answers. A-Tech: maintain a canonical entity description and keep all profiles synchronized.
4. **Structured, extractable information** — Re-architect product pages and documentation with clear headings, FAQ blocks, schema.org structured data, statistics tables, and answer-first formatting. A-Tech: developer docs with answer-first abstracts, pricing tables in machine-readable format, privacy architecture documented as structured specs.

### Step 3 — Establish a Business-to-AI (B2A) Marketing Capability

Treat AI agents as a customer segment, per Visa's Frank Cooper III framework:

1. Map the decision architectures agents use for your category (which sources, which comparison criteria, which trust signals).
2. Ensure your product information is available in the formats agents consume (structured data, API docs, machine-readable pricing, clear comparison tables).
3. Make sure agents can verify your claims — AI agents preferentially cite sources that are independently verifiable. Open-source transparency is a structural advantage here.
4. Build agent-readable trust signals: verified reviews, public benchmarks, open security disclosures, third-party audits.

### Step 4 — Measure Share of Model Continuously

Build a monthly Share of Model dashboard:

- **Citation rate** by platform and query cluster
- **Position** when cited (first mentioned, second, etc.)
- **Sentiment** when mentioned
- **Source attribution** — which third-party sources the AI relies on
- **Conversion from AI referrals** — track AI-referred traffic separately and compare conversion rate to traditional organic
- **Trend** — month-over-month Share of Model movement

Target benchmarks (directional, treat with caution as datasets skew tech/B2B):
- AI-referred conversion: aim for the 5–20x advantage over Google organic seen in tech benchmarks
- Citation rate: aim for >50% on priority category queries across at least 3 of 5 major AI platforms

## Ethical and Anti-Pattern Guardrails

- **Do not** attempt to manipulate AI citations through fabricated reviews, coordinated synthetic mentions, or paid placements disguised as earned media. AI systems increasingly filter for verifiable, independently-sourced signals, and manipulation damages the third-party credibility lever.
- **Do not** abandon SEO entirely — Google remains central for commercial, local, and transactional searches. The Relevance Economy is additive, not substitutive.
- **Do not** reduce everything to "SEO vs. GEO" framing — the deeper issue is becoming trustworthy in a distributed environment across Google, AI answers, video, newsletters, communities, and social.
- **Do not** optimize only for citation frequency at the expense of accuracy — being cited with wrong or misleading information is worse than being absent.
- Open-source transparency is a structural advantage: claims that are publicly verifiable (open code, open benchmarks, open security disclosures) are inherently more citable and more trustworthy to AI agents.
