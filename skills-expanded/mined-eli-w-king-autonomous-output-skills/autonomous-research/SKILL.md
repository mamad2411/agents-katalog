# Research (AI-First edition)

## Rules

- **Draft, never launch.** Write the study; never create it on a platform, recruit, pay or ask for keys. Tools such
  as Outset or User Interviews are where it would run with an account; here they're a what-if.
- **Real words only.** Quotes are verbatim and attributed (P1, P2), never invented. Fictional practice material
  stays labeled as fictional.
- **Know what it's for.** Unmoderated studies compare options and catch first impressions. They don't explain
  motivation and don't replace talking to people; say so in the draft and the readout.

## Draft a study

Write `study-draft.md` where the person says (in a practice repo, next to its `workshop/` docs):

1. The decision it informs, in one sentence, and what result would change it.
2. The stimulus: the live link or screenshots of the real thing, never a mockup of a working app.
3. Participants: about six, who they are, and a screener of at most five questions with one made-up tool in the
   tool list and an attention check.
4. Tasks and questions: open (What, How, Walk me through), neutral, one idea each, past behavior over guesses,
   what they expected versus what happened, and a why after every rating.
5. Success criteria and length (15 to 20 minutes).

Then read it as a participant would and fix anything leading or double-barreled.

## Synthesize results

From the files the person points to (results, transcripts, a feedback doc):

1. Read all of it and note each participant's key moments.
2. Rank the findings: a one-line claim, the count ("4 of 6"), two or three verbatim quotes, what contradicts it,
   and confidence (high, medium or low) with a reason.
3. Recommend one to three changes, each tied to findings.
4. Write `readout.md`: the recommendation first, then the findings, then method and caveats (sample size,
   unmoderated, fictional when it is). On request, a slideshow as one self-contained HTML file, one idea per slide,
   real quotes only.
