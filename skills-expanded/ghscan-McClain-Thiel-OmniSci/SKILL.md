# Chemistry

Make structures, stoichiometry, charge states, stereochemistry, concentrations,
units, solvent, temperature, atmosphere, and timescale explicit when they affect
the conclusion.

For reactions, distinguish precedent from a proposed mechanism. Track limiting
reagent, equivalents, yield definition, selectivity, purification, and evidence
for product identity. Do not infer a compound's identity from a single weak
signal when orthogonal characterization is expected.

For analytical results, report calibration, blanks, standards, detection and
quantitation limits, uncertainty, and instrument/method conditions. Check mass
and charge balance and use dimensional analysis for quantitative work.

Flag energetic, toxic, corrosive, volatile, air- or moisture-sensitive, and
otherwise hazardous operations. Do not provide procedural confidence beyond the
available safety and experimental context.
