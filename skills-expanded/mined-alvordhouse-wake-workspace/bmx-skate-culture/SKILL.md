# BMX & Skate Culture

Alex rides/has ridden BMX and skateboarding is part of his cultural DNA — same world as punk, same ethos as DIY. This isn't trivia, it's identity.

## The Ethos (Why It Matters)

BMX and skate share the same DNA as punk rock:
- **DIY** — you build your own ramps, your own spots, your own style
- **No gatekeepers** — you don't need a club, a league, a uniform, or permission
- **Community over corporate** — local crews, neighborhood spots, sharing knowledge
- **Style over spectacle** — how you ride matters as much as what tricks you land
- **Eat concrete and try again** — failure is part of the process, not shameful

The half-pipe and the hackspace are the same culture. The person who commits to a trick they've never landed before is the same person who deploys untested infrastructure at 2am. Same mindset, different terrain.

## BMX Reference

### Disciplines
- **Street** — rails, ledges, stairs, gaps. Urban terrain as skatepark.
- **Park** — purpose-built skateparks, bowls, transitions
- **Dirt** — jump lines, rhythm sections, natural terrain
- **Flatland** — pure technical riding, no obstacles, all balance and timing
- **Vert** — halfpipes, big ramps

### Key Tricks (Street/Park)
- **Bunny hop** — foundation of everything
- **180/360** — rotations
- **Barspin** — spinning handlebars
- **Tailwhip** — frame spins around the fork
- **Flair** — backflip with 180 rotation on vert
- **Grind tricks** — pegs on rails (smith, feeble, icepick)
- **Manual** — wheelie without pedaling (front or rear)

### PNW BMX Scene
- Pacific Northwest has a strong BMX/skate culture historically
- Seattle/Tacoma/Olympia scene connected to punk and alternative music
- Gig Harbor area — small town, but access to Tacoma's scene
- Outdoor terrain: natural dirt features, urban spots in Tacoma/Seattle

### Culture Touchstones
- **Stranger BMG** — Cult, Sunday, Stranger — brands with street credibility
- **Dave Mirra, Mat Hoffman** — legends who defined the sport
- **Baco** — legendary underground BMX video series
- **Props BMX** — influential video magazine
- Connection to punk labels, DIY venues, same community as the music scene

## Skateboarding Reference

### Disciplines
- **Street** — same as BMX street but on a board
- **Vert** — halfpipes, pools
- **Park** — skateparks
- **Cruising/Longboard** — transportation + flow

### Key Tricks
- **Ollie** — foundation
- **Kickflip/Heelflip** — board flips
- **Grinds** — 50-50, nosegrind, boardslide, etc.
- **Manual** — nose or tail wheelie
- **Grab tricks** — indy, mute, stalefish on vert

### PNW Skate Scene
- Seattle has a strong legacy — legendary spots like Marginal Way
- Olympia scene tied to riot grrrl/punk (Bikini Kill, Sleater-Kinney world)
- Burnside skatepark in Portland — legendary DIY community-built park under a bridge
- Rain doesn't stop PNW skaters — covered spots, indoor parks

### Gear Notes
- **Deck brands** with punk/anti-corporate cred: Baker, Deathwish, Toy Machine, Anti-Hero
- Independent trucks — the gold standard for decades
- Spitfire wheels — long-running independent brand

## Architecture Connection

Alex's appreciation for architecture — both physical and digital — connects to BMX/skate:
- **Terrain reading** — a BMX rider reads a spot the same way an architect reads a space
- **Flow** — how you move through an environment, where transitions happen
- **Structure as creative material** — skaters/BMXers see ledges, stairs, banks as possibility, not just function
- **Burnside** as case study: community-built concrete paradise under a Portland bridge. No permits, no corporate backing, pure DIY collective building. The anarcho-syndicalist model applied to concrete.

## Kids & Action Sports

Two kids with ADHD — action sports are actually excellent for ADHD:
- **Immediate feedback loop** — you land it or you don't, no ambiguity
- **Physical release** — burns the energy that ADHD kids have in abundance
- **Mastery-based** — progress is visible and self-directed
- **Community** — skate/BMX crews are welcoming to kids who don't fit conventional sports
- **No bench** — everyone rides, nobody sits out

If the kids show interest: support it fully. This is exactly the kind of culture that can channel ADHD energy productively.
