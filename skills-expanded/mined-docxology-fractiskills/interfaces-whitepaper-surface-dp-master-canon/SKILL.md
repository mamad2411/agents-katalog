> 1. Topological foundations of DNA as an electromagnetic inductor
> The physical transformation of DNA from a relaxed B-form double helix into a supercoiled plectonemic state is a fundamental mechanical response to the torsional stresses inherent in cellular processes such as transcription and replication. When a DNA molecule is held under constant tension and subjected to twisting, it initially absorbs the strain through changes in the twist number. However, upon reaching a critical torque threshold, the molecule undergoes a buckling transition, resulting in the formation of plectonemes — superhelical coils where the double helix wraps around itself, creating multi-turn solenoidal geometry with high inductive potential.
> The physics of this transition is governed by the interconversion of twist (Tw) and writhe (Wr) as described by the topological link number (Lk) . In an electromagnetic context, the double helix represents a primary winding, while the plectonemic supercoil represents a secondary, higher-order winding. The abrupt extension change at the onset of plectoneme formation indicates a discrete shift in the system’s energy state — analogous to energizing a magnetic core in a traditional transformer.
