# Interview Coach

Build a complete interview preparation kit from a resume and a target role.

## Preparation Framework

### 1. Build a Story Bank

Every interview answer should draw from a prepared set of stories. Each story is a real experience from your career that demonstrates a competency.

**Required story categories (prepare at least one per category):**

- Leading a team through a challenge
- Solving a hard technical or analytical problem
- Resolving conflict with a colleague or stakeholder
- Failing at something and recovering
- Delivering results under pressure
- Going beyond what was expected
- Making a decision with incomplete information
- Influencing someone without direct authority

### 2. STAR Story Format

For each story, structure it as:

**Situation** (1-2 sentences): Set the scene. Where were you, what was the context, what was at stake?

**Task** (1 sentence): What were you specifically responsible for?

**Action** (2-3 sentences): What did YOU do? Be specific about your individual contribution, not the team's.

**Result** (1-2 sentences): What happened? Use a number. If the result was not positive, explain what you learned and how you applied it later.

**Target length:** 90 seconds to 2 minutes when spoken aloud.

### 3. Create a "Tell Me About Yourself" Script

This is the most common opening question. Prepare a 90-second response following this structure:

"I am a [role] with [X years] in [domain]. Most recently at [company], I [top achievement]. Before that, I [relevant earlier experience]. I am excited about [this role/company] because [genuine specific reason]."

Practice until it sounds conversational, not rehearsed.

### 4. Predict Role-Specific Questions

From the job description, identify:
- The top 3 hard skills they will test (prepare examples demonstrating each)
- The top 3 soft skills they will test (prepare STAR stories for each)
- Any concerns they might have about your candidacy (gaps, short tenures, career changes)
- Company-specific questions they may ask ("why us?", "what do you know about our product?")

### 5. Prepare Questions to Ask

Have 3-5 questions ready for each interviewer type:

**For hiring managers:** "What does success look like in the first 90 days?" / "What are the biggest challenges the team is facing?"

**For peers:** "What is your favorite part of working here?" / "How does the team make decisions?"

**For executives:** "What is the company's top strategic priority this year?" / "How does this team contribute to that?"

**Never ask about:** Salary (save for HR/recruiter), basic facts available on the website, negative topics.

### 6. Difficult Question Playbook

**"What is your greatest weakness?"**
Pick a real weakness. Explain what you have done to address it. Keep it brief.

**"Why are you leaving your current job?"**
Stay positive. Focus on what you are moving toward, not what you are running from.

**"Why should we hire you?"**
Connect your top 2-3 strengths directly to their top 2-3 needs. End with genuine enthusiasm.

**"What are your salary expectations?"**
Deflect to their range first: "What is the range budgeted for this role?" If pressed, give a researched range based on market data.
