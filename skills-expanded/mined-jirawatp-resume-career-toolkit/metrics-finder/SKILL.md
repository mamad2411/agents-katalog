# Metrics Finder

Every job produces measurable output. This skill finds the numbers hiding in any experience.

## The Discovery Framework

For any role, probe these six dimensions:

**Volume:** How many things did you handle? (customers, projects, tickets, reports, transactions, documents, events, locations, products)

**Frequency:** How often? (per day, per week, per month, per quarter)

**Money:** What was the financial scale? (budget managed, revenue influenced, costs saved, deal sizes, spend overseen)

**Time:** What got faster? (processing time, turnaround, delivery speed, response time, time-to-hire, onboarding duration)

**Quality:** What got better? (error rates, satisfaction scores, accuracy, compliance rates, retention, NPS)

**Comparison:** How did you stack up? (vs. quota, vs. team average, vs. previous period, vs. industry benchmark)

## When You Truly Do Not Have Outcome Metrics

Quantify your inputs instead. Inputs are always countable:

- "Wrote documentation" becomes "Authored 60-page technical guide referenced by 40+ engineers"
- "Supported sales team" becomes "Created 25+ custom proposals per quarter for enterprise prospects"
- "Managed social media" becomes "Published 5 posts daily across 3 platforms to an audience of 15K followers"

## Estimation Techniques

**Conservative rounding:** If you think you saved 100 hours, say "75+ hours"
**Ranges:** "Managed 8-12 concurrent projects"
**Lower bound:** "Served 200+ customers monthly"
**Derived math:** 5 calls/day x 250 working days = "Conducted 1,250+ client consultations annually"
**Relative:** "Outperformed team average by 30%"

## Role-Specific Metric Sources

**Admin/Office:** Schedules managed, meetings coordinated, travel arrangements per month, office locations supported, expense reports processed

**Retail:** Daily transactions, average basket size, upsell rate, shrinkage reduction, customers served per shift

**Healthcare:** Patients per day, care team size, documentation accuracy, compliance audit scores, protocol adherence

**Education:** Students per class, test score improvements, curriculum units developed, parent meetings per semester

**Nonprofit:** Donors managed, funds raised, volunteers coordinated, program participants served, grant applications written

## Rules

- Every bullet must have at least one number after revision
- Numbers must be defensible in an interview
- When estimating, err on the conservative side
- Never fabricate -- only quantify what actually happened
- Two numbers per bullet is ideal; more than three is cluttered
