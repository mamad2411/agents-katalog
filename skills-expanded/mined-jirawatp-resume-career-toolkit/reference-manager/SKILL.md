# Reference Manager

Select, format, prepare, and coordinate professional references.

## Reference Selection

**Priority order:**
1. Recent direct manager (most valuable; employers expect this)
2. Senior leader who observed your work closely
3. Cross-functional partner who can speak to collaboration
4. Client or customer (for client-facing roles)
5. Direct report (for management roles)

**Avoid:** Personal friends, family, HR contacts who only verified employment, people from 10+ years ago unless the relationship continued, anyone who might be lukewarm.

**Rule:** If you are not confident someone will be enthusiastic, do not use them. A tepid reference is worse than a missing one.

## Reference List Format

```
PROFESSIONAL REFERENCES

[Full Name]
[Current Title]
[Current Company]
Phone: [number]
Email: [professional email]
Relationship: [how you worked together and for how long]
```

Provide 3-5 references. Match the formatting to your resume style. Use a separate page with the same header as your resume.

## Reference Preparation

### Step 1: Ask Permission
Call or email. Explain the specific role. Gauge enthusiasm. If they hesitate, thank them and move on.

### Step 2: Brief Them
Send: your resume, the job description, 2-3 specific talking points you want them to emphasize, and the timeline for when they might be contacted.

### Step 3: Follow Up
After the reference check, thank them regardless of outcome. Let them know the result. Offer to return the favor.

## Common Reference Check Questions

- "What were their primary responsibilities?"
- "What are their greatest strengths?"
- "What areas could they improve?"
- "How did they handle pressure?"
- "Would you rehire them?" (the most important question -- make sure your references can answer this with an enthusiastic yes)

## Timing

Do not include references with your initial application. Bring the list to interviews but do not offer it unprompted. Provide when asked, usually after final rounds. Always re-confirm permission before sharing each time.
