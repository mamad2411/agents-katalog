# Case Study Builder

Transform a resume bullet into a structured case study that shows how you think, not just what you delivered.

## Case Study Structure

### Overview (50 words)
Project name, your role, company, timeline, team size, and one-sentence result.

### Problem (100-150 words)
What was broken, missing, or needed? Include business context, user pain, and the specific goal you were trying to hit. Use a number to frame the severity ("68% cart abandonment, 13 points above industry average").

### Process (200-300 words)
Show your thinking:
- What research did you do? (user interviews, data analysis, competitive review)
- What options did you consider?
- Why did you choose the path you chose?
- What constraints shaped your decisions? (timeline, budget, technical limitations)

This is the most important section. Employers hire for process, not just results.

### Solution (150-200 words)
Describe what you built, designed, launched, or changed. Be specific about your individual contribution vs. the team's. Reference deliverables: mockups, architecture diagrams, campaign assets, code, documentation.

### Results (100 words)
Hard numbers with before/after comparisons and timeframes. Include both primary metrics (the goal you set) and secondary effects (unexpected benefits). If results are confidential, use percentages.

### Reflection (50-100 words)
What worked, what you would do differently, and what you learned. This section demonstrates self-awareness and growth mindset.

## Length and Format

- Target 800-1,200 words total (5-8 minute read)
- Use headers to make it scannable
- Include placeholders for visuals (before/after screenshots, charts, diagrams)
- Write in past tense, first person
- Prepare a 2-minute verbal version for interviews
