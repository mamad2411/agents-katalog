# Academic CV Guide

Build a comprehensive academic CV that follows disciplinary conventions.

## Academic CV vs. Industry Resume

An academic CV is a complete scholarly record. Unlike a resume, it grows throughout your career, includes everything, and follows field-specific conventions for ordering and formatting.

## Standard Sections (in typical order for research-focused roles)

1. Contact information (include ORCID, institutional email, academic website)
2. Education (all degrees, advisors, dissertation titles)
3. Academic appointments
4. Publications (peer-reviewed articles, books, chapters, conference proceedings -- number them)
5. Presentations (invited talks first, then conference presentations)
6. Grants and funding (agency, role as PI/Co-PI, title, dates, dollar amount)
7. Teaching (courses taught with enrollment, evaluations summary, curriculum developed)
8. Mentoring (graduate students, postdocs, undergraduates -- include current positions of graduates)
9. Service (to profession, university, department)
10. Professional memberships
11. Honors and awards

## Section Ordering by Role Type

- **Research faculty:** Lead with publications and grants
- **Teaching faculty:** Lead with teaching and curriculum development
- **Postdoc:** Lead with publications and research skills
- **Administrative:** Lead with leadership and service

## Publication Formatting

Bold your name in all author lists. Include DOIs. Indicate student co-authors with an asterisk. Use the citation style standard in your discipline (APA for social sciences, numbered for sciences, Chicago for humanities). Separate peer-reviewed from non-peer-reviewed work.

## CV Length by Career Stage

- Graduate student: 2-4 pages
- Postdoc: 3-6 pages
- Early career faculty: 5-10 pages
- Senior faculty: 10-30+ pages

Do not pad, but do not artificially constrain. The CV should be comprehensive.
