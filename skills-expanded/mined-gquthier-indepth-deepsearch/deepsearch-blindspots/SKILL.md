# Blind spots

Reads the whole dossier as a skeptical adversary and looks for what would make its conclusion wrong, including the conclusions you wrote yourself.

Give this stage to a fresh agent that did not write the studies. Hand it the files, without your conclusion. Read `../_shared/evidence-protocol.md`.

## The audit

Check each of these. For each, quote the passage and show the number it changes.

**Fairness of comparison**
- [ ] **Unequal depth.** How many searches, actors, Pages and quotes on each side? A deep dossier against a thin one flatters the thin one. If unequal, say which conclusions depend on it, and re-run the thin side.
- [ ] **Different denominators.** Is a cost divided by revenue on one side and by profit on the other? Recompute on one base.
- [ ] **Different assumptions for the same fact.** Did one side get a delay range from its stress test, and the other a shorter one without a source?
- [ ] **Sizes that are not comparable.** One side's raw universe against the other's filtered core.

**Logic of the scoring**
- [ ] **An argument counted twice,** for and against. Does the fact even exist? Open the primary source.
- [ ] **A score that judges an offer.** A low score may mean "this format does not fit", with the market itself never tested. What other format was scored?
- [ ] **A grid built for another goal.** Criteria set for 50 clients say little about a goal of 3. Does any criterion measure the real constraint?
- [ ] **Removed or added criteria.** Do the results survive if the removed one comes back, and the reverse?
- [ ] **Scores without sources.** Which notes rest on no evidence? Recompute with them marked down, and show the new gap.

**Quality of the evidence**
- [ ] **Few voices behind a big claim.** Who says it, how many people, and what do they sell?
- [ ] **Keystone numbers from vendors.** Which figure moves the answer the most, and who published it?
- [ ] **The keystone assumption.** Which unmeasured rate moves the funnel the most?
- [ ] **Who is missing.** Are the people who can pay anywhere in the corpus? Has anyone in the target been spoken to?
- [ ] **Stale figures.** How old is each decisive number? Do packaged copies still show numbers later corrected?
- [ ] **Survivors.** Are failed sellers and silent buyers absent from the sources?

**The founder**
- [ ] **Beliefs never tested.** List them. What is the founder hoping is true? Where does the excitement come from, and does that source hold?
- [ ] **The real constraint.** The grid may measure market size while the true obstacle is charging a high price to strangers with no case study.
- [ ] **Sunk attention.** Has more time on one niche made it feel either riskier or more familiar than it is?

## Pre-mortem

Assume it failed a year from now. Write the most likely story, in five lines. Then list the bottlenecks, ranked, each with its evidence, severity and a cheap test.

Look specifically for what kills the idea rather than what supports it: rules, delivery, the guarantee, the buyer's sales ability, capacity, seasonality, audience burnout on a small market, and copycats if the founder builds in public.

Then give the strongest arguments for proceeding anyway, and what evidence would change the verdict. A one-sided audit is its own blind spot.

## Output: `08-blindspots.md`

Keep it short and open with the verdict.

```markdown
**<One sentence: what the dossier gets right, and how much the conclusion moves once corrected.>**

## Why the result looks like this
<A table of the numbers that drove the result.>

## What was wrong
<Each error: the claim, the correction, the source, the effect on the numbers.>

## Your blind spots
1. <The blind spot, in one line. The evidence. The consequence.>

## Corrected reading
<Old number, new number, and how much of the new number is your judgement.>

## What I would do
<The cheapest step that settles the question.>
```

**Correct yourself out loud.** If an earlier study, or you, presented something as safe or settled, write that it was wrong and why. Then apply the corrections to the earlier files and to any packaged copy.

Do not overcorrect. A dossier that turned out to be biased may still reach the right conclusion, so recompute before you change the verdict, and label the parts that are your judgement.

## Done when

- [ ] Every checklist item was checked, with its result
- [ ] Each correction shows the number it changes
- [ ] The pre-mortem includes the strongest argument for proceeding
- [ ] The cheapest deciding step is named
- [ ] Corrections reached the earlier files
