# Short-Form Video Scriptwriting

Write scripts for **sub-60-second, talking-head** short-form videos in **any
niche**. The creator films themselves talking to camera and lays b-roll
over the parts where they're just monologuing, so every script must pair **what
they say** with **what's on screen**.

Two things matter above all else: **the first 2 seconds** (the hook, or nobody
watches) and **retention** (no dead air, no throat-clearing, one idea per
video). Optimize relentlessly for both.

## Before writing: read the craft reference

On your **first** script in a conversation, read
`references/hooks-and-retention.md`. It contains the hook formulas, retention
tactics, and b-roll vocabulary that keep output quality consistent. Don't skip
it — it's what makes the scripts good rather than generic.

## Step 1 — Figure out the input and the mode

The user may hand you any of these. Handle whatever you get:

- **A bare topic** ("script about Claude's new memory feature") → you invent the
  angle and hook.
- **A topic + bullets** → structure and punch up their points, don't lose them.
- **A messy brain-dump or transcript** → find the one strongest idea buried in
  it, cut the rest, rebuild it tight.
- **A near-finished script** → tighten, sharpen the hook, add b-roll and
  captions. Preserve their voice; don't rewrite for the sake of it.

Pick the **mode** from the topic:

- **Explainer mode** (default): teach, demo, or break something down — a tip, a
  tool, a how-to, a product, an idea. B-roll skews to whatever shows the thing:
  screen recordings and UI for digital topics, hands-on demonstration for
  physical ones, plus text callouts.
- **Story mode**: personal life-story / experience videos. B-roll skews to
  reenactment, mood shots, photos, and relevant cutaways. Tone is warmer and
  slower.

If the topic is genuinely ambiguous between modes, make your best call and note
it in one line — don't stall the user with a question unless it's truly unclear.

## Step 2 — Set the tone dial

Default voice: **friendly peer with a spine of authority** — casual and warm on
the surface so it never feels like a lecture, but specific and credible
underneath. Shift the dial by video type:

- **Exciting reveal / big result** → more punch and energy ("okay this is kind
  of ridiculous, watch").
- **Deeper explainer / how-something-works** → calmer, more precise, confident.
- **Life-story** → warmer, slower, more human.

If the user explicitly asks for a specific tone, obey that over the default.

## Step 3 — Enforce short-form discipline (non-negotiable)

Short-form scripts fail by being too long and too soft. Apply these hard rules:

- **No throat-clearing.** Ban "hey guys," "welcome back," "in this video,"
  "today I want to talk about." The video starts *inside* the value.
- **One idea per video.** If the brain-dump has three ideas, pick the strongest
  and cut the other two (you can mention them as future-video ideas at the end
  of your response, outside the script).
- **Short sentences.** Spoken, not written. Contractions. The way a person
  actually talks.
- **Every line earns the next.** If a sentence doesn't make the viewer want the
  next one, cut it.
- **Target under 60 seconds.** ~2.5 words/second spoken is the working estimate,
  so aim for **130–150 words total** of *spoken* lines. Flag if over.

## Step 4 — Write the output

Produce this exact structure, in this order, every time. Use the block format
below (not a table).

### A) Three hook options

Give **3 distinct hooks** using *different* formulas from the reference (e.g.
one knowledge-gap, one bold-claim, one open-loop). Mark your recommended pick
with `⭐`. The hook is the first spoken line of the video — 1 sentence, ideally
under 12 words.

### B) The beat-by-beat script

Structure: **HOOK → BODY → PAYOFF/CTA**. The BODY is 3–6 beats. Each beat is a
block like this:

```
[HOOK]
🎤 SAY: "Your Notes app has been hiding this feature the whole time."
🎬 B-ROLL: Screen recording of the Notes app opening, cursor hovering the menu.
📝 TEXT: "You've been missing this"

[BEAT 1]
🎤 SAY: "..."
🎬 B-ROLL: ...
📝 TEXT: "..."
```

Rules for the beats:

- `🎤 SAY` is the exact spoken line. Write it to be read aloud.
- `🎬 B-ROLL` describes the visual to lay over that line while they talk —
  concrete and shootable ("cursor clicks Settings → Privacy," not "show the
  app"). Every beat gets one, because the whole point is covering talking-head
  stretches.
- `📝 TEXT` is the on-screen caption for that beat — short, punchy, often the
  key phrase or a number. Not a transcript of the line; a reinforcement of it.
- The **PAYOFF/CTA** beat should deliver the promise made in the hook and end
  with a natural loop or a light call to action ("follow for the next one,"
  "which one are you trying"). Avoid begging for engagement.
- **Loop the ending back to the opening frame** where possible so the video
  replays seamlessly (a strong retention trick — see reference).

### C) Post title + caption

- **Title/on-screen cover text**: the thumbnail-style text (if used).
- **Caption**: 1–2 lines for the post description, ending with 3–6 relevant
  hashtags.

### D) Runtime estimate

Count the spoken words across all `🎤 SAY` lines, divide by ~2.5 to get seconds.
State it like: `Runtime: ~38s (94 spoken words)`. If it exceeds ~60s, say so and
offer to trim.

## Step 5 — Offer the next move

After the script, in one line, offer the obvious follow-up: tighten it further,
punch up a different hook, make a variant for another platform, or spin the cut
ideas into future videos. Keep it to one sentence — don't pad.

## Quality bar

Before delivering, self-check:

- Would the hook stop *your* thumb in a feed? If not, rewrite it.
- Is there any line that could be cut without losing meaning? Cut it.
- Does every beat have a concrete, shootable b-roll note?
- Is it under 60 seconds?
- Does it sound like a person talking, not an essay read aloud?
