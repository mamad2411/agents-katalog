# SEO Interview

## Overview

Extract what success means for search visibility before changing pages or templates.

## When to Use

- New client/site engagement
- Vague “improve SEO” requests

**Not for:** Implementing fixes (hand off to domain skills after the brief).

## Official sources

- https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo
- https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide

## Process

1. Ask **one question at a time** until ~90% confidence.
2. Cover: primary audience, markets/languages, conversion goal, CMS/stack, known GSC issues, competitors (optional), constraints (legal, brand, noindex areas).
3. Surface assumptions explicitly; wait for correction.
4. Produce a short **SEO Brief**:
   - Goal
   - In-scope URL patterns / templates
   - Out of scope
   - Success signals (GSC queries, index coverage, CWV — not “rank #1 guarantees”)
5. Recommend next skill: usually `seo-audit-plan` or a specific domain skill.

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "They said improve SEO — start rewriting." | Without goals/constraints you optimize the wrong thing. |
| "Success means rank #1." | Prefer measurable GSC/index/CWV outcomes — no guarantees. |
| "One workshop question is enough." | Ask until confidence is high; surface assumptions. |

## Red Flags

- Inventing ranking factors not documented by Google
- Skipping verification ("looks fine")
- Advising spammy shortcuts (cloaking, doorways, link schemes)

## Verification

- [ ] Brief written with goals and constraints
- [ ] No implementation started without agreement on scope
- [ ] Success metrics are measurable and non-manipulative
