# Point Partner Creative Review

Perform an evidence-led first pass that removes obvious defects and gives the appropriate human reviewer a shorter, clearer queue. The result is review support, not approval.

## Required guidance

Read [review-rubric.md](references/review-rubric.md) for every review.

Reuse the existing skills instead of restating their subject-matter rules:

- Use `point-brand-guidelines` whenever the submission has a visual component. Read its visual guidance and compare Point logos and fonts with its approved bundled assets.
- Use `point-brand-voice` whenever the submission contains copy or product messaging.
- Use `point-social-ad-ideation` only when the user also asks to create, replace, or substantially improve a concept. Ordinary triage does not require ideation.

Treat current Legal- or Compliance-approved materials and current product sources as authoritative. Historical partner creative, Slack examples, brand-guide mockups, and prior reviews show issue patterns; they are not current claim or disclosure sources.

## Review workflow

### 1. Establish the review frame

Identify, when available:

- the partner and every asset, page, slide, frame, or variant in scope
- the format, placement, audience, targeting, market or state, and intended destination
- whether the execution is partner-branded, Point-branded, or intentionally cobranded
- the requested review layer: Brand, Content, Compliance, or a general first pass
- the current approved copy, product facts, logo kit, or other comparison source

Continue with explicit assumptions when a missing detail does not change the review materially. When brand mode, destination, targeting, or jurisdiction would change the judgment, label the issue **Needs context** rather than inventing an answer.

If an asset, link, attachment, layer, or page cannot be inspected, list it under **Not reviewed**. Never imply that an inaccessible item passed.

### 2. Inspect the submitted work

Review the artifact itself, not only its description. For a batch, maintain an inventory and inspect each item independently before evaluating consistency across the set.

Anchor every finding to a precise location such as the filename, slide, page, frame, headline, CTA, footer, timestamp, or visual region. Compare Point brand elements with approved source files rather than judging them from memory.

### 3. Apply the obvious-issue pass

Use every applicable section of the rubric. Distinguish among:

- **Observed defect:** visible or stated in the submitted artifact.
- **Verification gap:** depends on a current fact, approval, link behavior, audience, state, or other missing evidence.
- **Quality suggestion:** improves clarity, consistency, credibility, or craft without correcting a clear error.

Partner styling may differ from Point's visual system. Treat that difference as intentional unless it misuses a Point element, conflicts with the stated brand mode, creates a jarring or untrustworthy Point handoff, or makes the set internally inconsistent.

### 4. Prioritize the findings

Assign one action level:

- **Fix before review:** an obvious brand misuse, factual contradiction, misleading statement, broken journey, serious legibility problem, or other defect that should be corrected before the next human review.
- **Verify or route:** a claim, disclosure, state rule, qualification, destination behavior, or contextual choice that requires an owner or authoritative source.
- **Consider:** a production-quality or messaging refinement that is useful but not a demonstrated defect.

Do not call an item legally compliant, approved, or safe to publish. Compliance determines compliance; Brand and Content own judgment calls in their domains.

### 5. Make each comment usable

For each finding, provide:

1. what is present
2. why it matters in this context
3. the smallest clear corrective action
4. the source or owner that should confirm the change when verification is needed

Use direct language. Separate a required correction from an optional rewrite. Do not silently replace a claim with a new factual claim; use a conditional recommendation or placeholder until current approved language is available.

### 6. Return the review

Default to this compact structure:

#### Review status

Use one of:

- **Obvious issues found**
- **No obvious issues found in the reviewed material**
- **Review incomplete**

Add one sentence describing the scope and the highest-priority pattern.

#### Findings

| Action | Location | Category | Finding and why it matters | Requested change or owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Order findings by action level, then by asset. Keep separate defects in separate rows.

#### Needs context or not reviewed

List only questions and gaps that could change the review. State which items were inaccessible or outside scope.

#### Human handoff

Name the remaining review owners:

- Brand/Design for identity and visual judgment
- Content for copy, positioning, and product explanation
- Compliance/Legal for claims, disclosures, licensing, state requirements, and final approval

If no obvious issue is found, say explicitly that this is a first-pass result rather than approval.

## Scope boundary

Complete the requested analysis only. Editing the partner's files, contacting the partner, submitting to Compliance, or marking work approved requires a separate user request.
