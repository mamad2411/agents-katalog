# Dan Martell Executive AI Prompts: Business Scaling & Operational Productivity Workflows

This skill provides a curated collection of AI prompt templates and strategic guidance, inspired by Dan Martell's approach to business scaling and operational productivity. It's designed to help entrepreneurs and executives leverage AI tools like ChatGPT to accelerate growth, refine strategies, and optimize workflows.

## Core Philosophy & Mental Models

*   **AI as a Force Multiplier:** The fundamental principle is that AI should not just automate tasks but act as a strategic partner, amplifying human capabilities. This means using AI for ideation, complex analysis, and generating structured outputs that would otherwise be time-consuming or require specialized expertise.
*   **The Master Prompt:** A well-crafted "Master Prompt" is crucial for consistent and high-quality AI interactions. It establishes the AI's persona, your context, desired tone, and output format, ensuring that subsequent interactions are more relevant and efficient. This is akin to setting the stage for a highly productive meeting.
*   **Iterative Refinement:** AI outputs are rarely perfect on the first try. The process of using AI for business development is iterative. Expect to refine prompts, ask follow-up questions, and adapt AI-generated content to your specific needs. This mirrors the agile development and continuous improvement cycles in business.
*   **Systemization for Scale:** The ultimate goal is to build systems and workflows that can scale. AI prompts are tools to help architect these systems, from initial business planning to ongoing operational optimization. The focus is on creating repeatable processes that drive efficiency and growth.
*   **Leveraging Expertise:** AI can synthesize vast amounts of information and mimic expert roles. This skill leverages that capability by providing prompts that instruct the AI to act as specific experts (e.g., marketing strategist, financial analyst, operations consultant), drawing on their simulated knowledge base.

## Key Decision Rules & Frameworks

*   **Use AI for Ideation & Exploration When:** You need to brainstorm new business ideas, explore market opportunities, or generate creative content.
    *   *Example:* Use the "Business Idea & Value Proposition" prompts when you have a nascent concept and need to refine its core offering.
*   **Use AI for Structured Planning When:** You need to create foundational documents like business plans, marketing strategies, or operational SOPs.
    *   *Example:* Use the "Business Plan & Strategy" prompts to generate an initial outline for your business plan.
*   **Use AI for Problem Solving & Analysis When:** You encounter specific business challenges, need to analyze competitors, or require data-driven insights.
    *   *Example:* Use the "Market Research & Competitive Analysis" prompts to understand your competitive landscape.
*   **Prefer Detailed, Contextual Prompts Over Vague Ones:** The more specific information you provide (industry, target audience, problem, desired output format), the better the AI's response will be.
    *   *Decision:* If an AI output is too generic, the next step is to add more context to the prompt, not to abandon the tool.
*   **Prefer Iterative Prompting Over Single, Complex Prompts:** For complex tasks, break them down into smaller, sequential prompts. This allows for course correction and ensures each step builds upon the previous one.
    *   *Decision:* If a single prompt yields an overwhelming or unfocused response, break the request into 2-3 smaller, more targeted prompts.
*   **Use the "Master Prompt" Framework When:** You anticipate frequent interactions with the AI on similar topics or for a specific persona.
    *   *Decision:* Create and save a Master Prompt before embarking on a significant project that requires consistent AI assistance.

## Anti-Patterns & Common Traps

*   **Treating AI as a Black Box:** Blindly accepting AI outputs without critical review or adaptation. AI is a tool, not an infallible oracle.
    *   *Avoid:* Copy-pasting AI-generated content directly into critical business documents without review.
*   **Over-Reliance on Generic Prompts:** Using basic prompts that don't provide sufficient context, leading to superficial or irrelevant answers.
    *   *Avoid:* Asking "Write a business plan" without specifying industry, product, or target market.
*   **Neglecting the Iterative Process:** Expecting a perfect, final output from the first prompt.
    *   *Avoid:* Giving up after one attempt if the initial response isn't exactly what you need.
*   **Failing to Define Your "Master Prompt":** This leads to inconsistent AI behavior and requires you to re-explain your context and preferences repeatedly.
    *   *Avoid:* Not taking the time to craft and save a Master Prompt for your primary AI persona.
*   **Ignoring the "Voice and Style" of Expert AI:** When using specialized AI like the Book Architect, failing to understand its intended output style can lead to mismatched results.
    *   *Avoid:* Expecting a Book Architect to generate a fictional narrative when its purpose is to outline non-fiction based on a person's work.
*   **Not Verifying Critical Information:** Relying solely on AI for legal, financial, or regulatory advice without consulting qualified human professionals.
    *   *Avoid:* Implementing legal structures or financial strategies based solely on AI recommendations without professional validation.

### **I. Foundational Business Development**

#### **1. Business Idea & Value Proposition**

*   **Prompt 1: Refining Your Core Idea and Strategic Innovation**
    *   **Role:** Experienced startup consultant.
    *   **Context:** Business idea in the `[INDUSTRY]` sector, offering `[PRODUCT/SERVICE]`. Target customers are `[TARGET AUDIENCE/DEMOGRAPHICS]`, solving `[CUSTOMER PROBLEM]`.
    *   **Objectives:**
        1.  Refine unique value proposition and identify differentiators.
        2.  Provide a concise "elevator pitch."
        3.  Recommend immediate next steps for idea validation.
    *   **Placeholders:** `[INDUSTRY]`, `[PRODUCT/SERVICE]`, `[TARGET AUDIENCE/DEMOGRAPHICS]`, `[CUSTOMER PROBLEM]`.

*   **Prompt 2: Testing Viability & Identifying Core Benefits**
    *   **Role:** Business coach.
    *   **Context:** Planned product/service is `[PRODUCT/SERVICE]`, aimed at solving `[CUSTOMER PROBLEM]` for `[TARGET AUDIENCE/DEMOGRAPHICS]`.
    *   **Objectives:**
        1.  List 3-5 core benefits for target customers.
        2.  Suggest quick, low-cost methods or MVP approaches to test these benefits.

        2.  Identify major customer segments beyond the immediate `[TARGET AUDIENCE/DEMOGRAPHICS]`.
        3.  List key industry trends or technologies shaping the market.

### **IV. Advanced AI Application**

#### **Master Prompting**

*   **Understanding and Creating Your Master Prompt:**
    *   **Concept:** A Master Prompt is a detailed instruction set that defines your persona, context, desired tone, language style, and output preferences for the AI. It ensures consistency and relevance in AI interactions.
    *   **Example (for Dan Martell):**
        ```
        "Hey ChatGPT — here’s how to show up for me every time we chat:
        My name’s Dan Martell.
        I’m a software entrepreneur, investor, and executive coach who’s exited 3 companies. I coach high-performing SaaS founders and help people buy back their time.
        I love my content to sound energetic, motivational, and casual — like I’m speaking on stage or to a friend. Think short sentences, occasional punchy fragments, conversational flow.
        My vibe? A mix of mentorship and blunt honesty, but always positive and supportive. I sometimes use phrases like “the truth is...”, “the best part?”, or “everything you want exists on the other side of fear.”
        I like explanations in simple language (around a grade 5 reading level) with metaphors and examples.
        I prefer content formatted with lots of line breaks so it’s easy to read, and I love numbered points for clarity.
        When you write for me:
        1. Keep it PG (no heavy swearing)
        2. Use American spelling
        3. Use rhetorical questions to hook readers
        4. Highlight important words with caps, bold, or italics
        5. End sections on a strong, impactful sentence
        Also, I sometimes want my content in specific formats like:
        1. Instagram captions
        2. Email newsletters
        3. YouTube scripts
        4. Book excerpts
        5. Short-form ads
        For future chats, remember who I am and how I speak. If you’re unsure, ask clarifying questions first."
        ```
    *   **Action:** Create your own Master Prompt, save it (e.g., as a PDF), and upload it or paste it into your AI project's initial instructions.

#### **Specialized AI Tools**

*   **Book Architect Custom GPT:**
    *   **Purpose:** To comb through the public content of prominent figures and generate a structured book outline based on their teachings, mental models, and language.
    *   **How to Use:** Provide the name of a public figure and the desired book topic.
    *   **Example Input:** "Mr. Beast book on Content Creation"
    *   **Link:** `https://chatgpt.com/g/g-6867e586d500819189774562feffe01c-book-architect?model=gpt-5-pro`
    *   **Voice and Style:** Insightful, concise, emotionally resonant, and faithful to the public figure’s tone. Avoids fluff, stays grounded in direct quotes and real content.
    *   **Behavior Instructions:**
        1.  **Research & Analyze:** Search and analyze top content (videos, interviews, blogs, quotes) of the figure. Extract mental models, frameworks, language, and metaphors. Use only verified content.
        2.  **Structure a Complete Book Outline:** Format includes Introduction, 5 Big Ideas, 5 Quotes/Aphorisms, and 12 Chapters (each with Title, Core Focus, Opening Story, 5 Big Ideas, 5 Quotes/Aphorisms). Chapters build logically.
        3.  **Match Their Voice:** Emulate the tone of figures like Peter Crone (grounded, direct), Naval Ravikant (philosophical), Tim Grover (intense), or Brene Brown (empathetic).
        4.  **Do Not Do This:** Do not fabricate quotes/ideas, include unnecessary biography, or add content in your own voice.
    *   **Final Output Requirements:** At least 2,000 words, 12 chapters + intro, 5 big ideas and 5 quotes per section, authentic voice, feels like the person wrote it.

#### **Prompt Engineering Examples**

These examples demonstrate how to structure prompts for specific business functions. Remember to replace bracketed placeholders with your specific details.

*   **Marketing Example:**
    *   **Prompt:** "Act as a headline generator and create 25 provocative, unique blog headlines for my boutique fitness studio that specializes in HIIT classes. Use these keywords as inspiration: Nutrition, mental health, fitness, weight loss, strength, endurance, longevity. Format them by major theme in a bullet list."

*   **Sales Example:**
    *   **Prompt:** "Act as an expert sales consultant and create a list of 10 common objections that my potential clients may bring up when I’m selling my premium YouTube services, along with three possible ways to overcome each one. My clients have at least 1000 subscribers and are comfortable on camera. Format the list in a nested bullet list."

*   **Customer Success Example:**
    *   **Prompt:** "Act as a customer support representative and draft an email reply to an upset guest at my luxury hotel, acknowledging the error, apologizing, but also gently inform them that we cannot provide the $2000 refund. The issue came from being downgraded upon arrival due to an overbooking. Format it in the first person when the writer is talking about themselves, but use “we” when referring to the company itself."

*   **Product Example:**
    *   **Prompt:** "Act as a product manager and create a list of all internal and external factors that could influence the outcome of our high-end treadmill sales goal. We’re looking to grow from 8.2 sales per retailer per month to 12 in the next 3 months. List highly specific details, such as the effectiveness of in-store product demonstrations, customer reviews, price sensitivity in different regions, competitive promotions, etc. Format as a table with internal in column 1 and external in column 2."

*   **Engineering Example:**
    *   **Prompt:** "Act as a software engineer and list the 20 most likely ways that my new feature could break, and what the test cases would be that I’d need to test against to ensure that we catch the bugs before we ship them to production? The new feature is group texting within a text marketing platform. Format the items ranked by severity if it broke."

*   **Operations Example (Spreadsheet Formula):**
    *   **Prompt:** "Act as a Google Spreadsheets expert and create the formula to measure “Progress To Goal” based on the “Monthly Total” and “Monthly Goal” columns in my spreadsheet. Column B is “Monthly Total”, column C is “Monthly Goal” and column D is “Progress to Goal”. Format the “Progress To Goal” red if it’s below the goal, and green if the goal has been met or exceeded."
