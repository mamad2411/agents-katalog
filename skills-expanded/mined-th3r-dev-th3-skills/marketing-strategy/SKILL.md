# Strategy

Price and listing come last. First decide who the service is for and why they should pick you;
the rest follows from that. Frameworks and their sources: [references/frameworks.md](references/frameworks.md).

## Workflow

1. **One buyer, one problem.** Segment the market, pick one target, write the position in one
   line: "for <who> who <need>, I <what> so that <result>". A narrow first offer earns reviews faster
   than a menu; widen after the first reviews.
2. **Value proposition from real work.** Every claim comes from something done and checkable: the
   user's profile, repo, past projects. Outcomes over tools: "แก้เนื้อหาเองได้", not the framework.
3. **Offer ladder.** Starter (small, fixed, low risk, wins the first reviews), main (most buyers),
   premium (bigger scope), and an ongoing package (maintenance, retainer) for income after delivery.
   Pricing fills in the numbers.
4. **Funnel.** Awareness (posts, the listing image), consideration (the description, work samples,
   the process image), conversion (packages, a clear first step: "ทักแชทมาเล่าโจทย์"), retention
   (support period, then the ongoing package). Keeping a client costs far less than finding one.
5. **Metrics.** Pick two or three: views to chats, chats to orders, repeat orders. Track them per
   month and change one thing at a time.

## Output

A short strategy note (position line, persona, ladder, funnel touchpoints, metrics) that pricing
and listing read. It is not published.

## Done when

- The position line names one buyer and one result, and every claim in it has a source.
- The ladder has a starter offer and an ongoing package.
