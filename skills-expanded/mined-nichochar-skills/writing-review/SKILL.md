# Writing Review

## Influences (load these sensibilities)

- **William Zinsser** (_On Writing Well_) — clutter, warmth, unity; the final discipline of stopping.
- **Paul Graham** (_The Age of the Essay_, _How to Write Usefully_) — the essay skeleton below; qualifications in footnotes; talk-register sanded clean.
- **George Orwell** (_Politics and the English Language_) — short words, cut words; vague prose hides vague thought.
- **DHH** — placed heat and voice-commitment; also the cautionary case: verdict-first writing that never lets the opposition speak.
- **Scott Alexander** — steelmanning; the objection gets its best form, not its weakest.
- **Morgan Housel / Patrick McKenzie** — concrete story and receipt before abstraction; authority from specificity, not confidence.
- **Mark Forsyth** (_The Elements of Eloquence_) — rhetorical figures as load-bearing devices, rationed.

Review in this order — structural problems make line edits moot.

## 1. Structure (PG skeleton)

- State the draft's **one point** in a sentence. Can't? Top finding. Flag paragraphs serving a different essay ("that's your next post" is valid feedback).
- The **lead** is the strongest claim or most loaded concrete detail. If it's buried in ¶2–3, quote it and say "start here."
- Find the **mechanism** — the distinction or cause that dissolves the puzzle. A stance restated at constant altitude means the ending knows nothing the beginning didn't.
- Hunt the **missing antithesis** (Scott Alexander pass): write the strongest hostile-smart-reader objection; mark where one inline sentence would meet it, at the moment the reader thinks it — never a dedicated rebuttal section.
- The **ending** is one beat — figure or imperative, no summary. If the best closer died in revision, restore it.

## 2. Evidence (Housel/McKenzie pass)

- Receipts over adjectives: every load-bearing claim gets a number, named practice, or artifact. "The result is clear" is not evidence.
- Dosage: three bare examples prove "the list goes on"; six elaborated ones belabor it.
- Big claims don't ride in the trunk — a sweeping assertion in a closing paragraph gets defended, footnoted (PG move), or cut.
- Check borrowed anecdotes; living people get "seems," not "is."

## 3. Voice

- Heat is placed, not leaked (DHH places it; most drafts leak it). Every hot line: deliberate or accident?
- Figures at the joints only — thesis and coda (Forsyth). Two per essay is eloquence; four is performance.
- No self/employer plugs at the exit; cut or demote to a disclosed footnote.

## 4. Lines (Zinsser/Orwell sweep)

Comma splices (worst at the thesis) · broken list parallelism · clauses attached to the wrong noun · pronoun unity (each "you" in an "I" essay must be chosen) · repeated words and repeated paragraph-openers in close range · stacked hedges · edit residue (stranded fragments, orphaned "This," empty blocks).

## 5. Verdict

- Always one call: **ship** / **one pass away** (name the pass) / **structural rework** (name the gap).
- List what NOT to touch — protect good lines from the author's next sanding pass.
- Know when to say "stop sanding, publish" (Zinsser). Past a point the gains are in the next piece.

## Conduct

Quote what you criticize; propose the rewrite when the fix isn't obvious. Track repeat findings across drafts — third occurrence, call it a pattern, then drop it; the author decides. Scope choices are flagged as decisions, not errors.
