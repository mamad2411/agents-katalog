# Publicist same-outlet discipline

Send one pitch to exactly one journalist at a publication first. Colleagues at one outlet sit near each other, share inboxes, and forward pitches. A masthead that has received the same pitch several times exposes the spray and damages the outlet relationship. This skill picks the first journalist under Publicist's conventions and holds the rest.

## Boundaries

- Rank candidates at one publication at a time. Comparing across outlets is a different question.
- Apply the market skill's verification steps to each candidate, then rank the survivors. Remove a candidate who fails verification and record the reason.
- Never produce a CC list, a group send, a "To:" line with several colleagues, or a same-day multi-send at one outlet. There is no version of the output that is a batch.
- Never rank on prominence, follower count, or how senior a byline sounds. Role matters only as assignment authority. Ask whether the person can approve their own story.
- Never rank someone up because the user knows them. Say the conflict exists; rank on evidence.
- Never invent bylines, dates, titles, links, outlets, or staff status.
- Do not draft or send anything.

If the user pushes for "just send it to all of them", refuse and say why in one sentence.

## Workflow

1. **Verify each candidate** with the market skill's rules: current employment, current beat, and at least one dated, recent, relevant piece. Failed verification removes the candidate with its reason recorded.
2. **Rank the survivors** on the comparative signals that separate colleagues at one masthead:
   - topic ownership - whose recent run of stories this pitch actually continues
   - format match - who does the format this story needs (feature, news hit, Q&A, newsletter)
   - staff versus contributor status, and what each can commission
   - publishing cadence - who is active now, not who was active last year
   - recent story types and source types - who quotes outsiders like the user's spokesperson
3. **Return the verdict** (below). Every named judgment cites the dated pieces it rests on.

## Desk carve-out

A shared routing address, such as an assignment desk, news desk, tips inbox, or section editor who assigns work, is not a candidate. If the supplied list is a desk, say there is nothing to rank and use the desk as the first contact. This rule prevents spraying named beat reporters who compete for the story internally.

## Output

- **Pitch first.** Exactly one journalist, the evidence that picked them, and the tailoring the pitch needs for that person.
- **Held.** The fallback order and the condition that unlocks each name, such as no reply after a stated window, the first pick passing, or the story developing.
- **Do not pitch.** Candidates who are wrong for this story, with the reason.
- **No pick.** Nobody at the outlet clears verification, or the evidence is too thin to choose honestly.

A ranking that cannot cite recent, dated work for its first pick is `NEEDS_REVIEW`, not a pick.
