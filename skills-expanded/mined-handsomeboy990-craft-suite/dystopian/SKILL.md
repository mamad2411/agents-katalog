# Dystopian

A dystopia describes a system that works. If it did not work, it would not
have lasted, and there would be no novel.

## 1. Reading contract

The reader demands: a coherent, effective system, credible acceptance by the
population, a character who sees what others no longer see, and a high price
for that lucidity.

## 2. The law that it works

A dystopian system must deliver something real: safety, abundance, order,
equality, freedom from anxiety. Without a benefit, nobody would accept it, and
the critique lands on nothing.

Write first what the system solves, then what it costs.

## 3. Mechanics of control

Four levers, combined:

1. Material: rationing, housing, mobility, health.
2. Informational: language, archives, measurement, statistics.
3. Social: denunciation, merit, visible hierarchy, belonging.
4. Intimate: family, sexuality, parentage, mourning.

The intimate lever is the most effective in fiction and the least often
handled.

Control need not be total. Zones of tolerance act as a valve and make the
system more credible than perfect surveillance would.

## 4. The language of power

- Official vocabulary replaces uncomfortable words with neutral administrative
  terms.
- Characters use that vocabulary without irony, which is what produces the
  effect.
- The narrator does not translate: the reader is left to see the gap.
- One well-chosen official phrase is worth more than ten neologisms.

## 5. The lucid character

- They do not begin in revolt. They begin compliant, and the crack comes from
  a detail, often an intimate one.
- Their lucidity must cost them before it serves them.
- They are not right about everything: their solutions can be worse.
- Collective revolt, if it comes, obeys a logistics: food, communication,
  money, betrayal.

## 6. Endings

Three possible endings, all legitimate:

- The system absorbs the revolt and strengthens.
- The character escapes individually, without changing the system.
- The system falls, and the narrative shows the cost of the fall and the
  disorder after it.

Forbidden: the system falling in one scene, with no logistics and no
consequence.

## 7. Cliches to turn or prohibit

- The single visible, hateable leader.
- The already-organised resistance that was waiting for the protagonist.
- The chosen one who triggers the revolution by existing.
- Total uniformity of the population.
- The final speech that explains the novel's thesis.

## 8. Exit checks

- The system's real benefit is shown before its critique.
- At least one sympathetic character sincerely believes in it.
- Control operates through all four levers, the intimate included.
- The protagonist's lucidity costs them before it profits them.
- No collapse of the system without logistics.

## 9. Auto-critique

The eleven constitution axes, plus four genre axes: credibility of the system,
sincerity of the acceptance, the intimate control embodied, absence of a
stated thesis.

Threshold: no axis below 3, average at least 4 on credibility of the system.
