# Panel Discussion / Interview / Fireside Chat

## Scope

Any session with multiple speakers in conversation:
- Panel discussion (3-5 panelists + moderator)
- Fireside chat (interviewer + 1-2 guests)
- Podcast recording (host + guest)
- Moderated Q&A (expert panel fielding audience questions)
- Internal roundtable discussion

## What Makes This Format Different

- **No slides** (usually) — the conversation IS the content
- **Moderator is critical** — bad moderation ruins good panelists
- **Unpredictable** — you can prepare but not script
- **Chemistry matters** — panelist selection is 80% of the work
- **Audience engagement** — they're watching a conversation, not a lecture

## Planning Process

1. **Define the topic and angle** — not too broad, not too narrow
2. **Select panelists** — diversity of perspective, complementary expertise
3. **Prepare the moderator** — questions, flow, time management
4. **Brief panelists** — expectations, format, key questions
5. **Design audience interaction** — when/how do they participate?

## Roles

### Moderator Responsibilities
- Keep time (firmly but gracefully)
- Ensure all panelists get air time (some will dominate)
- Ask follow-up questions that go deeper
- Bridge between topics with smooth transitions
- Handle audience questions (filter, rephrase, direct)
- Cut off tangents ("fascinating — let's come back to that. But first...")
- Open and close the session

### Panelist Guidelines
- Keep answers to 1-2 minutes (moderator should enforce)
- Share specific stories/examples, not generalities
- Disagree respectfully (disagreement is GOOD for panels)
- Build on what others said ("to add to what X mentioned...")
- Avoid re-introducing yourself before every answer

## Session Structure

### Opening (5 min)
- Moderator sets context (why this topic, why now)
- Quick panelist intros (30 sec each — OR moderator introduces them)
- Ground rules ("we'll take audience Q&A in the last 15 min")

### Conversation Rounds (main body)
- **Round 1: The Setup** — broad opening question (everyone answers)
- **Round 2-4: Deep Dives** — specific questions, 2-3 panelists respond
- **Round 5: Spicy Question** — the controversial or forward-looking one
- **Audience Q&A** — last 15-20 min

### Closing (3 min)
- "Lightning round: one sentence each — what's your key advice?"
- Thank panelists
- Point to resources/follow-ups

## Best Practices

### Moderator Craft
- **Never ask yes/no questions** — use "how", "why", "tell me about a time when"
- **Don't answer your own questions** — you're the facilitator, not a panelist
- **Name who should answer** — don't let the loudest person always jump in first
- **Rotate the starting order** — don't always start with the same panelist
- **Listen actively** — best follow-ups come from actually hearing the answer
- **Have backup questions** — if a topic dies, pivot smoothly
- **Watch the clock** — sacrifice questions, not the ending

### Question Design
- **Layered questions** — start broad, get specific
  - "What's the biggest challenge?" → "How did you solve it specifically?" → "What would you do differently?"
- **Experience-based** — "tell me about a time when..." beats "what do you think about..."
- **Productive tension** — "X, you disagree with Y on this. Why?"
- **Audience-relevant** — frame questions from the audience's perspective
- **Avoid leading questions** — don't telegraph the "right" answer

### Panelist Selection
- **Diverse perspectives** — different roles, companies, experiences
- **Complementary, not identical** — the CTO, the practitioner, the customer
- **At least one contrarian** — agreement is boring; respectful disagreement is gold
- **Good communicators** — domain expertise ≠ ability to articulate it conversationally
- **3-4 panelists ideal** — 5+ means not enough air time per person
- **Brief them separately** — share questions in advance, not the "right" answers

### Audience Engagement
- Collect questions throughout via chat/Slido (not just at the end)
- Moderator curates — don't take questions in order; group by theme
- Rephrase audience questions for clarity (and to save time)
- "Great question from [name]" — acknowledge the asker
- If no questions: have 3-5 prepared "audience questions" ready

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Panel of 5+ where everyone gets 2 minutes (too many panelists)
- ❌ Moderator talks more than panelists
- ❌ No prep call with panelists (they don't know the format or each other)
- ❌ Reading questions from a script without follow-ups (feels robotic)
- ❌ Letting one panelist dominate (others check out)
- ❌ All panelists agreeing on everything (boring)
- ❌ No audience interaction until the very end
- ❌ Going over time because "one more question"
- ❌ Panelists giving rehearsed marketing pitches instead of authentic answers

## Checklist

### 2 Weeks Before
- [ ] Panelists confirmed (3-4 ideal)
- [ ] Topic angle sharpened (not "AI" but "how to actually ship AI in prod")
- [ ] Moderator assigned and briefed
- [ ] Format agreed: duration, Q&A timing, recording?
- [ ] Prep call scheduled with all panelists

### 1 Week Before — Prep Call
- [ ] Share planned questions (5-8 main questions)
- [ ] Discuss format expectations (answer length, style)
- [ ] Identify areas of disagreement (gold!)
- [ ] Confirm logistics (where, when, tech setup)
- [ ] Agree on intro format (self-intro vs. moderator intro)

### Day Of
- [ ] Moderator has printed/visible question list
- [ ] Timer or timekeeper assigned
- [ ] Audience Q&A tool ready (Slido, chat)
- [ ] Backup questions prepared (3-5 extra)
- [ ] Recording setup verified
- [ ] Water for all panelists

### During — Moderator Reminders
- [ ] Start on time
- [ ] Set ground rules in the first minute
- [ ] Rotate who answers first
- [ ] Follow up on interesting answers ("tell me more about...")
- [ ] Check audience questions mid-session
- [ ] Watch the clock — protect Q&A time
- [ ] Lightning round at the end

## Podcast/Interview Specifics

- More intimate — lean into personal stories
- Longer format OK (30-90 min)
- Less structured — follow the interesting threads
- Edit later — you can remove tangents in post
- "Yes, and..." technique — build on what the guest says
- Research the guest deeply — reference their specific work/tweets/articles
- End with: "where can people find you?" (give them a plug)
