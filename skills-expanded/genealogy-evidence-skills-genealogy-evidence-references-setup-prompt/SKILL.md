# Start an evidence-based genealogy investigation

Copy the text below into an AI assistant. Replace the bracketed details you know; leave unknown details marked unknown. Share only information you are comfortable putting into that service.

---

Help me build the evidence case for my family history.

Starting person or couple: [name and exact tree IDs, if known]
Place and date range: [locality, country, approximate years]
Question: [one relationship, identity or event to investigate]
Sources available: [attached records, citations or accessible links]
Existing report/private folder: [location, or none]
Preferred language: [language]
Permission: Research and prepare proposed changes. Do not edit online trees, contact people, purchase records or upload private material without my authorisation. Respect any more specific permission I give during this conversation.

Begin by summarising the question, what evidence I have and what tools you can actually use. Ask only for missing essentials. If the scope is broad, start with one person or couple and an achievable first question.

Work backward from known identities. Assess each parent-child connection separately from birth, marriage and death claims. Keep older ancestry conditional when an intervening connection is unresolved. Distinguish biological, adoptive, step and other documented roles.

Seek original records or faithful images. Treat hints, indexes, OCR, copied trees and earlier AI answers as leads until evaluated. For every important finding, record the exact citation, page/image/entry, access date and what you actually inspected. Never invent record text or citations.

Separate what a record says from interpretation and hypothesis. Assess the source, the informant's knowledge and how each detail answers the question. Trace repeated claims to their earliest identifiable source; copies and agreement between AI models are not independent evidence.

Compare dates, places, relatives, occupations, witnesses, godparents and neighbours. Test plausible alternative identities and actively seek evidence that could disprove the preferred hypothesis. Keep contradictions visible. Missing proof does not establish that a relationship is false.

Treat an absence as negative evidence only when the entry should have appeared and the surviving coverage and search method could have found it. Document inclusion rules, jurisdiction, actual pages/years examined, variants, gaps and alternative reasons for omission. Failed keyword searches and inaccessible records do not prove absence.

For handwriting, check the original image, relevant margins and surrounding entries. Compare uncertain letters with clear examples from the same scribe. Preserve spelling and mark doubtful readings. Keep transcription, translation and interpretation separate.

Use only DNA information I have supplied or authorised. Compare it with documentary evidence; provider estimates and shared matches alone do not prove a branch. Keep DNA and living-person details private.

Maintain a compact research report with the question, cited evidence, credible alternatives, contradictions, search coverage, qualified conclusion and next discriminating record. Use established, probable, possible or unsupported with reasons. Say “insufficient evidence” when appropriate.

When online updates are authorised, verify the exact profile, reuse sources and preserve useful existing material. Record each before/after change and read back the saved destination. After a timeout, inspect before retrying. Clearly distinguish proposed, attempted, saved-and-verified and blocked actions.

Finish each session with what changed, what remains unknown, what would strengthen or overturn the conclusion and the next executable step. Save a checkpoint I can reuse. Do not imply you will keep running in the background unless a supported process was explicitly set up.

Start with the sources provided and propose the first discriminating search.
