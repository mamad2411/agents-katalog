# Cold Email Sequencing for Consultants

### When to use
When you have a validated ICP and a list of 50+ targets, and you're ready to convert them into discovery calls. Cold email alone gets ignored. Sequencing (4 touches over 2 weeks) increases response rates from ~5% to 25%+ when done right.

### The framework
1. **Touch 1 (Day 1): Hyper-personalized opener.** 3–4 sentences max. One specific piece of research about them or their company (recent hire, funding round, content they published). A hook about a problem you solve. No CTA, just "did you notice this." Example: "Saw you promoted three designers last quarter. Most creative teams hit chaos around that growth stage, do you run into the same?"
2. **Touch 2 (Day 5): Value-first follow-up, not a replay of Touch 1.** Share a relevant insight, case study, or framework they'll find interesting regardless of whether they become a client. Casually mention your work (not as the focus). Example: "Thought this resource might be useful, I collected how 20+ design-led companies structure their hiring."
3. **Touch 3 (Day 10): Light social proof.** Mention a specific result you've helped similar teams achieve. Keep it short. Restate your core promise in one sentence. Example: "One of your competitors went from 2-week timelines to 4-day turnaround. Worth a chat if you're in the same boat."
4. **Touch 4 (Day 15): Permission to drop the sequence.** Acknowledge the silence gracefully. "Sounds like this isn't a fit right now, I get it. If priorities shift, my inbox is open." This moves you from "annoying spammer" to "professional you'd take a call from later."

### How to apply it
You're a hiring consultant for tech companies. Your ICP: Chief People Officers at 30–200-person software companies. You've built a list of 100 CPOs.

Day 1, Touch 1: "Hi Sarah, saw your company raised Series B last month. Most teams struggle to scale hiring from 10 to 40 people without culture rot. Have you run into that pressure?"

Day 5, Touch 2: "Sarah, I put together a guide on how 12 Series B companies structured their hiring process during hypergrowth. Thought it might be useful. [Link]"

Day 10, Touch 3: "One founder told me their biggest regret in scaling was hiring too fast and skipping reference calls. His team went from 35–70 people in 8 months and had a 40% first-year churn. Worth a 15-min call to see if you're navigating similar tradeoffs?"

Day 15, Touch 4: "Sarah, no pressure. If hiring challenges come up later, I'm here."

Rules for cadence: Don't send two touches within 24 hours (looks desperate). Avoid daily follow-ups after Touch 1. Vary your message-to-message substance (don't just replay the same reason). If she replies after Touch 1 with interest, move straight to booking. If she says "not interested," respect it and drop the sequence.

Stop condition: If you hit Touch 4 with no response, move on. You've delivered value and made your offer. Send only one more touch 30+ days later if something genuinely new happened (they just hired a new exec, hit a milestone, etc.).

### Common traps
- **Sending the same message four times.** "Hi [Name], here's my offer" on Day 1, then "Hi [Name], just checking in" on Day 5 looks like you have nothing to say. Each touch must serve a different purpose: Hook → Value → Proof → Release.
- **Touching up too fast.** Two emails in a week annoys people. Stick to 5-day spacing; 7-day spacing if your list is cold.
- **Making the CTA too big.** On Touch 1-3, the CTA is "do you relate to this problem?" or "use this resource." Touch 4 is "if it's relevant, let's chat." Don't ask for the call until they've engaged.
- **Forgetting to personalize the opener.** A generic "found your company online" opener gets ignored. Your opener must show 30 seconds of research: a hire, a funding round, a piece of content, a specific result.
- **Stopping the sequence too early.** If no one replies to Touch 2, resist the urge to pivot. Run the full sequence on 30–50 people. You're testing whether the sequence works, not whether this one person likes you.
- **Ignoring warm vs. cold.** If you got a warm intro, use it in Touch 1. If you're cold, keep the message shorter and the proof stronger. Warm intros can go longer; cold ones must hook fast.
