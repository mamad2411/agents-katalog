# Twelve-Vector Account Expansion

### When to use
Within 30 days of closing an initial client project, systematically map the account for expansion opportunities. Most consultants deliver, wrap up, and disappear. Account expansion is structured internal networking inside an account you already own trust in: the highest-probability pipeline for repeat revenue.

### The framework
1. **Identify horizontal expansion (same buyer, new problems).** List 3–5 other initiatives or problems your champion manages that you could solve. Example: You fixed their sales process; they also struggle with customer retention, pricing, or onboarding. Schedule a discovery call with your original contact and ask: "What else is keeping you up at night?"
2. **Map vertical expansion (other levels or roles).** Who else in the organization would benefit from your work? In a 100-person company: salespeople, operations, finance, the CFO, the CEO, other department heads. Vertical expansion is your champion introducing you to peers at their level or to their boss.
3. **Uncover organizational expansion (sister companies, parent, divisions).** Is your client part of a larger group, holding company, or multi-brand org? If they have a parent company or sister divisions, your proof case might open doors there. Ask: "Are there other divisions or companies in your family I should talk to?"
4. **Discover initiative expansion (adjacent business problems).** What strategic initiatives is your client pursuing? Acquisition, expansion into a new market, product launch, cost reduction? Every major initiative needs advisory support. You're not pitching; you're asking: "What's your biggest strategic initiative right now?"
5. **Explore referral expansion (external connections).** Ask your champion: "Who would you refer me to outside your company: a peer, a vendor partner, a board member: who faces similar challenges?" One client introduces you to 3–5 warm prospects. That's referral leverage.
6. **Build a weekly or monthly touchpoint cadence.** Commit to one call per month with your champion (advisory, update, new-opportunity discussion). Visibility = mind share. Disappearing for 6 months means your next outreach feels like a sales call, not a partnership.

### How to apply it
You finished a project with James, VP of Sales at a mid-market SaaS company, where you redesigned their discovery process. 30 days post-project, you schedule a debrief call.

You ask: "What else would make your life easier?" James mentions their onboarding is a mess and churn is high. Horizontal expansion found: you can help there.

You ask: "Who else should I talk to?" He says the VP of Customer Success (vertical expansion) and the CMO (who owns pipeline quality). He also mentions his company is preparing for acquisition and they're evaluating their tech stack (initiative expansion). You ask: "Would it help if I sat in on some of those decisions?" He says maybe, and gives you an intro to the CFO.

You ask: "Are there other companies in the family I should know about?" The parent company owns three software brands; one is in a similar market. He sets up an intro to that company's VP.

Two months later, you've opened three new conversations from one successful project. The onboarding work is a $15k project; the acquisition advisory is a $25k retainer; the sister company is a $20k discovery. Total expansion pipeline from account leverage: $60k. You spent 4 hours on phone calls to uncover it.

### Common traps
- **Asking for too much too soon.** Proposing five expansion ideas in week 2 feels greedy. Ask one question per call: "What else would help?" Then listen. Opportunities emerge over 2–3 conversations, not one.
- **Disappearing after delivery.** Two weeks of silence kills momentum. Commit to a monthly or quarterly touchpoint before you leave the engagement. Calendar it now.
- **Treating expansion like a sales pitch.** You're genuinely asking what would help. If they say "nothing right now," accept it and move on. Pushy expansion attempts burn goodwill and burn referrals.
- **Overselling capability.** If your champion mentions an area outside your expertise, say so and refer them to a partner. Trust > revenue. One honest referral creates 10x more leverage than a fake expansion play.
- **Forgetting the economic buyer.** Horizontal and vertical expansion require NEW economic buyers (the CFO, the CEO, the other department head). Your original champion can't approve $20k+ in other departments' budgets. Get warm intros, not just names.
