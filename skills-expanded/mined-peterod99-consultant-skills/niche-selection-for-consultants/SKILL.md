# Niche Selection for Consultants

### When to use
You're deciding what type of client or problem to specialize in. You have some expertise already but aren't sure whether to niche down or stay generalist. You've been approached by multiple buyer types and don't know which to pursue first. You're starting your consulting practice and need to pick a wedge.

### The framework
1. **Inventory your existing proof.** List 3-5 past engagements or results you're most proud of. What industry were they in? What problem did you solve? Who was the buyer? Don't rate them by market size, rate them by how clearly you solved a specific, measurable problem.

2. **Map your buyer access.** Where do you already have relationships, reputation, or visibility? LinkedIn network, speaking history, past clients, referral sources, alumni networks, community standing. Niche selection without buyer access is fantasy.

3. **Identify the repeatable problem.** Across your proof examples and your access points, is there a single problem that appears 2+ times? Not "I help tech companies" but "I help Series B SaaS companies transition from founder-led sales to scalable sales ops." Specificity is the moat.

4. **Test the niche hypothesis.** Talk to 3-5 potential buyers in your target niche. Ask them about their version of the problem, how they currently solve it, what they'd pay for a better solution. If you can't get these conversations easily, your niche lacks buyer access.

5. **Commit to the niche for 18-24 months.** You will never be the obvious expert in something new if you pivot every 12 months. Pick a niche where your proof is already credible enough to win first clients, then build on that momentum.

### How to apply it
A fractional Chief Revenue Officer (CRO) has done three engagements: sales ops redesign for a 20-person SaaS company, sales training for a financial services firm, and pipeline review for a consulting agency. The SaaS engagement felt most energizing, they had strong founder-driven sales but needed processes to scale. The CRO's network includes 40 people in the "Series A-B SaaS" world from conference speaking and a Slack community. Three of those people have explicitly asked "Do you help with scaling sales ops?"

Niche hypothesis: "Sales operations scaling for Series A-B SaaS companies transitioning from founder-led to repeatable sales." The CRO calls 4 founders in the network. All four recognize the problem immediately and have a clear picture of what they'd pay ($50-150k for a 90-day engagement). Two offer to introduce the CRO to other founders.

The CRO commits: all outreach, speaking proposals, content, and case studies for 18 months focus on SaaS sales ops. Within 12 months, the CRO has moved 70% of revenue to SaaS clients and doubled the project fee.

### Common traps
- **Picking a niche without buyer access.** You've researched the market and it's a $500M TAM, but you don't know anyone in that vertical. You'll spend months prospecting cold. Pick a niche where you already know 20+ potential buyers.
- **Choosing a niche based on market size, not proof.** You've done one financial services engagement and want to specialize there because "finance is huge." Without 2-3 strong references and a repeatable methodology, you're just guessing. Prove your niche first, then scale it.
- **Niching too narrow too fast.** "I help CFOs at Series B SaaS companies with headcount planning." This is true but reduces your addressable market below sustainable. Niche down on the problem before you niche down on the buyer profile.
- **Staying too broad to seem safe.** You tell everyone "I help businesses improve sales" and end up competing on price with generalist consultants. Specificity is how you command premium fees. Being known for one thing beats being okay at ten.
