# Problem Validation

Test the problem before proposing a feature. A participant agreeing with your solution is not evidence that they faced the problem.

## Workflow

1. Write the problem hypothesis in the person's own context, the alternative explanation, what would disconfirm it, and a go, revise or stop decision rule **before** interviews. Name behavioural evidence required, not a target number of people who say they like the idea.
2. Define a sampling frame spanning roles, frequency, non-users and likely counterexamples. Record recruitment channels and selection bias; do not generalise from convenient friends or a narrow paying cohort to everyone. Decide what a small sample can and cannot establish.
3. Ask for consent to participate and to record notes; allow refusal or withdrawal. Ask about a recent specific occasion, what triggered it, what they did, time/money lost and workarounds used. Ask for contrary occasions and observe available non-sensitive artefacts where consent allows. Do not pitch the proposed solution, ask leading hypotheticals or collect identifiers or personal data into the research summary.
4. Deidentify summaries; keep only role/context and behaviour necessary for the decision. Distinguish witnessed or corroborated past action from recalled claim, willingness to pay backed by a past trade-off from enthusiasm, and unmet demand from a problem already solved by an acceptable workaround.
5. Compare patterns and counterexamples to the prewritten rule, stating sample limits and missing segments. If the evidence is thin or contradicts itself, return **insufficient evidence** with the next discriminating observation rather than a build recommendation. Only after the problem gate passes hand solution tests to `usability-test-plan`.

## Output

Write a decision memo with hypothesis, disconfirming tests, sampling limits, deidentified behavioural evidence and contrary cases, observed demand, go/revise/stop/insufficient-evidence decision and next test. Do not infer market size from interview frequency.

## Anti-patterns

**Showing the mockup first.** Feedback on a design tests the design, not whether the problem existed before the pitch.
