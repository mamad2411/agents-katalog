## Goal

Decline or renegotiate extra work while preserving trust, making tradeoffs visible, and keeping relationships intact.

## Preconditions

- A request for additional work, deadline, meeting, project, or responsibility.
- Knowledge of your current priorities, deadlines, and capacity.
- Enough authority to decline, defer, or ask your manager to prioritize.

## Steps

1. **Clarify the request.** Ask what outcome is needed, by when, why it matters, and who is requesting it. → *Expect:* you know the actual size and urgency.
2. **Check your capacity.** Compare the request against current commitments and deadlines. → *Expect:* your response is based on tradeoffs, not mood.
3. **Decide your response.** [BRANCH: decline | defer | trade] decline if it does not fit; defer if timing is the issue; trade if something else must move. → *Expect:* you have a constructive alternative.
4. **Use a clear script.** Say, "I cannot take this on by Friday without delaying the client report. I can help next Tuesday or review a draft for 20 minutes." → *Expect:* the no includes useful options.
5. **Name the tradeoff.** Explain which priority, deadline, or quality bar would be affected. → *Expect:* the requester sees the cost.
6. **Offer a narrower contribution.** Suggest a template, quick review, referral, later slot, or partial deliverable if appropriate. → *Expect:* the relationship gets support without full ownership.
7. **Escalate priority conflicts.** If the requester insists, ask your manager or decision owner to choose between competing commitments. → *Expect:* prioritization moves to the right authority.
8. **Confirm in writing.** Send a short recap of what you declined, deferred, or agreed to do instead. → *Expect:* expectations are documented.

## Decision points

- If the request comes from your manager → ask which current work should move.
- If the request is urgent but important → offer the smallest useful help and flag what will slip.
- If the requester is a peer → be respectful but clear that your committed work still has owners.
- If refusal could affect performance expectations → document the tradeoff and involve your manager.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 No sounds dismissive:** detect curt phrasing like "not my job" → restate priorities and offer a practical alternative.
- **F2 You accept by default:** detect resentment or overloaded calendar → reopen the conversation with the tradeoff.
- **F3 Priority conflict stays hidden:** detect multiple people expecting the same time → ask the decision owner to rank the work.

## Verification

The requester has a clear answer, the tradeoff is visible, any alternative help is bounded, and changed commitments are documented.

## Variations

- Senior stakeholder request: frame the issue around impact and priority, not personal busyness.
- Repeated extra work: discuss role scope and capacity with your manager.
- Team emergency: help with the urgent issue, then renegotiate deadlines afterward.

## Safety & privacy

Medium career risk because refusal can affect relationships and workload expectations. Keep tone factual and avoid blaming other people for your capacity.
