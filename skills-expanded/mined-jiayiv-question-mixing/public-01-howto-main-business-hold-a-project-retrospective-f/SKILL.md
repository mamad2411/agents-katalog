## Goal

Hold a project retrospective that produces a short set of improvement actions for future project work.

## Preconditions

- The project, milestone, sprint, or delivery period has ended.
- Participants have direct experience with the work.
- A facilitator and note location are chosen.

## Steps

1. **Define the retro scope.** Name the project period, milestone, or delivery event being reviewed. → *Expect:* participants discuss the same body of work.
2. **Prepare prompts.** Use prompts such as what worked, what did not, what surprised us, and what to change. → *Expect:* feedback is structured.
3. **Collect input silently.** Give participants time to write notes before discussion. → *Expect:* quieter participants can contribute.
4. **Group themes.** Combine similar points and separate facts from interpretations. → *Expect:* the discussion focuses on patterns.
5. **Discuss top themes.** Ask what caused each theme and what would improve future work. → *Expect:* the team moves from complaint to cause.
6. **Choose actions.** Select one to three improvements with owners and due dates. → *Expect:* actions are small enough to complete.
7. **Publish the summary.** Share themes, decisions, actions, owners, and follow-up date. → *Expect:* the project record includes the retrospective outcome.

## Decision points

- If blame appears → restate the issue as an observable process, decision, or handoff problem.
- If too many improvements are suggested → prioritize only the highest-impact one to three.
- If sensitive personnel issues arise → move them to the appropriate manager or HR channel.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 No actions:** detect the retro ends with discussion only → recover by choosing at least one owner and next step.
- **F2 Unsafe discussion:** detect personal attacks or retaliation concerns → recover by pausing, reframing, or switching to private follow-up.
- **F3 Actions forgotten:** detect prior retro actions still open → recover by reviewing them before adding new commitments.

## Verification

The retrospective summary contains themes, one to three improvement actions, owners, due dates, and a follow-up date.

## Variations

- Sprint retrospective: focus on process changes that fit the next sprint.
- Project closeout retrospective: include planning accuracy, stakeholder communication, and launch quality.
- Support operations retrospective: include queue signals, escalation quality, and customer impact.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Retrospectives may include sensitive performance or conflict details, so publish summaries rather than raw comments when needed.
