## Goal

Cabbage is salted and fermented under brine into crisp, sour sauerkraut.

## Preconditions

- Fresh cabbage, non-iodized salt, scale, clean jar, weight, and a plate or tray to catch overflow.
- Ability to keep the jar at cool room temperature, away from direct sun.

## Steps

1. **Weigh cabbage.** Remove dirty outer leaves, core the cabbage, and weigh the shredded edible cabbage. → *Expect:* a known cabbage weight in grams.
2. **Salt at 2 percent.** Add 20 g salt per 1000 g cabbage and toss thoroughly. → *Expect:* salt is evenly distributed and cabbage begins to look glossy.
3. **Massage for brine.** Squeeze and knead 5-10 minutes until liquid pools in the bowl. → *Expect:* cabbage bends without snapping and releases enough brine to drip from a handful.
4. **Pack the jar.** Press cabbage tightly into a clean jar, leaving at least 3 cm headspace. → *Expect:* no large air pockets and brine rises above the cabbage.
5. **Submerge everything.** Place a weight or clean folded cabbage leaf on top so solids stay below brine. → *Expect:* cabbage is fully covered by brine.
6. **Ferment at room temperature.** Cover loosely or use an airlock and set the jar on a tray for 5-21 days. [BRANCH: mild | sour] → *Expect:* bubbles appear after 1-3 days and aroma turns pleasantly sour.
7. **Burp if sealed.** Open a tight lid daily to release gas. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* pressure can crack glass; do not let a sealed jar build pressure. → *Expect:* gas escapes and brine remains over the cabbage.
8. **Taste and refrigerate.** When sour enough, remove any harmless surface kahm yeast, cap, and refrigerate. → *Expect:* kraut is pale, crisp, salty-sour, and cold storage slows fermentation.

## Decision points

- Brine does not cover cabbage after packing → add 2 percent salt brine, not plain water.
- Temperature is above 24 °C → move cooler because soft texture and yeast grow faster.
- White film appears on the surface → skim if it is flat and yeasty; discard if fuzzy mold or colored growth appears.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Mold growth:** detect fuzzy blue, green, black, or pink patches → discard the batch; mold roots can spread below the surface.
- **F2 Slimy kraut:** detect ropy brine or mushy strands → discard and restart with measured salt and cooler fermentation.
- **F3 Dry top:** detect cabbage exposed to air → push down, add 2 percent brine, and use a better weight if no mold is present.
- **F4 Too salty:** detect harsh salt after fermentation → rinse portions before serving and use the 2 percent ratio next batch.

## Verification

Sauerkraut is fully submerged during fermentation, tastes cleanly sour, remains crisp, and has no fuzzy mold, pink color, rotten smell, or slimy brine.

## Variations

- `caraway`: add 1 tsp caraway seed per medium cabbage.
- `red-cabbage`: ferment the same way; color turns vivid magenta.
- `quick-small-batch`: use a 500 ml jar and begin tasting on day 4.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from fermentation errors and glass pressure. Use measured salt, keep vegetables under brine, vent sealed jars, and discard any moldy, slimy, putrid, or pink batch.
