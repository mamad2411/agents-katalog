## Goal

Food is deep-fried in a pot with controlled oil temperature, safe loading, and clear fire response.

## Preconditions

- Heavy pot, high-smoke-point oil, thermometer, spider or tongs, dry food, sheet tray, paper towels or rack, and a lid or baking sheet that can cover the pot.
- Working stove area, no water near hot oil, and a Class B or kitchen-rated fire extinguisher if available.

## Steps

1. **Choose the pot.** Use a heavy pot and fill oil no more than halfway. → *Expect:* at least 8 cm empty space remains above the oil.
2. **Dry the food.** Pat food dry and shake off excess marinade, ice, or loose flour. → *Expect:* surface is damp at most, with no dripping water.
3. **Set up landing zone.** Place a rack or towels on a tray beside the stove. → *Expect:* cooked food has a clear destination before oil is hot.
4. **Heat oil slowly.** Warm oil to the recipe temperature, usually 165-190 °C. → *Expect:* thermometer rises steadily and oil shimmers without smoking.
5. **Load small batches.** Lower food away from your body with tongs or a spider. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* dropped food can splash hot oil; lower pieces gently and do not crowd. → *Expect:* bubbling surrounds food and oil level stays safely below the rim.
6. **Control temperature.** Adjust heat to keep oil in range. [BRANCH: oil too cool | oil too hot] → *Expect:* food bubbles actively without smoking.
7. **Cook to doneness.** Fry until exterior is browned and interior reaches its safe temperature, such as chicken at 74 °C. → *Expect:* coating is crisp and meat juices run clear when applicable.
8. **Drain and season.** Lift food out, let oil drip back, then move to rack and salt while hot. → *Expect:* food crackles lightly and excess oil drains away.
9. **Cool oil before handling.** Turn off heat and leave oil uncovered until fully cool. → *Expect:* oil is room temperature before straining or disposal.

## Decision points

- Oil smokes → turn off heat, move pot off burner if safe, and let it cool below 190 °C.
- Oil catches fire → turn off heat if reachable, cover with lid, use extinguisher if needed, and never use water.
- Food browns before inside is cooked → lower oil temperature and finish in an oven if necessary.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Oil fire:** detect flames from pot → cover with metal lid or baking sheet, turn off heat, use a rated extinguisher, and call emergency services if not out immediately.
- **F2 Violent splatter:** detect popping and oil jumping → step back, lower heat, and dry or thaw food better before continuing.
- **F3 Greasy food:** detect pale oily coating → increase oil temperature and fry smaller batches.
- **F4 Raw center:** detect unsafe internal temperature → return to oil or finish in oven until thermometer confirms doneness.

## Verification

Oil stayed below smoke point, food is crisp and fully cooked to safe internal temperature, no water contacted hot oil, and cooled oil was handled only after reaching room temperature.

## Variations

- `shallow-fry`: use less oil but keep the same no-water and fire rules.
- `double-fry`: fry once at lower heat to cook through, rest, then fry hotter to crisp.
- `electric-fryer`: follow fill lines and thermostat settings, but still keep a lid or extinguisher nearby.

## Safety & privacy

High risk from burns and oil fires. Never leave hot oil unattended, never pour water on burning oil, keep children and clutter away, and cool oil completely before disposal.
