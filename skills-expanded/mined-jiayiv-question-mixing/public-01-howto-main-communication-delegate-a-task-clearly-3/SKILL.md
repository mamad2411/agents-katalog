## Goal

Delegate a task so the recipient understands the outcome, authority, constraints, deadline, and check-in rhythm.

## Preconditions

- A task that can be owned by someone else.
- Clarity on the desired outcome and any quality, budget, or approval constraints.
- A recipient with enough context, time, and authority or a plan to provide them.

## Steps

1. **Define the outcome.** Write the desired end state in one sentence, such as "Create a two-page vendor comparison for Friday's decision." → *Expect:* the task is described by result, not activity.
2. **Explain the context.** State why the task matters, who will use the output, and what decision it supports. → *Expect:* the recipient can make sensible tradeoffs.
3. **Set boundaries.** Clarify budget, tools, people to consult, quality bar, and decisions they can make alone. → *Expect:* authority and limits are explicit.
4. **Agree on deadline and checkpoints.** [BRANCH: routine task | high-risk task] use a final deadline for routine work; add interim checkpoints for high-risk or unfamiliar work. → *Expect:* progress will surface before it is too late.
5. **Provide resources.** Share files, examples, contacts, and prior decisions. → *Expect:* the recipient can start without hunting for basics.
6. **Ask for playback.** Say, "Can you tell me what you understand the deliverable and deadline to be?" → *Expect:* mismatches are caught immediately.
7. **Document the handoff.** Send a short written note with outcome, owner, deadline, resources, and check-in plan. → *Expect:* both sides have the same record.
8. **Follow up without taking over.** Ask about blockers and decisions, not every detail of execution. → *Expect:* ownership stays with the delegatee.

## Decision points

- If the task is developmental → explain the learning goal as well as the deliverable.
- If the person lacks authority → either delegate authority or keep the task yourself.
- If the deadline is immovable → name the consequence and checkpoint earlier.
- If mistakes would be costly → define review points before external sharing.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Outcome unclear:** detect many clarifying questions or wrong first draft → restate the deliverable with an example.
- **F2 Micromanagement:** detect yourself approving every small choice → reset around constraints and checkpoints.
- **F3 Hidden blocker:** detect missed checkpoint or silence near deadline → ask what decision, access, or dependency is blocking progress.

## Verification

The delegated task has one owner, a written outcome, deadline, constraints, resources, authority level, and agreed checkpoint cadence.

## Variations

- Delegating upward: frame it as a decision or escalation request, not a command.
- Delegating to a peer: ask for agreement and tradeoffs because you may not control their priorities.
- Delegating to a contractor: include acceptance criteria and payment or approval terms.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk unless the task involves customers, money, legal commitments, or personnel data. Do not delegate access or approval authority casually.
