## Goal

Conduct a structured job interview that gathers role-relevant evidence and gives the candidate a respectful, consistent experience.

## Preconditions

- An approved role, candidate resume or profile, and interview stage.
- A scorecard or list of competencies to evaluate.
- Awareness of prohibited or inappropriate interview topics in your workplace and locale.

## Steps

1. **Review the scorecard.** Identify the competencies this interview owns, such as technical judgment, collaboration, leadership, or domain knowledge. → *Expect:* you know what evidence to collect.
2. **Prepare questions.** Write consistent behavioral or work-sample questions tied to the scorecard. → *Expect:* candidates are evaluated on comparable prompts.
3. **Open professionally.** Introduce yourself, confirm timing, explain the structure, and leave space for candidate questions. → *Expect:* the candidate knows what to expect.
4. **Ask for concrete examples.** Use prompts like "Tell me about a time you had to change a stakeholder's mind." → *Expect:* answers include situation, action, and result.
5. **Probe fairly.** Ask follow-ups about tradeoffs, ownership, conflict, measurement, and lessons learned. → *Expect:* you get evidence beyond rehearsed claims.
6. **Avoid protected or irrelevant topics.** [BRANCH: candidate volunteers personal information | no personal information] redirect to role requirements if personal information appears; continue with prepared questions otherwise. → *Expect:* the interview stays job-related.
7. **Leave time for questions.** Answer honestly about role, team, process, and next steps without overselling. → *Expect:* the candidate can evaluate fit.
8. **Submit notes promptly.** Record evidence against the scorecard before discussing with other interviewers. → *Expect:* feedback reflects your observations, not group influence.

## Decision points

- If the candidate is late or has technical trouble → follow the company's reschedule and fairness policy.
- If a question fails to produce evidence → ask for a specific example or move to a work-sample prompt.
- If bias language appears in debrief → redirect to job-related evidence.
- If the candidate asks about compensation or accommodations → answer according to company policy or route to the recruiter.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Interview becomes informal chat:** detect little scorecard evidence → return to prepared competencies and ask structured follow-ups.
- **F2 Illegal or inappropriate topic arises:** detect questions about age, family, health, religion, or protected status → stop, redirect, and consult recruiting or HR.
- **F3 Notes are conclusory:** detect statements like "not a culture fit" → replace with observable behaviors and examples.

## Verification

The submitted interview feedback includes role-relevant evidence for each assigned competency, a recommendation, and no prohibited personal information.

## Variations

- Panel interview: assign competencies before the interview to avoid duplicate questioning.
- Technical interview: use the same rubric and clarify whether hints are allowed.
- Internal candidate: protect confidentiality and avoid relying only on reputation.

## Safety & privacy

Medium legal and career risk. Keep candidate data confidential and follow employment law, accessibility, and company hiring policy.
