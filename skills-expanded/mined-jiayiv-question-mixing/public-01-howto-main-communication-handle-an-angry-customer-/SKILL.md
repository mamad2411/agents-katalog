## Goal

Respond to an angry customer in a way that lowers tension, captures the issue, sets realistic next steps, and protects the business relationship.

## Preconditions

- A customer complaint received by phone, email, chat, ticket, or in person.
- Access to account, order, service, or case information allowed by your role.
- Knowledge of refund, escalation, safety, and abuse policies.

## Steps

1. **Regulate your response.** Pause, use a steady tone, and avoid matching the customer's volume or sarcasm. → *Expect:* you can respond professionally.
2. **Acknowledge the emotion and issue.** Say, "I can hear how frustrating this has been, and I want to understand what happened." → *Expect:* the customer hears recognition before problem-solving.
3. **Gather facts.** Ask for order number, date, product, impact, prior contacts, and desired resolution. → *Expect:* the complaint becomes specific.
4. **Confirm understanding.** Summarize: "You were charged twice on August 12 and still cannot access the account." → *Expect:* the customer can correct or confirm the facts.
5. **State what you can do now.** [BRANCH: authorized resolution | escalation needed] offer the approved fix if authorized; explain escalation path, owner, and timeline if not. → *Expect:* next steps are realistic.
6. **Set boundaries if needed.** Say, "I want to help, but I cannot continue if you insult or threaten staff." → *Expect:* abusive behavior has a clear limit.
7. **Document the interaction.** Record facts, promises, policy references, and emotional or safety concerns in the case system. → *Expect:* the next person has context.
8. **Follow up when promised.** Send a recap with case number, resolution path, deadline, and contact channel. → *Expect:* the customer has a written record.

## Decision points

- If the customer threatens harm or violence → follow emergency or safety escalation policy immediately.
- If the customer requests compensation → follow approved refund, credit, or goodwill limits.
- If the company caused the problem → apologize plainly and avoid defensive explanations.
- If the customer is wrong about policy → explain the policy and offer the closest available alternative.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Argument loop:** detect repeated blame with no new facts → restate the issue and offer the next concrete step.
- **F2 Unauthorized promise:** detect yourself about to guarantee money, timing, or outcome beyond policy → pause and say you need to verify options.
- **F3 Abuse continues:** detect insults, threats, or harassment after a warning → end or transfer the interaction according to policy.

## Verification

The case record contains the customer's issue, confirmed facts, offered resolution or escalation, promised timeline, and any boundary or safety notes.

## Variations

- Phone call: speak slowly and summarize before ending.
- Email or ticket: quote only the relevant issue and use clear subject lines.
- Enterprise customer: include account owner or customer success manager in escalation.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because money, identity, safety, or legal claims may be involved. Verify identity before discussing account details and do not promise exceptions beyond authority.
