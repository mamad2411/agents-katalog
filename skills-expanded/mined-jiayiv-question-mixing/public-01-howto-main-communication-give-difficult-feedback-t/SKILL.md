## Goal

Give difficult feedback to a direct report in a way that is specific, fair, documented, and tied to future expectations.

## Preconditions

- You manage or formally lead the person receiving feedback.
- You have specific examples of behavior, work product, impact, and prior context.
- You know whether HR, performance management, or legal guidance is required.

## Steps

1. **Prepare examples.** Write observable facts, dates, work artifacts, and impacts, avoiding labels like "lazy" or "negative." → *Expect:* feedback is evidence-based.
2. **Check severity.** Decide whether this is coaching, formal performance feedback, policy issue, or urgent risk. → *Expect:* you know whether HR or another process is needed.
3. **Schedule privately.** Set a private meeting soon after the relevant event, with enough time for discussion. → *Expect:* the report is not surprised in public.
4. **State the issue directly.** Say, "I need to discuss a pattern in missed handoff deadlines and its impact on the launch team." → *Expect:* the topic is clear early.
5. **Describe behavior and impact.** Use "When X happened, Y was affected" and pause for their perspective. → *Expect:* the report understands the gap.
6. **Listen for context.** [BRANCH: skill gap | motivation issue | external blocker] offer training for skill gaps; clarify expectations for motivation issues; remove or escalate blockers where real. → *Expect:* response matches cause.
7. **Set future expectations.** Define what good looks like, by when, and how progress will be checked. → *Expect:* the report knows how to recover.
8. **Document the conversation.** Send a concise recap with examples, expectations, support offered, and follow-up date. → *Expect:* both sides have a fair record.

## Decision points

- If the issue involves harassment, discrimination, safety, fraud, or retaliation → involve HR or the required formal process immediately.
- If this is the first minor issue → use coaching language and keep the focus developmental.
- If the pattern is repeated → document prior feedback and set sharper expectations.
- If the employee raises a health, disability, or protected issue → pause and involve HR for appropriate process.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Feedback is too vague:** detect words like "professionalism" without examples → add specific behavior, date, and impact.
- **F2 Conversation becomes punitive too early:** detect no chance for context → ask what happened and what support is needed before deciding next steps.
- **F3 No written follow-up:** detect only verbal feedback after a serious issue → send a factual recap promptly.

## Verification

The report receives specific examples, impact, expected future behavior, support offered, follow-up date, and written recap appropriate to the issue's severity.

## Variations

- Remote report: use video for sensitive feedback and written recap afterward.
- New manager: check prior commitments before treating a pattern as new.
- Formal performance plan: coordinate with HR and use approved language.

## Safety & privacy

Medium employment and legal risk. Keep personnel details confidential, avoid discriminatory assumptions, and use HR guidance for protected or severe issues.
