## Goal

Pitch an idea to your boss with a clear problem, business value, costs, risks, and specific decision request.

## Preconditions

- An idea relevant to team goals, customer outcomes, revenue, cost, risk, quality, or employee experience.
- Basic evidence that the problem exists.
- Awareness of your boss's priorities and decision authority.

## Steps

1. **Define the problem.** Write one sentence naming who is affected, what is happening, and why it matters. → *Expect:* the idea starts from a real need.
2. **Describe the proposal.** State the idea in plain language and avoid solution jargon. → *Expect:* your boss can repeat the idea after one read.
3. **Estimate value and effort.** Include expected benefit, required people, cost, timeline, and tradeoffs. → *Expect:* the pitch feels practical.
4. **Address risks.** Name likely objections such as budget, priority, adoption, compliance, or maintenance. → *Expect:* concerns are anticipated rather than ignored.
5. **Ask for a specific decision.** Use "I'm asking for approval to spend two weeks validating this with three customers." → *Expect:* the next action is clear.
6. **Choose the format.** [BRANCH: quick idea | significant investment] use a short message for a small test; use a one-page brief and meeting for significant investment. → *Expect:* pitch size matches decision size.
7. **Invite critique.** Ask "What would need to be true for this to be worth trying?" → *Expect:* feedback improves the proposal.
8. **Follow up in writing.** Send the decision, open questions, and next step after the conversation. → *Expect:* momentum does not depend on memory.

## Decision points

- If the idea conflicts with current priorities → frame it as a tradeoff and ask what should be deprioritized.
- If evidence is weak → ask for permission to validate before asking for full approval.
- If your boss is not the decision-maker → ask who must be included and what criteria they use.
- If the idea affects another team → talk to that team before presenting it as settled.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Pitch is solution-first:** detect no clear problem statement → rewrite around pain, impact, and affected people.
- **F2 Ask is vague:** detect "What do you think?" as the only close → replace with a specific approval, feedback, or introduction request.
- **F3 Costs are hidden:** detect missing effort or tradeoffs → add time, budget, owner, and opportunity cost.

## Verification

Your boss receives a concise pitch that states problem, proposal, value, cost, risks, and a specific requested decision or next step.

## Variations

- Innovation idea: pitch a small experiment before a major program.
- Process improvement: include current-state evidence and expected time saved.
- Career growth idea: connect the proposal to team outcomes, not only personal interest.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but strategy, customer data, or budget information may be sensitive. Do not present another team's work as approved without their agreement.
