## Goal

Respond to a recruiter clearly, whether you are interested, not interested, or need more information.

## Preconditions

- A recruiter message by email, LinkedIn, phone, or another professional channel.
- Basic ability to identify the recruiter, company, role, and whether the message appears legitimate.
- A decision about your level of interest or the information you need.

## Steps

1. **Check legitimacy.** Verify the recruiter's name, company, email domain, profile, and whether the role seems real. → *Expect:* obvious scams or impersonation are filtered out.
2. **Decide your stance.** [BRANCH: interested | unsure | not interested] choose whether to pursue, ask questions, or decline. → *Expect:* the reply has a clear purpose.
3. **Reply with the key signal.** For interest, write "I'm open to learning more"; for decline, write "I'm not looking right now." → *Expect:* the recruiter knows your position.
4. **Ask for essentials if interested.** Request role scope, company, location or remote policy, compensation range, interview process, and visa or work authorization constraints if relevant. → *Expect:* you can screen fit before a call.
5. **Offer availability.** Provide 2-3 time windows and timezone if you want a call. → *Expect:* scheduling can happen without back-and-forth.
6. **Set boundaries.** Say what roles, locations, salary range, or timing you will consider. → *Expect:* future outreach is better targeted.
7. **Protect sensitive information.** Do not send SSN, bank details, copies of ID, or confidential work samples during initial outreach. → *Expect:* your response shares only professional information.
8. **Track the conversation.** Save the role, recruiter, date, and next step in a job-search tracker or notes. → *Expect:* you can follow up without confusion.

## Decision points

- If the message asks for money, identity documents, or unusual downloads → treat it as suspicious and do not proceed.
- If compensation is far below your range → decline or state your range before scheduling.
- If you are employed and discreetly searching → use personal email and avoid company devices.
- If you are not interested but like the company → decline politely and invite future contact for matching roles.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Scam signal:** detect personal email mismatch, upfront fees, or identity requests → stop responding and report through the platform if appropriate.
- **F2 Unclear role:** detect missing company, scope, or compensation → ask for essentials before booking time.
- **F3 Oversharing:** detect confidential employer details in your draft → remove them and keep the reply high level.

## Verification

Your reply clearly states interest level, asks for or provides the next step, protects sensitive information, and records the recruiter and role for follow-up.

## Variations

- Passive candidate: ask for compensation and scope before agreeing to a call.
- Active search: include resume or portfolio link if appropriate.
- Referral request: ask whether they can share a formal application link.

## Safety & privacy

Low to medium risk because job scams and employment privacy are common. Use personal contact details and never share financial or identity documents with an unverified recruiter.
