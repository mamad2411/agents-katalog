## Goal

Run a focused brainstorming session that produces usable options, not just a lively conversation.

## Preconditions

- A defined problem, decision owner, and time box.
- Participants who represent relevant functions or users.
- A shared board, doc, or room setup for capturing ideas.

## Steps

1. **Define the prompt.** Write a narrow question such as "How might we reduce onboarding time from 10 days to 5 without adding headcount?" → *Expect:* participants know the problem and constraint.
2. **Invite the right mix.** Include people with user knowledge, delivery knowledge, and decision authority, but keep the group small enough to participate. → *Expect:* the room has useful perspectives without becoming a crowd.
3. **Set constraints.** State time box, decision owner, budget limits, and what will happen after the session. → *Expect:* ideas are grounded in reality.
4. **Generate silently first.** Give 5-10 minutes for individual idea writing before discussion. → *Expect:* quieter participants contribute before anchoring starts.
5. **Share ideas round-robin.** Ask each person to add one idea at a time without debate. → *Expect:* ideas are captured before evaluation.
6. **Cluster themes.** Group similar ideas and name each cluster in plain language. → *Expect:* the team can see patterns instead of a long raw list.
7. **Select next experiments.** [BRANCH: vote | decision owner chooses] vote when input matters most; have the decision owner choose when accountability matters most. → *Expect:* the strongest options move forward.
8. **Close with owners.** Assign an owner, next step, and decision date for each selected idea. → *Expect:* the brainstorm turns into action.

## Decision points

- If the goal is alignment rather than ideas → run a decision meeting instead.
- If senior voices dominate → switch to silent writing and anonymous voting.
- If ideas are too abstract → ask for examples that could be tried within two weeks.
- If no option is feasible → capture constraints and schedule a smaller problem-framing session.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Prompt is too broad:** detect unrelated ideas → rewrite as "How might we achieve X for Y under Z constraint?"
- **F2 Groupthink appears:** detect everyone building on one early idea → pause and ask for opposite or risky options.
- **F3 No follow-through:** detect selected ideas without owners → assign one owner for each next experiment.

## Verification

The session ends with captured ideas, grouped themes, 2-5 selected options, named owners, and a date for deciding or testing next steps.

## Variations

- Remote session: use a shared board and silent timers.
- Large group: split into rooms and reconvene for synthesis.
- Sensitive topic: collect anonymous input before discussion.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but ideas may include confidential strategy or employee feedback. Clarify who can see the notes before collecting candid input.
