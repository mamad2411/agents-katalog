## Goal

Mediate a workplace conflict so coworkers can agree on facts, impacts, boundaries, and next working agreements.

## Preconditions

- Both coworkers are willing to participate or a manager has asked for facilitated discussion.
- The issue is workplace-related and not an active safety, harassment, discrimination, or legal investigation.
- You can stay neutral or have authority to facilitate fairly.

## Steps

1. **Screen for escalation needs.** Check whether the conflict involves threats, harassment, discrimination, retaliation, or policy violations. → *Expect:* serious issues are routed before informal mediation.
2. **Talk to each person separately.** Ask each coworker what happened, what impact it had, and what outcome they want. → *Expect:* you understand each perspective before convening.
3. **Set meeting rules.** State that the conversation will use observable facts, impact statements, no interruptions, and future-focused requests. → *Expect:* the discussion has boundaries.
4. **Name the shared problem.** Phrase the issue as "The handoff process is failing" rather than "Alex is careless." → *Expect:* the conversation targets work behavior.
5. **Let each person speak.** Give each coworker uninterrupted time to describe facts and impact. → *Expect:* both feel heard before problem-solving.
6. **Identify agreements and gaps.** Separate facts both accept from interpretations still in dispute. → *Expect:* the group knows what must be solved.
7. **Create working agreements.** [BRANCH: process conflict | interpersonal conflict] define handoff, review, or decision rules for process conflict; define communication boundaries for interpersonal conflict. → *Expect:* future behavior is specific.
8. **Document follow-up.** Send a neutral recap with agreements, owners, dates, and escalation path if agreements fail. → *Expect:* accountability does not depend on memory.

## Decision points

- If either person reports harassment, discrimination, threats, or retaliation → stop informal mediation and involve HR, legal, or the formal reporting channel.
- If one person has authority over the other → account for power imbalance and consider a manager or HR facilitator.
- If emotions are too high → pause and reconvene after separate conversations.
- If facts cannot be reconciled → agree on forward-looking rules instead of forcing consensus on the past.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Mediator takes sides:** detect advocacy for one story → return to observable facts, impact, and agreed future behavior.
- **F2 Blame language escalates:** detect labels like "lazy" or "hostile" → ask for the specific action and impact.
- **F3 Agreement is vague:** detect promises like "communicate better" → convert to channel, timing, owner, and response expectation.

## Verification

Both coworkers receive a neutral written recap that lists agreed behaviors, owners, follow-up date, and escalation path if the agreement is not followed.

## Variations

- Manager-facilitated: clarify whether you are coaching, deciding, or documenting performance.
- Peer mediation: ask both people to opt in before holding a joint conversation.
- Remote team: use video for the joint meeting and written notes for exact agreements.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because personnel conflict can affect careers and legal obligations. Do not promise confidentiality beyond company policy or mediate serious misconduct informally.
