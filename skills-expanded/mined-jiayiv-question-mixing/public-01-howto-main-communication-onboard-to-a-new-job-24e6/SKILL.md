## Goal

Start a new job with a clear operating map: people, tools, expectations, first deliverables, and communication norms.

## Preconditions

- A confirmed start date and manager or onboarding contact.
- Access to company email, calendar, chat, and required systems or a way to request them.
- A place to track notes, links, names, and open questions.

## Steps

1. **Confirm first-week logistics.** Ask where to be, when to arrive or log in, what equipment to bring, and what accounts should work. → *Expect:* you know the first-day plan.
2. **Set up your workspace.** Configure email, calendar, chat, password manager, payroll, HR, device security, and required team tools. → *Expect:* you can receive messages and access core systems.
3. **Build a people map.** List your manager, teammates, cross-functional partners, onboarding buddy, HR contact, and IT support. → *Expect:* you know who handles common questions.
4. **Clarify success measures.** Ask your manager, "What would make my first 30 days useful to the team?" → *Expect:* success is defined in outcomes, not only meetings attended.
5. **Learn communication norms.** Ask which decisions go in chat, email, tickets, docs, and meetings. → *Expect:* you can choose the right channel for routine work.
6. **Create a question list.** Group open questions by product, people, process, and permissions. → *Expect:* you can batch questions instead of interrupting constantly.
7. **Deliver a small early win.** [BRANCH: assigned task | no assigned task] complete the assigned starter task; if none exists, ask for a low-risk documentation, bug, or analysis task. → *Expect:* you contribute something visible and bounded.
8. **Send a first-week recap.** Send your manager a note with what you learned, what is blocked, and what you plan next. → *Expect:* expectations are aligned before habits harden.

## Decision points

- If access is blocked → document each missing system and ask the onboarding owner for priority order.
- If expectations are vague → ask for 30-, 60-, and 90-day outcomes in writing.
- If meetings overwhelm learning time → ask your manager which sessions are essential this week.
- If you inherit urgent work → confirm what can wait while you gain context.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Missing access:** detect login failures or invite gaps → send one concise list with system, error, and business need.
- **F2 Too much context:** detect scattered notes and no priorities → summarize themes and ask your manager to rank them.
- **F3 Silent confusion:** detect repeated guessing → ask "Who is the right person to explain this workflow?"

## Verification

You can name your top priorities, key contacts, required tools, first deliverable, and next check-in date without guessing.

## Variations

- Remote onboarding: overuse written recaps and ask for recorded demos.
- Manager onboarding: add team health, performance cycles, and inherited commitments.
- Regulated workplace: complete compliance training before accessing sensitive data.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but credentials, personnel information, and internal strategy may be exposed. Keep notes in approved systems and avoid storing secrets locally.
