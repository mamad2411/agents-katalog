## Goal

Prepare for a performance review with evidence, a clear self-assessment, and specific asks for growth, compensation, or support.

## Preconditions

- A scheduled review or review cycle.
- Access to goals, feedback, project records, and prior review notes.
- Awareness of company norms for promotion, compensation, and ratings.

## Steps

1. **Collect evidence.** Gather goals, shipped work, metrics, stakeholder notes, and examples of collaboration or leadership. → *Expect:* you have a fact base instead of relying on memory.
2. **Map evidence to expectations.** Match each accomplishment to role expectations, team goals, or business outcomes. → *Expect:* your review story shows why the work mattered.
3. **Write your self-assessment.** Use this structure: "This period I delivered X, which changed Y; I learned Z; next I want to improve A." → *Expect:* the draft is concise and evidence-backed.
4. **Name misses honestly.** List 1-3 areas where outcomes, communication, or execution could have been better, plus what changed. → *Expect:* weaknesses are framed as owned learning, not excuses.
5. **Prepare your asks.** [BRANCH: growth | compensation] ask for specific projects, feedback, or scope for growth; ask about process, timing, and criteria for compensation. → *Expect:* you know what you want from the conversation.
6. **Rehearse hard questions.** Practice answers to "What was your biggest miss?", "What support do you need?", and "Why do you think you are ready for more?" → *Expect:* you can answer directly without rambling.
7. **Send supporting notes.** Share your self-assessment or agenda before the meeting if your review process allows it. → *Expect:* your manager can review evidence before giving feedback.
8. **Capture outcomes after the review.** Write down feedback, commitments, dates, and open questions within the same day. → *Expect:* the conversation becomes an action plan.

## Decision points

- If compensation is handled separately → ask when pay decisions are made and what evidence influences them.
- If your manager disagrees with your self-assessment → ask for specific examples and calibration criteria.
- If you want promotion → ask what gap remains between your current level and the next level.
- If the review includes surprising negative feedback → request examples, impact, and a follow-up plan.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Evidence is vague:** detect phrases like "helped a lot" → replace with metric, artifact, stakeholder, or decision impact.
- **F2 Review turns defensive:** detect rapid rebuttals or raised tension → pause, acknowledge the point, and ask for one concrete example.
- **F3 No next steps:** detect praise or criticism without commitments → ask "What should I do differently before the next check-in?"

## Verification

Your review packet includes accomplishments, misses, learned themes, and 2-3 explicit asks, and the post-review notes list owner, action, and date for each commitment.

## Variations

- Promotion-focused: include level expectations and evidence for each criterion.
- New manager: add context about inherited goals and decisions.
- Remote role: include written artifacts and stakeholder quotes because informal visibility is lower.

## Safety & privacy

Medium career and compensation risk. Do not exaggerate metrics, reveal confidential personnel data, or rely on verbal promises for pay or title changes.
