## Goal

Negotiate with a vendor to improve price, terms, scope, risk allocation, or service without damaging the working relationship.

## Preconditions

- A vendor proposal, renewal, contract, statement of work, or invoice under discussion.
- Authority to negotiate or clear boundaries on what you can request.
- Knowledge of budget, must-haves, alternatives, and procurement or legal process.

## Steps

1. **Define your walk-away point.** Identify maximum price, required terms, and acceptable alternatives. → *Expect:* you know your limits before negotiating.
2. **Separate needs from preferences.** Mark requirements such as data security, uptime, delivery date, or legal clauses apart from nice-to-haves. → *Expect:* concessions are deliberate.
3. **Research leverage.** Compare market pricing, competing vendors, usage data, renewal timing, and vendor incentives. → *Expect:* requests are grounded in evidence.
4. **Open collaboratively.** Say, "We want to make this work, but we need the proposal to fit our budget and security requirements." → *Expect:* the tone is firm without hostility.
5. **Make specific asks.** Request exact changes: discount, payment schedule, service credits, termination right, implementation support, or scope adjustment. → *Expect:* the vendor can respond concretely.
6. **Trade, do not concede silently.** [BRANCH: price issue | terms issue] exchange longer commitment or reference call for price; exchange narrower scope or controls for terms. → *Expect:* every concession gets value back.
7. **Confirm in writing.** Summarize agreed terms, open points, deadlines, and who must approve. → *Expect:* negotiation history is clear.
8. **Route final documents.** Send the contract or quote through procurement, finance, legal, or security before signature. → *Expect:* internal approval happens before commitment.

## Decision points

- If the vendor says price is fixed → negotiate scope, payment timing, support, service levels, or exit rights.
- If the vendor pressures for immediate signature → slow down and cite required internal review.
- If the deal involves sensitive data → require security, privacy, and legal review.
- If no acceptable agreement is possible → prepare to use the alternative vendor or defer purchase.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 You negotiate without authority:** detect uncertainty about approval limits → pause and get internal boundaries before making promises.
- **F2 Verbal agreement drifts:** detect terms not reflected in the quote or contract → request corrected written documents before proceeding.
- **F3 Relationship turns adversarial:** detect threats or personal criticism → return to requirements, constraints, and options.

## Verification

The final vendor agreement reflects written price, scope, service, timing, risk terms, and required internal approvals before anyone signs or pays.

## Variations

- Renewal: use actual usage, support history, and switching cost as leverage.
- New vendor: compare multiple quotes and ask for implementation commitments.
- Enterprise contract: involve legal, security, finance, and procurement early.

## Safety & privacy

Medium financial and legal risk. Do not sign, commit budget, share sensitive data, or accept unfavorable terms without proper authority.
