## Goal

Write a business proposal that connects a customer or stakeholder problem to a credible solution, scope, timeline, price, and decision request.

## Preconditions

- A defined opportunity, client need, internal initiative, or request for proposal.
- Enough information about goals, constraints, budget, stakeholders, and decision criteria.
- Authority to propose scope, price, or next steps, or a reviewer who has that authority.

## Steps

1. **Clarify the audience.** Identify the decision-maker, influencers, users, and procurement or finance reviewers. → *Expect:* the proposal speaks to the right concerns.
2. **State the problem.** Write the current pain, impact, and cost of doing nothing. → *Expect:* the reader understands why action is needed.
3. **Present the solution.** Describe what you will do, how it works, and why it fits the stated problem. → *Expect:* the proposal has a clear recommendation.
4. **Define scope.** Include deliverables, exclusions, assumptions, timeline, dependencies, and acceptance criteria. → *Expect:* boundaries are explicit.
5. **Show value and proof.** Add relevant metrics, case examples, qualifications, or expected return. → *Expect:* the reader can justify the investment.
6. **Include price and terms.** [BRANCH: fixed fee | phased proposal] state one fixed price for clear scope; propose paid discovery or phases when uncertainty is high. → *Expect:* commercial terms match risk.
7. **Name risks and mitigations.** Explain schedule, adoption, technical, legal, or resource risks and how they will be managed. → *Expect:* credibility increases because limits are visible.
8. **Close with next step.** Ask for approval, signature, meeting, pilot, or procurement action by a specific date. → *Expect:* the reader knows how to proceed.

## Decision points

- If requirements are unclear → propose discovery before fixed implementation.
- If budget is unknown → include options or ask for budget range before writing a full proposal.
- If multiple stakeholders care about different outcomes → include an executive summary and role-specific detail.
- If legal terms are required → use approved templates and review before sending.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Proposal is generic:** detect reusable claims with no client-specific facts → add the client's problem, metrics, and constraints.
- **F2 Scope creep hidden:** detect vague deliverables like "support as needed" → define limits, assumptions, and change process.
- **F3 No decision ask:** detect an ending that only says "let me know" → replace with a concrete next step and date.

## Verification

The proposal includes problem, solution, scope, exclusions, timeline, price or commercial approach, proof, risks, and a specific decision request.

## Variations

- Internal proposal: emphasize strategic fit, staffing, opportunity cost, and success metrics.
- Sales proposal: include buyer outcomes, implementation path, and procurement steps.
- Grant or public-sector proposal: follow the requested format exactly.

## Safety & privacy

Medium financial and legal risk. Do not promise scope, pricing, data handling, or timelines beyond approved authority.
