## Goal

Write a concise project status report that tells stakeholders what changed, what is blocked, and what decisions are needed.

## Preconditions

- The reporting period and audience are known.
- Current project progress, risks, blockers, and next milestones are available.
- You know where the report should be posted or sent.

## Steps

1. **Open the reporting destination.** Open the document, project update field, email draft, or status tool. → *Expect:* the report can be edited.
2. **State the overall status.** Choose a simple status such as on track, at risk, blocked, or complete. → *Expect:* the reader can understand the project state at a glance.
3. **Summarize progress.** List the most important completed work since the last update. → *Expect:* accomplishments are concrete and recent.
4. **Name risks and blockers.** Describe each blocker, owner, and needed decision or dependency. → *Expect:* unresolved issues are actionable.
5. **Update timeline and scope.** Note changes to milestones, due dates, deliverables, or assumptions. → *Expect:* stakeholders see what changed.
6. **List next steps.** Add the next one to five actions with owners and dates. → *Expect:* the report points to future work.
7. **Publish or send the report.** Share it in the agreed channel. → *Expect:* stakeholders can access the same status record.

## Decision points

- If the project is at risk → lead with the risk and the decision needed.
- If the audience is executive → use outcomes, dates, and decisions instead of detailed task lists.
- If no progress occurred → state why and what will change before the next report.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too vague:** detect phrases like "working on it" without evidence → recover by adding specific deliverables, dates, or blockers.
- **F2 Missing decision request:** detect a blocker with no ask → recover by naming the decision owner and deadline.
- **F3 Audience mismatch:** detect too much or too little detail for readers → recover by rewriting around their decisions.

## Verification

The report includes overall status, recent progress, risks or blockers, timeline changes, next steps, owners, and publication location.

## Variations

- Weekly report: include changes since the prior week and next week's focus.
- Executive report: keep detail high-level and decision-focused.
- Customer report: avoid internal blame and include only approved commitments.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Remove confidential customer data, personnel comments, and unapproved financial details before sharing broadly.
