# How to read your LinkedIn analytics

Run `/linkedin-analytics` and paste the numbers (or a screenshot). Interpretation rules from the slide:

## Profile level
| Metric | What it tells you | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Follower growth | How many people decided they want more of you | Line spikes up against what you published that week; double down on those formats |
| Total social engagements | All reactions, comments and reposts in the period | Sanity check on reach: high reach with low engagement usually means the wrong audience |
| Total impressions | Times your content appeared in front of anyone | Volume, not performance: rising impressions with flat profile views means people see you but are not looking for more |
| Total members reached | Individuals who saw you, not times you appeared | Compare with impressions; a wide gap means the same audience again and again |
| Search appearances | Times you turned up in search results | Flat while reach is high: your headline lacks the words people search for |

## Post level
| Metric | What it tells you | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions | Times this post was served | Judge against your own average; it is the baseline |
| In-network vs out-of-network | Split of viewers inside vs outside your network | High in-network share: your network did not respond well, so it was not pushed wider |
| Profile viewers from post | People curious enough to click your profile | Note topic, format and hook; double down |
| Followers gained from post | People who followed straight from the post | Clear interest signal; the names are visible, so message them as a follow-up |
| Saves | People who want to return later | Strongest signal a post was useful; cheat sheets and frameworks earn these |
| Sends | People who shared it privately | A private share is a recommendation with a name on it; analyse topic and format |
| Link clicks | People who took the next step | Rise with a strong emotional journey in the caption or a clearly outlined value |

## Reading discipline (added here)
Compare each post to your own median of the last 10, not to other people. Change one variable at a time (hook, format or topic). A single post is noise; judge a format over at least 4 posts. The example numbers on the slide belong to one account and are not benchmarks.
