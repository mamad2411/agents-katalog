# 5 mental models every CEO should master

| Model | Use when | How (from the slide) | Existing command |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Pre-mortem | Launching a product, rolling out a strategy, presenting to your board | Spot failure points before they happen; place them on a likelihood x impact matrix (high-high: avoid; low-high: leverage or reduce; high-low: share the risk; low-low: accept); refine the plan | `/premortem` |
| 2. The 5 Whys | Recurring performance problems | Ask "why" five times in a row to reach the root cause (slide example: missed revenue target, down to "we prioritised investor optics over go-to-market readiness") | `/5whys` |
| 3. Decision tree | Complex decisions with several variables or trade-offs | Draw each decision path; attach outcomes, risks and potential returns; choose on clarity | `/decision-tree` |
| 4. Rapid SWOT | Realigning strategy, pitching investors, refining positioning | Answer: Strengths (what are we doing well right now?), Weaknesses (what is holding us back?), Opportunities (where can we grow or win quickly?), Threats (what risks or disruptions are on the horizon?) | `/swot-analysis` |
| 5. Impact vs Effort | Resources are stretched and you need clarity on what drives results | Map by impact vs effort. High impact, low effort: quick wins, do now. High impact, high effort: strategic plays, plan and invest. Low impact, low effort: automate or delegate. Low impact, high effort: drains on focus, cut or avoid | `/impact-effort` (new) |

## Routing
- "Why did this happen?" -> 5 Whys. "What could go wrong?" -> Pre-mortem. "Which option?" -> Decision tree. "Where do we stand?" -> SWOT. "What first?" -> Impact vs Effort.
- Do not use 5 Whys on a problem with several independent causes: it forces one chain. Split the problem first. (Judgement, not from the slide.)
- A SWOT without owners and dates is a list; end every model with one decision and one owner.
