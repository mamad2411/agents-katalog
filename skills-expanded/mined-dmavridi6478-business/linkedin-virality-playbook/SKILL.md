# How to Go Viral on LinkedIn

Source: infographic by Josh Sanders, backed by real performance data (117K impressions, 96% out-of-network reach, 5,303 engagements on the source post). The core diagnostic in the data: 96% of reach was out-of-network — a post's own following is not what drives virality on LinkedIn, repostability to strangers is.

## The 9 tactics

1. **Study what's working, instead of who's popular.** Screenshot posts in the niche getting saves/comments/likes. Look for the pattern in the hook, structure, and CTA — not the account's follower count. *Action: save 5 posts this week, note the hook style/structure/CTA each used, apply the pattern to the next batch of content.*
2. **Find the angle that gets repostable.** Every post has a "safe" version and a version people can't scroll past. Push past the safe draft until it says something people will stand behind by resharing. *Action: write the safe draft first, then rewrite the opening line to make a stronger claim.*
3. **Lead with proof, instead of opinions.** The first two lines should contain a number, result, or date. People trust receipts and are more likely to repost something backed by facts than a bare opinion. *Action: rewrite the opening line to lead with a stat or result — eye-catching and on-topic.*
4. **Speak to interests.** Posts travel furthest when they speak to a widely-understood problem. *Action: ask who, outside the usual audience, has this exact problem — if there's no answer, that's the gap.*
5. **Build high-performing graphics and visuals.** Every visual should follow a reusable template — shareable, saveable, built to be screenshotted. *Action: pick one graphic format, reuse it for the next 5 posts, vary only colors, and track which version travels furthest.*
6. **Strong engagement strategy.** End with a real question — one that opens a conversation rather than closing it. The more it invites someone to share their own story, the more likely a response (and a repost). *Action: end the next post with a question that needs more than a one-word answer.*
7. **Make it actionable.** If a reader can act on it the moment they finish reading, they dwell longer — and dwell time drives distribution. *Action: add one concrete next step the reader can do today, to every post.*
8. **Talk to more than one person.** The wider a post speaks (founders, marketers, ops leads — whoever's reading should see themselves in it), the further it travels. *Action: before publishing, check whether someone in a different role would still agree with the post's message.*
9. **Paint the dream outcome.** People follow and repost content because they can see themselves at the other end of it. *Action: add one line showing where this takes the reader, and how to get there.*

## How to use this playbook

- Apply it as a **pre-publish checklist**, not a template to fill in mechanically — a post that hits all 9 boxes but reads like a checklist itself will read as formulaic. The tactics should shape the post, not become visible in it.
- For **diagnosing an underperforming post**: check tactics 2, 3, and 6 first — a weak hook (no proof/stakes in the first two lines) and no real question at the end are the most common failure points.
- **Out-of-network reach is the metric that matters** for virality specifically — if reviewing performance, check the in-network vs. out-of-network split before concluding a post "worked," not just raw impressions.
