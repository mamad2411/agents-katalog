# 8 Ways to Build Executive Presence

Source: infographic by Dora Vanourek (dora.coach, "First Year in a new role"). It is a coaching checklist, not research — use it as a rubric, never as proof that a person "lacks presence".

## The eight habits

| # | Habit | Principle | Phrase to practise |
|---|-------|-----------|--------------------|
| 1 | Practise strategic silence | Constant talking signals insecurity; leaders who listen first command more respect | "What are your thoughts?" — then hold the pause |
| 2 | Name the elephant | Meetings carry an unspoken tension; the person who names it calmly earns credibility | "Can we talk about what's really going on?" |
| 3 | Make others look brilliant | Respected leaders give credit; it signals security and builds loyalty | "That was Sarah's idea." (before adding your take) |
| 4 | Simplify complex ideas | Expertise is making others understand, not showing what you know | Replace jargon with everyday words and one concrete example |
| 5 | Own your authority | Undermining phrases erase years of work | Drop "just", "kind of", "I think maybe". Say "I recommend." |
| 6 | Calibrate your reactions | People notice how you handle setbacks; overreacting costs credibility | Ask "Will this matter in 6 months?" and pause before replying |
| 7 | Bring solutions, not problems | Good leaders spot problems; great leaders are valued for solving them | Never raise an issue empty-handed: a recommendation, a next step, or "here's what I need to find out" |
| 8 | Disagree without drama | Most people avoid conflict or escalate; presence lives in the middle: calm, direct, unafraid | "I see it differently. Here's why." No softening, no aggression |

## Procedure

1. Take the text (draft, transcript, agenda, planned talking points). If none is given, ask for one. Never score a person from a single sentence.
2. Score each habit 0–2 from quoted evidence: 0 = contradicted, 1 = absent or unclear, 2 = visible. Quote the line that earns each score. "No evidence" is 1, not 0.
3. Flag undermining phrases found for habit 5 (just, kind of, sort of, I think maybe, sorry to bother, does that make sense) with counts.
4. Rewrite the three weakest lines. Keep the speaker's meaning and voice; change only the framing.
5. For habit 7, check every problem raised has an attached recommendation or next step.
6. Output the scoring table, the three rewrites, and ONE habit to practise this week with the exact phrase.

## Guardrails
- Presence is context-dependent; do not rank people, and do not give gender- or culture-neutral claims that the source does not make.
- Habit 8 does not mean "always disagree"; if the user agrees with the plan, say so.
- Do not fabricate quotes. Evidence must come from the supplied text.
