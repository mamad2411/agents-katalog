# Unlock GTM: the first 100 customers

Source: an infographic by Megha Sharma for OneGTMLab. It carries its own caveat: "Stages are guideposts, not guarantees." The "why" notes are mine.

| Stage | Goal | Lead generation | Channels shown | Build this | Working when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Understand customers (before selling) | Talk to buyers: learn their pain, current workaround and reason to buy | Conversations | n/a | n/a | You can state the pain, the workaround and why they would switch |
| 0 to 10: sell it yourself | Prove someone will pay for the problem you solve | Personal outreach and real buyer conversations | LinkedIn, Gmail, Reddit | Clear offer and a simple one-page; first customer proof | Buyers pay and get the promised result |
| 10 to 50: repeat what works | Make your best customer-winning play repeatable | Repeat the winning offer and follow-up; track conversations to offers to sales | LinkedIn, X, Gmail, Reddit, LinkedIn ads | Simple CRM and follow-up playbook; case studies and consistent content | Similar buyers convert for similar reasons |
| 50 to 100: scale what holds up | Grow without weakening delivery or margins | Scale the strongest channel gradually; test paid ads with a capped budget | Google Ads, LinkedIn, X, Gmail, Reddit, LinkedIn ads, Meta; referrals are the strongest existing channel | Landing page and conversion tracking; reliable onboarding and a referral process | Customer results and margins hold as volume grows |

## Reading it critically [Likely]

- The thresholds are rules of thumb. A high-priced B2B product may never reach 100 customers; a low-priced one may pass 100 in a week. Use them as order of operations, not targets.
- "Working when" is the useful part: each stage has a test that is about customers, not activity.
- Compare with `gtm-infographics-9-cover` (stages by ARR) and `gtm-strategy`; the two frames agree on the order: learn, then repeat, then scale.
