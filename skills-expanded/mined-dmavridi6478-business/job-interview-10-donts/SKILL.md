# 10 things not to say in a job interview

Source: an infographic by Dr. Christian Poensgen. The table follows its content; the "make it yours" column is mine.

| # | Avoid | Why (from the graphic) | Say instead (from the graphic) | Make it yours |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "I'm open to anything." | Seems unfocused and unsure of what you want | "I'm particularly interested in this role because it aligns with my expertise in [specific skill or industry]." | Name one skill and one result you got with it |
| 2 | "I don't know much about your company." | Signals lack of preparation and curiosity | "I was impressed by [specific company initiative], and I'd love to learn more about how this role contributes." | Read the last three news items or posts first |
| 3 | "I don't have any weaknesses." | Seems arrogant or lacking self-awareness | "One area I've been developing is [skill]. I've taken steps like [specific action] to improve, and it's made a big difference." | Pick a real, non-critical weakness with real evidence |
| 4 | "How soon can I get promoted?" | Seems impatient rather than focused on adding value first | "What does growth typically look like in this role, and how does success get measured?" | Ask after you have shown interest in the work |
| 5 | "I'm a perfectionist." | Overused, sounds insincere, gives no insight | "I hold myself to high standards, but I've learned to balance quality with efficiency by setting clear priorities." | Add one example of a trade-off you made |
| 6 | "I left my last job because I couldn't stand my boss." | Speaking negatively about a past employer raises red flags | "I was looking for a role that offers [specific opportunity], which is why I'm excited about this position." | Name the opportunity honestly; do not invent one |
| 7 | "I prefer to work alone." | Collaboration is key in most roles | "I'm comfortable working independently but also enjoy collaborating with teams to drive results." | Give one example of each |
| 8 | "What's the salary?" (as the first question) | Seems like compensation is your primary concern | "I'd love to learn more about the role and expectations. Could you share how the company approaches compensation and career growth?" | Do research on pay ranges beforehand so you can answer when asked |
| 9 | "Time management isn't my strong suit." | Highlighting a weakness without a solution hurts credibility | "I've been refining my time management by using [specific tool or strategy], which has helped me stay ahead of deadlines." | Only claim a tool or habit you actually use |
| 10 | "I don't have any questions." | Signals lack of interest | "What are the biggest challenges for someone stepping into this position?" | Prepare three questions; keep one for the end |

## Limits

The replacement lines are templates: reading them out word for word sounds rehearsed, and claiming a skill, tool or initiative you do not have is a bigger problem than the original answer. Rules differ by country and culture (salary talk in particular). Related commands in this repo: `mockinterview` and `job-interview-cheat-sheet-12`. Use `/interview-donts`.
