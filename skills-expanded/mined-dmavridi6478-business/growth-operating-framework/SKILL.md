# The Growth Operating Framework

Source: infographic by David Manela, "The Growth Operating Framework — The 7 steps to unlock capital efficient growth." This is a sequence, not a menu — steps build on each other; skipping ahead (e.g. optimizing for scale before the data foundation is trustworthy) produces confident-looking numbers that are actually wrong.

## The 7 steps

1. **Align on the real business.** Define the actual problem and the constraints around it: budget, payback, margin, timeline. Growth plans built before this step optimize for a version of the business that doesn't match its actual financial constraints.
2. **Nail your North Star metric.** The right metric depends on the strategy, not a generic default. Going for long-term profitability rather than pure top-line growth? A 12–18 month LTV:CAC ratio might be the real North Star, not monthly signups.
3. **Get your data foundation right.** The common failure mode: 10 tools, 10 teams, 10 different definitions of "customer." Growth decisions built on inconsistent definitions are decisions built on noise — fix definitions and instrumentation before trusting any dashboard.
4. **Strengthen the feedback loop.** Attribution alone isn't enough — it tells you where a conversion was last touched, not whether the business is healthy. Unified metrics (consistent across teams/tools) are what make the feedback loop actually trustworthy.
5. **Build cross-functional accountability.** Marketing finds ways to scale *within* the constraints finance sets; finance unlocks more budget *when* those constraints are met. This step fails when marketing and finance operate as adversaries reporting different numbers instead of a single shared scoreboard.
6. **Optimize for capital efficiency and scale.** "What works" isn't the bar — the real question is "what works best, and is it scalable and replicable?" A channel that works once at small spend isn't necessarily a channel that works at 10x spend.
7. **Execute fast, iterate faster.** The payoff of the first six steps: test cycles that used to take weeks now take days, because the foundation (steps 1-6) means less time is spent re-litigating what a result even means. Signal-to-decision-to-deployment speed is the compounding advantage.

## How to use this framework

- **Diagnosing why growth spend isn't working**: walk the steps in order and find where the chain actually breaks — teams often assume it's step 6 (channel optimization) when the real problem is step 3 (the data foundation) or step 5 (marketing and finance aren't actually aligned).
- **Structuring a growth plan or review**: use the 7 steps as section headers rather than an ad hoc list of initiatives — it forces the plan to show its constraints (step 1) and metric choice (step 2) explicitly instead of jumping straight to tactics (step 6-7).
- **Setting a growth cadence**: steps 3-5 are largely one-time/foundational setup; steps 6-7 are the ongoing operating loop once the foundation exists.
