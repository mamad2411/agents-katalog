# Quarterly Review Cadence

Source: reviewed from an uploaded infographic — "How to Run a Quarterly
Review" (Amy Gibson). No literal AI prompt — a structured retrospective
process, written up as a skill the way the source presented it.

This is a **backward-looking retrospective + forward-looking target-set**,
distinct from `quarterly-okr-architect` (a forward-only goal-architecture
fusion of 4DX/MASTER/DUMB/Backward-Goal-Setting). The two are
complementary: this skill is what closes out the quarter that just ended
and feeds the next one's targets; `quarterly-okr-architect` is the
heavier-weight tool for architecting those targets once this session has
surfaced them, when the targets need full rigor (lead measures,
scoreboard, accountability cadence) rather than just a stated top-3 list.

## The 5-step quarterly review

Five steps, three questions each — a simple framework for reviewing the
last quarter and planning the next one:

1. **Top 3 Wins** — celebrate what worked and why it landed.
   - "What are you most proud of this quarter?"
   - "What helped you get there?"
   - "What would you do the same way again?"

2. **Top 3 Challenges** — name what created friction or stalled momentum.
   - "Where did you get stuck?"
   - "What would have made it easier?"
   - "What do you need from me next quarter?"

3. **Top 3 Learnings** — find the patterns inside the wins and setbacks.
   - "What surprised you this quarter?"
   - "What would you do differently?"
   - "What did this quarter teach you about how you work?"

4. **Top 3 Unfinished** — what needs to be carried forward, delegated, or
   released.
   - "What still needs your attention?"
   - "What's no longer worth your time?"
   - "Who else could own this?"

5. **Top 3 Next-Quarter Targets** — set goals shaped by what was just
   uncovered, not chosen independently of it.
   - "What does a great next quarter look like for you?"
   - "What's the one thing that would make the biggest difference?"
   - "How will we know you've hit it?"

The five run as a loop, not a one-off — each quarter's Unfinished and
Learnings feed directly into the next quarter's Wins/Challenges review.

## The prep timeline

Run over roughly 3 weeks around the review itself, not just the meeting:

- **2 weeks before** — gather the data (~30 min): pull metrics, project
  updates, and team feedback into one place.
- **1 week before** — share the agenda (~15 min): send the 5 steps to
  the team/person so they come prepared, not cold.
- **Review day** — run the session (~90 min): walk through all 5 steps
  together.
- **Week after** — lock targets and share the reset (~60 min): finalize
  the top-3 next-quarter targets, assign owners, and distribute the
  summary so everyone starts aligned.

## 5 signs it's actually working

Use these to tell a review that's genuinely changing behavior from one
that's just a recurring calendar item:

1. **Fewer repeat problems** — the same obstacles stop showing up quarter
   after quarter.
2. **Faster decisions** — the team spends less time debating and more
   time doing.
3. **Wins compound** — each quarter builds on the last instead of
   starting over.
4. **Honest conversations increase** — challenges surface earlier because
   people feel safe sharing them.
5. **Alignment without meetings** — shared priorities mean fewer
   check-ins are needed to stay on track.

If none of these are showing up after a few cycles, the review itself is
the thing to diagnose — not just the targets it's producing.
