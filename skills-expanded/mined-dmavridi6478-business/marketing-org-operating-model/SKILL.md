# Marketing Org Operating Model

Source: reviewed from an uploaded infographic — "How to Future-Proof Your
Marketing Organization" (Lisa Cole, The Limitless CMO). A 5-step operating-
model redesign, distinct from this repo's existing `growth-operating-framework`:
that skill is about *growth strategy* (which metric to chase, how to spend
capital efficiently); this one is about *how the marketing function's work
itself gets organized and executed* — the org-design and build/buy/automate
layer underneath any growth strategy.

## The 5 steps

1. **Marketing mandates.** Map board-level growth goals (revenue targets,
   pipeline goals, ICP clarity, board mandates) directly to the marketing
   function's accountabilities and priorities — every workflow that follows
   should trace back to one of these mandates, not exist because "that's
   how it's always been done."
2. **Codify the work.** Document every workflow, output, and deliverable
   needed to execute against the mandate: campaign calendar, content
   workflows, channel plans, messaging framework, enablement assets, demand
   programs, brand guidelines. You can't route or automate work that isn't
   written down first.
3. **In-house, outsource, or automate.** Apply decision logic to route each
   codified work stream to its most strategic execution path — skill
   assessment, fixed-cost audit, partner shortlist, execution routing,
   workflow mapping, build-vs-buy, SLA definition. This is a deliberate
   per-workflow decision, not a blanket "automate everything" or "keep
   everything in-house" default.
4. **Apply AI.** Embed AI as a force multiplier *across* every workflow —
   internal, outsourced, and automated alike — rather than bolting it onto
   one channel. Concretely: predictive insights, speed-to-market, closed
   performance loops, dynamic personalization, AI-assisted scoring, content
   automation, prompt libraries, agent workflows.
5. **Prove and demonstrate.** Measure full-funnel impact and translate it
   into finance's language, not marketing's — pipeline impact, ROI
   dashboard, CAC reduction, velocity metrics, win-rate data, revenue
   contribution, CFO-ready reporting. This step is what earns the next
   budget cycle's trust; skipping it (or reporting only activity metrics)
   is the most common reason marketing loses ground to finance internally.

## How to use this

- **Redesigning a marketing team's structure**: walk the 5 steps as an
  actual audit — for each existing workflow, ask which mandate (step 1) it
  serves, whether it's actually codified (step 2), and whether its current
  in-house/outsource/automate placement (step 3) was a deliberate choice or
  just inertia.
- **Building an AI adoption plan for a marketing function**: step 4 only
  works once steps 1-3 exist — AI applied to an undocumented, un-routed
  workflow multiplies chaos, not output. Don't start at step 4.
- **Making the case for headcount, tooling, or budget**: step 5 is the
  deliverable — build the finance-language proof before asking, not after.

## Related skills in this repo

- **growth-operating-framework**: the growth-*strategy* layer (North Star
  metric, data foundation, capital efficiency) this skill's mandates (step
  1) should trace back to — use that framework to decide *what* growth
  looks like, this one to decide *how the org executes* against it.
- **quarterly-okr-architect**: turn this skill's step-1 mandates into an
  actual goal cascade with lead measures and a scoreboard.
- **business-decision-frameworks**: apply RICE/Value-vs-Effort when step 3
  needs to rank which workflows get automated first, not just which get
  automated at all.
- **ceo-reporting-skill** / **business-intelligence-report**: the delivery
  layer for step 5's finance-language proof.
- **outreach-execution** / **content-automation-pipeline**: concrete
  examples of step 4 (AI/automation embedded in a specific marketing
  workflow) already built out in this repo.
