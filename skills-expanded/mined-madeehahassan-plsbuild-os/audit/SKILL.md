# /audit

Use this before any significant decision. Product direction, feature prioritization, pricing, a pivot, a partnership. Also use it when you catch yourself rationalizing something you already want to do.

---

## The protocol

**Step 1: Name the decision in one sentence.**
If you can't, the thinking isn't done yet.

**Step 2: Identify the type.**
Product / Strategic / Operational / People / Financial

**Step 3: Surface the real motivation.**
Why now? Why this? Is it driven by user signal, data, or instinct? Or by fear, boredom, ego, or social pressure?

**Step 4: Check for the common traps.**

Shiny object syndrome: Is this new idea getting attention because it's genuinely better, or because the current thing feels hard?

Solution looking for a problem: Have users actually asked for this?

Avoiding the hard thing: Is this a way to avoid something more important?

Premature optimization: Are you building for scale you don't have yet?

Sunk cost: Are you continuing because you've invested time and money, not because it's still the right direction?

**Step 5: The honest questions. Pick 2-3.**
- "If you were starting fresh today, would you make this same decision?"
- "What would you tell a friend who was about to do this?"
- "What's the worst realistic outcome if this is wrong?"
- "What signal would tell you in 6 months that this was right?"
- "Who disagrees, and what's their strongest argument?"
- "What are you not doing because of this decision?"

**Step 6: Verdict.**

Decision: [what's being considered]
Motivation: [signal-driven / instinct / fear / other]
Pattern flags: [name any traps, or "none"]
Honest read: [1-2 sentences]
Recommendation: [Do it / Don't do it / Do it differently / Get more signal first]

---

## Tone

Say the thing once, clearly. Don't soften a flag if it's there. The founder makes the call. The audit is a mirror, not a veto.
