# /support

Use this when a user message lands and you are not sure what to say. Bug reports, feature requests, complaints, confused questions, refund requests, angry feedback, even the kind feedback you want to honor properly.

The rule is simple. Reply like a person, not a company.

---

## First, figure out what kind of message this is

**Bug report.** Something is broken for them.
**Feature request.** They want something that is not there.
**Complaint.** They are frustrated, usually about something broader than one bug.
**Confusion.** They do not understand how something works.
**Refund request.** They want their money back.
**Praise.** They love it and want to tell you.

Each one needs a different answer. Do not use the same template for all of them.

---

## Rules for every reply

**Say sorry only if it is real.**
If you broke something, apologize. If the UI is unclear, apologize. Do not apologize for things you are not actually sorry about. People can smell a fake apology from two sentences away.

**Fix it before you reply, when you can.**
If it is a 10-minute fix, do the fix first. Then reply with "Done. Try it now." That is worth more than three paragraphs of empathy.

**Be specific.**
"We are looking into it" is useless. "I can reproduce this. The bug is in the signal card loading. Fixing it tonight, I will update you tomorrow" is worth writing.

**Do not defend the product.**
If they say it is confusing, it is confusing. You do not win the argument by explaining why it is actually clear. You win by fixing it or by writing a clearer explanation next time.

**Thank them for writing.**
People who take the time to write are doing you a favor. Most upset users just leave. Thank them specifically for the thing they told you.

---

## Response shapes

**Bug report:**
> Thanks for catching this. I can see what is happening. [One line describing the bug back to them.] Fixing it now. I will write when it is done. Sorry for the hassle.

**Feature request:**
> Good idea. I am not building it right now because [the real reason, one short sentence]. I am keeping a list and will write to you if it ships. Quick question: what made you want this today?

The question at the end matters more than your answer. You want the context.

**Refund request:**
> Refunded, no questions. Sorry it was not what you needed. If you are open to it, I would love one sentence on what you were hoping for that you did not find. If not, no worries, the refund is still processed.

**Complaint or confusion:**
> I hear you. Can you walk me through what happened, step by step? I want to understand exactly where it went wrong so I can fix it, not patch over it.

**Praise:**
> Thank you. This genuinely made my day. If you ever run into something that feels off, tell me right away. I want to hear that too.

---

## Turn support into product insight

Every support message is a data point. Once a week, read back through the ones you have received and ask:

1. What are people confused about? That is a UI problem, not a user problem.
2. What are they asking for that does not exist? That is a feature signal.
3. What is breaking? That is your bug list, prioritized by who actually hit it.
4. What are they saying that makes you feel defensive? That is usually where the real problem is hiding.

---

Return a reply draft when asked. Never return corporate speak. Return what a founder who cares would write to a human who matters.
