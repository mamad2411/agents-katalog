# Meeting Prep

Prepare the user for an upcoming meeting by generating a structured briefing package. The
meeting itself is theirs; this package gets them ready for it.

## Step 1: Gather Context

If not already provided in `$ARGUMENTS` or the conversation, ask for:

- Who is attending (names, roles, and their primary concerns or priorities)
- The meeting purpose (status update, decision point, escalation, discovery, and so on)
- Current project status and any recent developments the attendees may not be aware of
- What the user needs from this meeting (a decision, an approval, a commitment, information)
- Any sensitivities, history, or political dynamics to be aware of

If relevant project files are available (status data, the last meeting's notes, the SOW), read
them to inform the preparation. Say which files you read.

## Step 2: Produce the Meeting Prep Package

### Meeting Brief

**Meeting:** [Meeting name / call type]
**Date/Time:** [If provided]
**Attendees:** [Names and roles]
**Objective:** [One sentence: what a successful meeting looks like]

### Agenda (with time allocations)
| Time | Topic | Lead | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|

### Key Messages
For each agenda item, 2 to 3 crisp talking points. Lead with the bottom line; follow with
supporting evidence. Tailor to the stated audience; C-suite language differs from working-team
language.

### Anticipated Questions & Tough Topics
| Likely Question | Recommended Response | Notes |
|---|---|---|

Focus on the hard questions, not the easy ones. For sensitive topics, prepare both the direct
answer and a bridging statement if the conversation needs to be redirected.

### What You Need from Them
Be explicit about decisions, approvals, or commitments being sought. Bring three options whenever
asking for a decision; it is easier to choose than to create from scratch.

### Pre-Meeting Checklist
- [ ] Relevant documents shared with attendees in advance
- [ ] Key data points confirmed and up to date
- [ ] Any follow-ups from last meeting addressed or flagged
- [ ] Technology and logistics confirmed (dial-in, room, slides)

## Step 3: Draft Follow-Up Email

After the prep package, produce a ready-to-edit follow-up email:

**Subject:** [Meeting topic]: Summary and Next Steps

[Opening sentence]
[Key decisions and agreements, 3 to 5 bullets]
[Action items table: Action | Owner | Due Date]
[Next touchpoint / closing]

This is a draft written before the meeting. The user rewrites it afterward to reflect what was
actually decided. Never present it as a record of the meeting.

## Tone

Professional and direct. For executive meetings, lead with the bottom line; senior audiences read
the opening statement of each section and scan the rest.
