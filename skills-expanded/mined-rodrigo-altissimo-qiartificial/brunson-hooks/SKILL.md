## SECTION 2: CORE KNOWLEDGE — HOOK-STORY-OFFER

### 2.1 The Universal Marketing Structure

Hook-Story-Offer is not just a framework. It is the DNA of every successful piece of marketing ever created. From a 15-second TikTok to a 90-minute webinar, the structure is the same:

```
HOOK-STORY-OFFER
================

HOOK (3-5 seconds)
├── Purpose: Capture attention and earn the right to be heard
├── Mechanism: Pattern interrupt + curiosity gap
├── Test: "Would I stop scrolling?"
├── Length: 1-2 sentences max (shorter is better)
└── Outcome: The audience decides to keep consuming

STORY (30 seconds to 30 minutes, context-dependent)
├── Purpose: Create emotional connection and increase perceived value
├── Mechanism: Epiphany Bridge — share a transformative experience
├── Structure: Backstory → Desire → Wall → Epiphany → Achievement
├── Test: "Would I feel something?"
└── Outcome: The audience believes the transformation is possible for THEM

OFFER (variable length)
├── Purpose: Present an irresistible call-to-action
├── Mechanism: Value stack + urgency + risk reversal
├── Structure: What they get → What it is worth → What they pay → Why now
├── Test: "Would I feel stupid saying no?"
└── Outcome: The audience takes the desired action
```

### 2.2 The HSO Diagnostic

When something is not converting, the answer is ALWAYS in one of the three elements. Here is the diagnostic protocol:

```
HSO CONVERSION DIAGNOSTIC
==========================

SYMPTOM: Low click-through rate / low views / low engagement
DIAGNOSIS: Hook failure
PRESCRIPTION: Test 10 new hooks. Keep story and offer the same.

SYMPTOM: High clicks but low time-on-page / high bounce / low watch time
DIAGNOSIS: Story failure
PRESCRIPTION: The hook promised something the story is not delivering.
              Realign story to fulfill the hook's promise. Add Epiphany Bridge.

SYMPTOM: High engagement but low conversions
DIAGNOSIS: Offer failure
PRESCRIPTION: The story built desire but the offer did not close.
              Add value stack. Add guarantee. Add urgency. Add scarcity.

SYMPTOM: Low everything
DIAGNOSIS: Multiple failures — start with the hook.
PRESCRIPTION: Fix in order: Hook first, then Story, then Offer.
              Never fix Offer before Hook is working.
```

### 2.4 The Hook Hierarchy

Not all hooks are created equal. Here is the hierarchy from weakest to strongest:

```
HOOK HIERARCHY (weakest to strongest)
======================================

Level 1: GENERIC HOOK
  "Learn how to grow your business"
  PROBLEM: No specificity, no curiosity, no pattern interrupt
  SCORE: 1/10

Level 2: SPECIFIC HOOK
  "Learn the 3-step system that grew my business to $1M"
  PROBLEM: Better, but still expected. Sounds like everyone else.
  SCORE: 4/10

Level 3: CURIOSITY HOOK
  "I accidentally discovered something that tripled my revenue in 30 days"
  BETTER: Creates curiosity gap, adds specificity, opens a loop
  SCORE: 6/10

Level 4: CONTRARIAN HOOK
  "Everything you know about growing your business is wrong — here is proof"
  BETTER: Challenges beliefs, creates tension, demands resolution
  SCORE: 7/10

Level 5: STORY HOOK
  "Last Tuesday at 2am, I almost shut down my entire business. Then this happened."
  BETTER: Narrative tension, emotional engagement, impossible to ignore
  SCORE: 8/10

  "Stop. Do NOT read this if you are happy making $5K/month."

  "The $47 mistake that cost me $2.3M — and why 97% of entrepreneurs

  SUPREME: Combines story + specificity + contrarian + curiosity + identity
  SCORE: 10/10
```

## SECTION 3: HOOK TYPES & FORMULAS

### 3.1 Curiosity Hooks

Curiosity hooks exploit the human need for cognitive closure. When an information gap is opened, the brain physically demands resolution. This is not a preference — it is neuroscience.

**Core Mechanism:** Open an information loop that cannot be resolved without continuing.

**Curiosity Hook Formulas (15 templates):**

```
CURIOSITY HOOK TEMPLATES
=========================

CH-01: "The [unexpected thing] that [impressive result]"
  Example: "The $3 notebook that built a $10M business"
  Why it works: Unexpected cause-effect creates cognitive dissonance

CH-02: "[Authority figure] does NOT want you to know this about [topic]"
  Example: "Facebook does NOT want you to know this about their algorithm"
  Why it works: Forbidden knowledge + authority creates irresistible curiosity

CH-03: "I was wrong about [commonly held belief] — here is what actually works"
  Example: "I was wrong about email marketing — here is what actually works"
  Why it works: Vulnerability + correction pattern demands resolution

CH-04: "The [hidden/secret/unknown] reason why [common problem] keeps happening"
  Example: "The hidden reason why your ads stop working after 2 weeks"
  Why it works: Names a specific pain + promises an unknown cause

CH-05: "[Specific number] [people/companies] are doing this. You are not."
  Example: "247 ClickFunnels users are doing this. You are not."
  Why it works: Social proof + exclusion creates FOMO + curiosity

CH-06: "What happens when you [common action] but [unexpected twist]"
  Example: "What happens when you raise your prices by 300% and tell nobody"
  Why it works: Familiar setup + unexpected variable = must know the answer

CH-07: "I spent [time/money] to learn [topic]. Here is what nobody tells you."
  Example: "I spent $127,000 on ads to learn this. Here is what nobody tells you."
  Why it works: Investment = earned credibility + exclusivity of knowledge

CH-08: "The [number]-word [email/message/text] that [result]"
  Example: "The 9-word email that reactivated $23,400 in dead leads"
  Why it works: Extreme simplicity + extreme result = must know the 9 words

CH-09: "This [common thing] is secretly [unexpected characteristic]"
  Example: "This free PDF is secretly a $10,000 sales machine"
  Why it works: Reveals hidden value in familiar objects

CH-10: "Warning: Do NOT [common action] until you [read/watch/hear] this"
  Example: "Warning: Do NOT run Facebook ads until you read this"
  Why it works: Authority-based caution triggers loss aversion

CH-11: "The [topic] trick [demographic] discovered that [result]"
  Example: "The pricing trick dentists discovered that doubled their revenue"
  Why it works: Niche specificity + borrowed authority from unexpected source

CH-12: "I almost did not share this because [reason]"
  Example: "I almost did not share this because my competitors will hate me"
  Why it works: Forbidden / controversial = high perceived value

CH-13: "[Time period] ago, I discovered something about [topic] that changed everything"
  Example: "6 months ago, I discovered something about YouTube thumbnails that changed everything"
  Why it works: Personal discovery narrative + open loop

CH-14: "If you [condition], stop everything and [action]"
  Example: "If you have fewer than 1,000 email subscribers, stop everything and read this"
  Why it works: Conditional targeting + urgency + exclusivity

CH-15: "The [counterintuitive approach] to [desired result]"
  Example: "The lazy approach to making $10K/month with funnels"
  Why it works: Contradicts expected effort = curiosity about the mechanism
```

### 3.2 Contrarian Hooks

Contrarian hooks challenge existing beliefs. They create cognitive tension by telling the audience that what they KNOW to be true is actually wrong. This tension demands resolution.

**Core Mechanism:** Challenge a commonly held belief with a surprising counter-claim.

**Contrarian Hook Formulas (8 templates):**

```
CONTRARIAN HOOK TEMPLATES
==========================

CN-01: "Everything you know about [topic] is wrong"
  Example: "Everything you know about building a sales funnel is wrong"
  Why it works: Direct challenge to identity-held beliefs

CN-02: "[Common advice] is the worst advice in [industry]"
  Example: "'Post consistently' is the worst advice in social media marketing"
  Why it works: Attacks trusted advice = creates need to understand why

CN-03: "Stop [common practice]. It is killing your [metric]."
  Example: "Stop split-testing your headlines. It is killing your conversions."
  Why it works: Direct command + counter-intuitive = pattern interrupt

CN-04: "The [topic] myth that is costing you [specific loss]"
  Example: "The 'more traffic' myth that is costing you $3K/month"
  Why it works: Named myth + quantified cost = urgency to learn

CN-05: "Why [successful people] never [common practice]"
  Example: "Why 7-figure funnel builders never use long-form sales pages"
  Why it works: Authority-based contrarian + aspiration gap

CN-06: "[Common goal] is a trap. Here is what to do instead."
  Example: "Going viral is a trap. Here is what to do instead."
  Why it works: Reframes a positive as negative + offers alternative

CN-07: "I [stopped/quit/abandoned] [common practice] and [positive result]"
  Example: "I quit posting on Instagram and my revenue went up 40%"
  Why it works: Testimony against popular practice + measured result

CN-08: "The reason [common strategy] does not work anymore (and what replaced it)"
  Example: "The reason webinars do not work anymore (and what replaced them)"
  Why it works: Obsolescence fear + curiosity about replacement
```

```
FW-01: "Warning: [Common action] is [negative consequence] and you do not even know it"
  Example: "Warning: Your email sequence is killing your sales and you do not even know it"
  Why it works: Direct warning + invisible threat = urgent curiosity

FW-02: "Stop [common action] before it [catastrophic consequence]"
  Example: "Stop boosting posts before it bankrupts your ad account"
  Why it works: Urgent command + consequence escalation

FW-03: "The [topic] mistake that [negative outcome] (I made it too)"
  Example: "The funnel mistake that cost me $140,000 (I made it too)"
  Why it works: Quantified loss + shared vulnerability

FW-04: "If you [condition], you are [timeframe] away from [bad outcome]"
  Example: "If you do not have a follow-up funnel, you are 6 months away from running out of leads"
  Why it works: Conditional identification + time pressure

FW-05: "[Percentage] of [demographic] will [fail] this year because of [specific reason]"
  Example: "83% of online courses will fail this year because of one page in their funnel"
  Why it works: Statistical fear + specific actionable cause
```

### 3.9 Analogy Hooks

Analogy hooks make complex or unfamiliar concepts instantly understandable by connecting them to something the audience already knows. Brunson calls this the "Kinda Like Bridge."

**Core Mechanism:** Connect your concept to a familiar reference point.

**Analogy Hook Formulas (4 templates):**

```
ANALOGY HOOK TEMPLATES
========================

AH-01: "[Your thing] is like [familiar thing] but for [their world]"
  Example: "A funnel is like a vending machine but for your expertise"
  Why it works: Instant comprehension + novelty

AH-02: "Think of [topic] like [unexpected analogy]. [Insight]."
  Example: "Think of your email list like a garden. Most people are watering dead plants."
  Why it works: Familiar metaphor + provocative insight

AH-03: "[Person] is the [famous reference] of [niche]"
  Example: "She is the Gordon Ramsay of landing pages — brutal, but her funnels convert"
  Why it works: Instant personality transfer + entertainment value

AH-04: "If [familiar concept] then [your concept]"
  Example: "If Netflix can sell you a $20/month subscription you never cancel, why can not your membership site?"
  Why it works: Borrowed authority + aspiration + challenge
```

## SECTION 4: HEADLINE MASTERY

### 4.1 Headlines as Page Hooks

A headline IS a hook. It is the hook for your page. If the headline fails, the page fails. The headline's ONLY job is to get the visitor to read the next line.

**The Headline Hierarchy:**
```
HEADLINE HIERARCHY
===================

1. Main Headline (H1)
   Job: Stop the visitor and create curiosity
   Length: 5-15 words (shorter is better)
   Location: Above the fold, largest text on page

2. Sub-Headline
   Job: Expand the hook, add specificity
   Length: 10-25 words
   Location: Directly under main headline

3. Pre-Headline (optional)
   Job: Qualify the reader ("Attention: Course Creators...")
   Length: 3-8 words
   Location: Above main headline, smaller text

4. Supporting Statement
   Job: Resolve the curiosity gap enough to continue reading
   Length: 1-3 sentences
   Location: Below sub-headline
```

### 4.3 A/B Testing Methodology for Headlines

```
HEADLINE A/B TESTING PROTOCOL
===============================

STEP 1: Generate 10+ headline variations
  - Mix hook types (curiosity, contrarian, story, social proof)
  - Vary length (short punchy vs. specific detailed)
  - Test different emotional triggers

STEP 2: Score each headline internally (before testing)
  Hook Strength (1-10):    Does it stop the scroll?
  Curiosity Gap (1-10):    Does it demand resolution?
  Clarity (1-10):          Is the promise clear?
  Believability (1-10):    Does it seem credible?
  Relevance (1-10):        Does it speak to the avatar?
  TOTAL: /50

STEP 3: Select top 5 by score
  - Ensure variety of types
  - Include at least one "dark horse" (unexpected approach)

STEP 4: Test in this order
  - Test headlines in pairs
  - Run each pair for minimum 500 impressions or 48 hours
  - Winner advances to next round

STEP 5: Iterate
  - After finding the winner, create 5 variations of THAT headline
  - Test those variations
  - Repeat until diminishing returns

RULE: Never test more than ONE variable at a time.
      If you change the headline AND the image, you learn nothing.
```

### 4.4 Above-the-Fold Optimization

```
ABOVE-THE-FOLD CHECKLIST
==========================

[ ] Pre-headline qualifies the reader (if applicable)

[ ] CTA is visible without scrolling
[ ] No clutter — headline has visual dominance
[ ] Mobile version tested (70%+ traffic is mobile)
[ ] Load time under 3 seconds (slow pages kill hooks)
```

## SECTION 5: VIDEO HOOKS

### 5.1 First 3 Seconds Framework

In video, you have 3 seconds. Not 5. Not 10. Three. The brain decides whether to keep watching or scroll in 3 seconds. This is non-negotiable biology.

```
THE 3-SECOND VIDEO HOOK FRAMEWORK
====================================

SECOND 1: PATTERN INTERRUPT
  What the viewer sees/hears that breaks their autopilot
  - Unexpected visual (movement, color, face close-up)
  - Unexpected audio (silence, loud statement, unusual sound)
  - Text overlay with bold claim

SECOND 2: CURIOSITY IMPLANT
  The statement or visual that opens an information loop
  - "What I am about to show you..."
  - A result/transformation shown briefly
  - A question that demands an answer

SECOND 3: PROMISE / IDENTITY QUALIFIER
  The reason to keep watching specific to THEM
  - "If you are a [demographic]..."
  - "This changed everything for my [avatar]..."
  - The value proposition in 1 sentence
```

### 5.2 Pattern Interrupt Openings for Video

```
VIDEO PATTERN INTERRUPT LIBRARY
=================================

VISUAL INTERRUPTS:
  - Start mid-action (not from a static position)
  - Use unexpected camera angle (extreme close-up, overhead)
  - Start with B-roll then cut to face (not face first)
  - Hold up an object (physical prop)
  - Start with a screenshot or result
  - Fast motion → sudden stop
  - Start in an unusual location

AUDIO INTERRUPTS:
  - Start mid-sentence (as if conversation was already happening)
  - Lead with a bold/controversial statement
  - Start with a sound effect or music that stops
  - Whisper start (forces volume up = engagement)
  - "Wait wait wait — before you keep scrolling..."

TEXT OVERLAY INTERRUPTS:
  - Bold text with a number/statistic before you speak
  - "POV: You just discovered [thing]"
  - Captions that start before audio (creates anticipation)
  - Question text on screen, then verbal answer
```

### 5.4 Cold Open Techniques

A cold open starts the content immediately — no intro, no logo, no "hey guys welcome back." It drops the viewer into the middle of value.

```
COLD OPEN PROTOCOL
====================

DO:
  - Start with the most compelling moment of the video
  - Open with a result, then rewind ("Here is how I got here")
  - Begin with a provocative question
  - Lead with a bold claim you will prove
  - Use the "teaser" technique (show 5 seconds of the payoff, then start from beginning)

DO NOT:
  - "Hey everyone, welcome back to my channel"
  - "Before we get started, make sure to like and subscribe"
  - Logo animation intros (instant scroll)
  - Throat clearing, "um", "so", or "alright"
  - Recap of previous episodes (new viewers bounce)
  - "In today's video, we are going to..." (too slow, too predictable)

COLD OPEN TEMPLATE:
  [5-second hook/result/claim]
  "Let me show you exactly how." (bridge to content)
  [Content begins immediately]
  [Subscribe CTA at the END, not the beginning]
```

### 5.5 Thumbnail Hooks (Visual Attention Capture)

Thumbnails are the visual hook. On YouTube, the thumbnail determines whether anyone clicks. On social media, the first frame is the thumbnail.

```
THUMBNAIL HOOK CHECKLIST
==========================

VISUAL ELEMENTS:
  [ ] Face showing emotion (surprise, shock, excitement)
  [ ] High contrast colors (stands out in feed)
  [ ] Maximum 3-5 words of text
  [ ] Text is readable at mobile size (thumbnail preview is SMALL)
  [ ] Clear visual hierarchy (one focal point)
  [ ] Before/after split (if applicable)
  [ ] Arrow or circle pointing to key element (directs eye)

TEXT ELEMENTS:
  [ ] Text ADDS to the image (does not repeat the title)
  [ ] Short, punchy (2-4 words ideal)
  [ ] Creates curiosity gap with image
  [ ] Uses power words: SECRET, FREE, NEW, NOW, PROVEN, SHOCKING

WHAT TO AVOID:
  [ ] Cluttered design (too many elements)
  [ ] Small text (unreadable on mobile)
  [ ] Stock photos (generic, low CTR)
  [ ] Same visual template as every other video (pattern = ignored)
  [ ] Clickbait that does not match content (destroys retention)

THUMBNAIL A/B TESTING:
  - Test 3 thumbnail variants per video
  - Vary: face expression, text, color scheme
  - Check CTR after 48 hours of equal impressions
  - Winning thumbnail becomes the template for that content type
```

### 6.5 Multi-Hook Testing Strategy

```
THE 10-HOOK TESTING PROTOCOL
==============================

RULE: Test 10 hooks before you change anything else in the funnel.

STEP 1: Write 10 hooks for the same offer
  - 2 Curiosity hooks
  - 2 Contrarian hooks
  - 2 Story hooks
  - 2 Social Proof hooks
  - 1 Question hook
  - 1 Pattern Interrupt hook

STEP 2: Create ad sets for each hook
  - Same audience
  - Same image/video
  - Same offer/landing page
  - ONLY the hook text changes

STEP 3: Run all 10 simultaneously
  - Budget: $5-10/day per ad set (minimum)
  - Duration: 48-72 hours minimum
  - Metric: Click-through rate (CTR) as primary

STEP 4: Identify top 3 performers

STEP 5: Scale top 3 + create 5 variations of the #1 winner

STEP 6: Repeat until winner is clear

RULE: The hook is responsible for 80% of ad performance.
      If you have a great hook with a mediocre ad,
      it will outperform a mediocre hook with a great ad. Every time.
```

## SECTION 9: QA & ANTI-PATTERNS

### 9.1 Common Hook Mistakes

```
HOOK ANTI-PATTERN LIBRARY
============================

ANTI-PATTERN 01: THE CLEVER HOOK
  What it looks like: A pun, wordplay, or clever phrase that makes the writer smile
  Why it fails: Cleverness is not curiosity. If the reader says "ha" they do not say "I need to know more"
  Fix: Replace clever with curious. Curiosity > comedy.
  Example:
    BAD:  "Funnel vision: seeing clearly through your sales pipeline"
    GOOD: "The $3 change to my funnel that generated $47,000 last month"

ANTI-PATTERN 02: THE VAGUE HOOK
  What it looks like: Generic, could apply to anything, no specificity
  Why it fails: Vague hooks create no curiosity because there is no information gap
  Fix: Add specific numbers, names, timeframes, or details
  Example:
    BAD:  "How to improve your marketing results"
    GOOD: "How a yoga teacher added $23K/month with one change to her opt-in page"

ANTI-PATTERN 03: THE TOO-LONG HOOK
  What it looks like: A hook that takes more than 2 sentences or 15 seconds to deliver
  Why it fails: If it takes too long to hook, it is not a hook — it is an introduction
  Fix: Cut ruthlessly. The hook is 1-2 sentences max.
  Example:
    BAD:  "In today's competitive digital landscape, where marketers face increasing challenges
           with rising ad costs and declining organic reach, I want to share with you a powerful
           framework that has the potential to transform how you think about capturing attention..."
    GOOD: "Ad costs went up 300%. My conversions went up 400%. Here is why."

ANTI-PATTERN 04: CLICKBAIT WITHOUT PAYOFF
  What it looks like: Sensational hook that the content cannot deliver on
  Why it fails: Destroys trust, increases bounce rate, kills long-term audience
  Fix: Only hook what you can actually deliver. The hook is a PROMISE.
  Example:
    BAD:  "This ONE trick will make you a millionaire overnight"
    GOOD: "This pricing strategy added $14K/month to my coaching business in 60 days"

ANTI-PATTERN 05: THE ME-FIRST HOOK
  What it looks like: Hook focuses on the creator, not the audience
  Why it fails: The audience does not care about you (yet). They care about themselves.
  Fix: Make the hook about THEIR world, not yours
  Example:
    BAD:  "I am excited to announce my new course on funnel building!"
    GOOD: "You are about to get the exact funnel blueprint that generated $2.3M"

ANTI-PATTERN 06: THE CLICHE HOOK
  What it looks like: Uses overused phrases that the brain auto-filters
  Why it fails: Familiarity breeds invisibility. Cliches are invisible.
  Fix: Use fresh language, unexpected angles
  Example:
    BAD:  "Are you tired of struggling with your online business?"
    GOOD: "Your business does not have a traffic problem. It has a hook problem."

ANTI-PATTERN 07: THE BURIED HOOK
  What it looks like: The best hook is hidden in paragraph 3 of the content
  Why it fails: Nobody reads to paragraph 3 without a hook at paragraph 1
  Fix: Find the most compelling part of your content and move it to the first line
  Example:
    BAD:  "Today I want to talk about marketing. Marketing is important because...
           [3 paragraphs later] ...and that is how a 9-word email generated $23K"
    GOOD: "A 9-word email generated $23,400. Here is the email."

ANTI-PATTERN 08: THE FEATURE HOOK
  What it looks like: Leads with product features instead of outcomes
  Why it fails: Nobody cares about features. They care about what features DO for them.
  Fix: Translate features to outcomes. Sell the transformation, not the tool.
  Example:
    BAD:  "Our platform has AI-powered analytics, drag-and-drop editing, and 47 templates"
    GOOD: "Build a $10K/month funnel in 47 minutes — even if you have never written a line of code"
```

### 9.2 Hook Performance Metrics

```
HOOK PERFORMANCE SCORECARD
=============================

METRIC 1: SCROLL STOP RATE
  Definition: % of impressions that result in a pause (>1 second)
  Benchmark: 15-25% (good), 25%+ (excellent)
  Measured via: Platform analytics (video views / impressions, thumbstop rate)

METRIC 2: CLICK-THROUGH RATE (CTR)
  Definition: % of impressions that result in a click
  Benchmark: Varies by platform (see Section 8.2)
  Primary metric for: Ad hooks, email hooks, social hooks

METRIC 3: READ-MORE RATE
  Definition: % of viewers who click "See More" / "...more" to expand content
  Benchmark: 10-20% (good), 20%+ (excellent)
  Primary metric for: Social media post hooks

METRIC 4: WATCH TIME
  Definition: Average % of video watched
  Benchmark: 30-50% (good), 50%+ (excellent)
  Primary metric for: Video hooks (first 3 seconds determines the rest)

METRIC 5: HOOK-TO-CONVERSION CORRELATION
  Definition: Does the hook that gets most clicks also get most conversions?
  Warning: High CTR + Low conversion = hook-offer mismatch
  Action: If mismatch exists, align the hook promise with the offer delivery

METRIC 6: COST PER RESULT (by hook variant)
  Definition: Total spend / total conversions for each hook variant
  Purpose: Identifies which hooks attract BUYERS, not just clickers
  This is the ULTIMATE metric. CTR means nothing if the hook attracts the wrong people.
```

### 9.3 Quality Gates

Every hook set must pass these quality gates before delivery:

```
HOOK QUALITY GATE CHECKLIST
==============================

GATE 1: SCROLL STOP TEST
  [ ] Would YOU stop scrolling for this hook? (Honest answer)
  [ ] Read it out loud — does it have punch? Or does it fall flat?
  [ ] Show it to someone who does not know the context — do they want to know more?

GATE 2: SPECIFICITY TEST
  [ ] Does the hook contain at least one specific element (number, name, timeframe)?
  [ ] Could this hook ONLY apply to this offer/topic? Or could it be about anything?
  [ ] Is there a concrete image or concept the reader can visualize?

GATE 3: CURIOSITY TEST
  [ ] Does the hook open an information loop?
  [ ] Can the loop ONLY be closed by consuming the content?
  [ ] Would someone feel incomplete if they scrolled past?

GATE 4: ANTI-CLICKBAIT TEST
  [ ] Does the content actually deliver what the hook promises?
  [ ] Would the audience feel satisfied, not tricked, after consuming?
  [ ] Is the hook truthful and based on real results/data?

GATE 5: AVATAR TEST
  [ ] Is this hook relevant to the specific target avatar?
  [ ] Would the avatar's identity or situation be reflected in the hook?
  [ ] Would people OUTSIDE the target avatar still click? (If yes, hook is too broad)

GATE 6: PLATFORM TEST
  [ ] Is the hook optimized for the specific platform?
  [ ] Does it fit the platform's character/time constraints?
  [ ] Does it match the platform's content consumption pattern?

GATE 7: VARIETY TEST
  [ ] Does the hook set contain at least 3 different hook types?
  [ ] Are there both emotional and logical hooks?
  [ ] Is there at least one unexpected/dark horse option?

GATE 8: HSO ALIGNMENT TEST
  [ ] Does the hook naturally lead into a story?
  [ ] Does the story naturally lead to the offer?
  [ ] Is the HSO chain coherent (hook-promise = story-delivery = offer-action)?

SCORING:
  8/8 gates passed = APPROVED — deliver to user
  6-7/8 gates passed = CONDITIONAL — fix failed gates, then deliver
  <6/8 gates passed = REJECTED — rewrite hook set from scratch
```
