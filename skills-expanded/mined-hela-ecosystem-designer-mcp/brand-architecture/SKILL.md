# Brand Architecture

Turn a portfolio of brands and offers into an explicit system of relationships that customers, teams, and future acquisitions can understand.

## Core distinction

Brand architecture is the strategic structure of a portfolio: brand roles,
relationships, naming hierarchy, visible endorsement, equity transfer, and
governance. It is not merely a logo chart, a product taxonomy, a design system,
or a campaign calendar. Keep those layers separate and show where they connect.

## Workflow

1. **Inventory the portfolio.** List the corporate/master brand, ranges,
   sub-brands, products, services, variants, descriptors, campaigns, acquired
   brands, and internal names. Record audience, promise, market, lifecycle,
   current name, current visual relationship, owner, and evidence quality.
2. **Clarify roles and equity.** For every entity, state what it contributes,
   what it borrows from a parent, what must remain independent, and what would
   be damaged if the parent or sibling changed. Mark assumptions instead of
   inventing customer research.
3. **Choose a relationship model.** Compare branded house, sub-brand,
   endorsed brand, house of brands, and hybrid options against shared audience,
   parent credibility, differentiation, reputational coupling, investment,
   acquisition autonomy, and regulatory or geographic constraints. Use the
   decision matrix in `references/brand-architecture-framework.md`.
4. **Define the naming hierarchy.** Specify the grammar from master brand to
   offer family, product/service, and descriptor or variant. Define collision,
   abbreviation, localization, trademark, domain, and retirement rules. Flag
   legal or linguistic checks as work to be verified; never imply clearance.
5. **Define expression rules.** Describe the verbal endorsement, logo lockup,
   typography, color, imagery, navigation, metadata, and packaging relationship
   for each level. State the invariant parent cues and the permitted reasons for
   differentiation. Route actual token or mark production to Phenotype tools
   only after the strategic relationship is approved.
6. **Design governance.** Create an intake and approval path for new offers,
   acquisitions, partnerships, local exceptions, renames, and retirements.
   Assign decision rights, required evidence, review cadence, and a single
   source of truth for the portfolio map.
7. **Plan migration.** For an existing portfolio, sequence customer notice,
   naming changes, redirects, packaging, product UI, sales enablement, and
   measurement. Preserve legacy equity deliberately; do not recommend a clean
   rebrand merely because the diagram looks untidy.
8. **Stress-test the system.** Test a new product, acquisition, regional
   exception, co-brand, failed sibling, and retired offer. If the architecture
   cannot answer where each belongs, the rules are incomplete.

## Required deliverables

Produce only the artifacts the brief needs, normally:

- a portfolio inventory and relationship map;
- a model decision with rejected alternatives and trade-offs;
- naming hierarchy and naming grammar;
- verbal/visual relationship rules;
- governance and exception matrix;
- migration or rationalization roadmap; and
- a measurement plan with baselines, hypotheses, and owners.

## Quality gates

- Every entity has one declared role and one accountable owner.
- The chosen model follows business and audience evidence, not visual taste.
- Parent-brand visibility and reputational coupling are explicit.
- Naming rules produce valid examples and reject plausible collisions.
- Hybrid architecture has boundaries; it is not permission for arbitrary cases.
- Legacy equity, legal review, localization, and migration cost are visible.
- Claims about awareness, preference, or customer behavior are sourced or
  labeled as hypotheses.

## Phenotype integration

- Use `brand_fetch_design_md` for an existing brand's published design context.
- Use `brand_list` or `logo_search_library` for bounded comparative reference,
  never as proof of strategic fit.
- Use `generate_tokens`, `generate_rules`, or `logo_workflow` after the
  architecture and expression rules are approved.
- Store the final architecture decision and unresolved assumptions in Genome
  when the workflow includes project memory.

Read [the research-backed framework](references/brand-architecture-framework.md)
before choosing a model or writing a portfolio recommendation.
