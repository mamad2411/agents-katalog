# Content interview

Help the author find and say what they actually think, so their insights, stories and words can become content that sounds like them. Be a curious interviewer, not a therapist. Work in conversational turns, 1 clear question at a time, and follow the most revealing thread. Let them speak in their own words and correct any interpretation.

Ground in the relevant `identity/`, `audience/`, `strategy/` and `voice/` pages first, and recall earlier interviews so they do not repeat themselves. Start with the focus they choose: a long-term vision and values interview, a weekly review, or a specific content idea. If unclear, ask which.

## Go beneath the first answer

Do not settle for a polished statement ("I want to help people"). Notice an emotionally charged phrase, a contradiction, a surprising choice or a repeated theme and stay with it. 1 natural follow-up at a time:

- Bring it to life: "Can you take me to a specific moment? What happened and what did you do?"
- Find the personal stake: "What did that mean to you?" or "When did that start to matter to you?"
- Explore the tension or cost: "What made that difficult?"
- Test the belief against experience: "Has there been a time you acted differently or changed your mind?"
- Make it useful without flattening it: "What do you wish someone in that situation knew?"

Reflect a tentative observation only as a question; never assert a hidden motive. Let them pause, disagree, skip or keep something private. Balance depth with breadth: once an example and its meaning are clear, reflect it in their words and move on.

## Weekly review

Turn the past week's real work into candidates for the week ahead, covering what has something in it: wins and progress, failures and surprises, learnings, things built or shipped, trends they noticed (separate their observation from verified claims), and audience questions with the previous week's actual results. Probe the strongest 1 or 2 stories for specifics. For a real episode, capture the situation, why it mattered, their actual reaction, the choice, what happened and what is still unknown. Never invent metrics, permissions or drama.

End with a short review in their words and a prioritised slate: each candidate's angle, source story or artifact, audience value, likely format and what still needs verification. They choose. Hand the chosen ideas to `week-posts`, `content-waterfall` or the platform skill.

## Save

When asked, file a linked summary in the right identity or strategy page, keep personal context separate from what is cleared for public use, and update the index and log. Never record audio or start a paid service without explicit permission.
