# Write a LinkedIn post

Read `AGENTS.md`, `index.md`, relevant identity and audience pages, `identity/proof.md`, `voice/linkedin-voice.md`, `voice/formats.md` and the selected pillar/funnel context. Read the full source behind the central claim.

Drafts sound like a bot when the only voice input is a style guide. If the context holds the author's verbatim words (calls, DMs, voice notes, past posts), a story bank or a page on what performs on their account, read the relevant parts too. A story the author has not told in public needs their yes before it goes into copy.

## Find the post

Identify one useful point, its source, the intended reader and the post's job. Work from the supplied brief and existing context; ask for missing information only when it would change the substance. Do not invent a milestone, anecdote or customer quote to make an idea interesting.

Choose a structure that suits the material: a sequence for a process, a story for a real change, evidence for a claim, or a comparison for a decision. Do not force every post into one house format.

Write an opening that the body can substantiate. Prefer a specific observation over manufactured suspense. Use the author's actual rhythm and vocabulary. Do not add slang, self-deprecation, deliberate mistakes, dramatic reversals or numeric claims just to sound human.

A stub voice page does not block useful editing of a supplied draft. Preserve the user's wording and use plain language, while being clear that the voice has not yet been calibrated.

## Edit for substance

Check that each paragraph adds information. Cut generic praise, unsupported superlatives, repeated morals, empty transitions and slogan-like conclusions. Keep necessary explanations and qualifications. Read the opening, paragraph starts and ending together: the argument should remain understandable.

Check every factual claim, quote, date and number against its source and public-use status. Keep reported results, targets and illustrative examples distinct. Use a CTA only when it fits the chosen funnel job and a real available offer. Resource and video links go in the first comment and the post says so; a link to the author's own site carries UTM parameters (source `linkedin`, 1 campaign slug per piece or waterfall).

For repurposing, select one argument from the source rather than compressing the entire source into a post. For revisions, address the user's requested changes first and preserve their facts and meaningful phrasing.

## Deliver or save

Return one paste-ready draft, brief source notes and any material uncertainty. Offer alternate hooks only when useful or requested. Do not put process commentary inside the copy.

When saving to Notion is requested, follow `strategy/notion-schema.md`, fetch the configured destination and use `templates/notion-content.md`. Set Status to Creating, one Pillar and the selected Platform. Add a Publish date only if a date is agreed. Check for the existing card before creating another.

If Notion is unconfigured, deliver in chat and state it was not saved. Never silently use another board. Saving a draft does not schedule or publish it.

## Newest method (2026-11 refresh)

**Check the current stance first.** Before drafting, read the author's newest stances and their recent posts on the topic. Build on what they said or flag the change ("Yesterday I said I don't get the hype. Here is what changed."). A new hook never silently contradicts a public stance from days ago; tell the author when their brief does.

**Structure before sentences.** Pick 1 spine for the shape (descending, ascending, ranked, countdown or flat) and mix at most 2 devices; blending spines is the most common structural failure. Then run a skim test: line 1, the list heads and the last line alone must carry the argument. If they do not, re-pick the structure before editing sentences.

**The shape of posts that perform** (re-measure on the author's own account; these held for a founder account at 3x its median or more):

- Give the reader a system to copy: a framework, org chart, architecture, stack or list with a name and a number, shown in full on the visual.
- Line 1 is a big claim or result under about 55 characters; line 2 says what follows or proves it. Always 1 empty line between line 1 (the hook) and line 2 (the re-hook).
- Line breaks: in the intro and story, every sentence is its own paragraph. A section header (`1) The first hook`) gets an empty line after it. The `→` items under a header stay stacked. An explanation of 3 or 4 short lines may stack; its conclusion stands alone.
- 1 belief line for the why. Each step names the tool doing the work; generic steps flop.
- 1 honest line beside the claim and 1 contrast number when there is one.
- End by handing something over: the link in the comments, the graphic's instructions, a DM, or 1 real question.
- A lead-magnet post never writes "comment X for Y" in the text. The graphic carries the steps (Connect, Comment KEYWORD); the text only points to them.
- Keep the author's texture. An event, launch or milestone gets a copyable system inside it; on its own it tends to underperform.

**Firsthand stories.** When the material is a real decision or an unresolved situation, stay with it long enough for the reader to understand the stakes, what the author thought or felt if supplied, what they tried, what happened and what remains uncertain. Let the lesson emerge from that experience. Never invent dialogue, feelings or results.

**Tag who the post names.** A post about work with a customer or partner tags the author's company page, the other company's page and each named person at first mention; later mentions stay plain. Verify every handle with the publishing tool before queueing; guessing `firstname-lastname` often fails, so ask for the profile URL instead.

**One piece, one post.** When the same piece goes out on several platforms, schedule it as 1 post with 1 channel per platform (each with its own copy and media), not as separate posts. Every channel stays unapproved until the author approves it.

Run the whole draft through `voice/slop-filter.md` before showing it.
